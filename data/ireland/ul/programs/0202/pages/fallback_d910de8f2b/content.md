Equidae (commonly known as the horse family) is the [taxonomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxonomy_(biology)) [family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_(taxonomy)) of [horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_horse) and related animals, including [asses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asinus), [zebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zebra), and many extinct [species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species) known only from [fossils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil). The family evolved more than 50 million years ago, in the [Eocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eocene) epoch, from a small, multi-toed [ungulate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ungulate) into larger, single-toed animals. All [extant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extant_taxon) species are in the [genus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus) [Equus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equus_(genus)), which originated in North America. Equidae belongs to the order [Perissodactyla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perissodactyla), which includes the extant [tapirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapir) and [rhinoceros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinoceros), and several extinct families. It is more specifically grouped within the [superfamily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superfamily_(taxonomy)) [Equoidea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equoidea), the only other family being the extinct [Palaeotheriidae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaeotheriidae).

The term equid refers to any member of this family, including any [equine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equinae).

The oldest known [fossils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil) assigned to Equidae were found in North America, and date from the early [Eocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eocene) epoch, 54 million years ago. They were once assigned to the genus [Hyracotherium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyracotherium), but the [type species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_species) of that genus is now regarded as a [palaeothere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaeotheriidae). The other species have been split off into different genera. These early equids were fox-sized animals with three toes on the hind feet, and four on the front feet. They were herbivorous browsers on relatively soft plants, and already adapted for running. The complexity of their brains suggest that they already were alert and intelligent animals. [Later species reduced the number of toes, and developed teeth more suited for grinding up grasses and other tough plant food.](#cite_note-3)

The equids, like other perissodactyls, are [hindgut fermenters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindgut_fermentation). They have evolved specialized teeth that cut and shear tough plant matter to accommodate their fibrous diet. [Their seemingly inefficient digestion strategy is a result of their size at the time of its evolution,](#cite_note-4) [as they would have already had to be relatively large mammals to be supported on such a strategy.](#cite_note-5)

The family became relatively diverse during the [Miocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miocene) epoch, [with many new species appearing. By this time, equids were more truly horse-like, having developed the typical body shape of the modern animals.](#cite_note-6) [Many of these species bore the main weight of their bodies on their central third toe, with the others becoming reduced and barely touching the ground, if at all. The sole surviving genus, Equus, had evolved by the early](#cite_note-7) [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene) epoch, and spread rapidly through the world.
