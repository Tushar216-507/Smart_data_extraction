  • What is mathematics?

    Mathematics is the science of structure, order, and relation that evolved through counting, measuring, and describing shapes. It utilizes logical reasoning and quantitative calculation, with increasing idealization and abstraction of its subject matter. Since the 17th century, mathematics has been essential to the physical sciences and technology, and it has become increasingly important in the quantitative aspects of life sciences.

    All mathematical systems use combinations of axioms and theorems that can be logically deduced from them. The most basic notion in mathematics is the equation, which states that two mathematical expressions are equal.

  • What are addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division?

    Arithmetic involves fundamental operations on numbers.

    • Addition combines numbers to find their total. Synonyms include increase, gain, and rise.
    • Subtraction takes away an amount from a total. It is the inverse of addition.
    • Multiplication is adding a number to zero a number of times.
    • Division splits a number into equal parts. It is the inverse of multiplication.

  • What is geometry in mathematics?

    Geometry is a branch of mathematics that deals with the shape of objects, their spatial relationships, and the properties of the space around them. It is one of the oldest branches of mathematics, with its origins in practical problems such as surveying and construction. The word "geometry" comes from Greek words that mean "Earth measurement".

    Geometry is not limited to the study of flat surfaces (plane geometry) and rigid three-dimensional objects (solid geometry). Even abstract thoughts and images can be represented and developed in geometric terms. From its beginnings in ancient Egypt and Mesopotamia around 3100 BCE, geometry has evolved into a broad and abstract field that explores the nature of space and shape.

  • What is algebra and how are variables used in math?

    Algebra is a branch of mathematics that uses symbols to represent numerical values or quantities in equations. It generalizes arithmetic operations by applying them to these abstract symbols rather than specific numbers. These operations include addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division.

    A key characteristic of algebra is the use of variables, which are symbols, usually letters, that stand in for unknown numerical values. Commonly used variables are ( x ) and ( y ), and they can represent real-number unknowns, time (( t )), radius (( r )), or arc length (( s )). In the equation ( ax^2 + bx + c = 0 ), ( x ) is the variable, while ( a ), ( b ), and ( c ) are coefficients.

  • How is mathematics used to solve real-life problems?

    Mathematics offers tools for describing reality and analyzing problems in many fields. Algebra uses symbols to represent quantities and relationships, providing flexibility in problem-solving. Calculus helps to calculate rates of change and totals, with applications in physics, economics, and more. Statistics provides tools for understanding uncertainty and is applied across sciences, business, and policy. Geometry, which originally meant "Earth measurement," now has applications in astronomy, cartography, and other fields. Math is a tool in understanding phenomena. Mathematical models can analyze anything that can be expressed mathematically.

mathematics, the science of structure, order, and relation that has evolved from elemental practices of counting, measuring, and describing the shapes of objects. It deals with logical reasoning and quantitative calculation, and its development has involved an increasing degree of idealization and abstraction of its subject matter. Since the 17th century, mathematics has been an indispensable adjunct to the physical sciences and technology, and in more recent times it has assumed a similar role in the quantitative aspects of the life sciences.

In many cultures—under the stimulus of the needs of practical pursuits, such as commerce and agriculture—mathematics has developed far beyond basic counting. This growth has been greatest in societies complex enough to sustain these activities and to provide leisure for contemplation and the opportunity to build on the achievements of earlier mathematicians.

All mathematical systems (for example, Euclidean geometry) are combinations of sets of axioms and of theorems that can be logically deduced from the axioms. Inquiries into the logical and philosophical basis of mathematics reduce to questions of whether the axioms of a given system ensure its completeness and its consistency. For full treatment of this aspect, see mathematics, foundations of.

This article offers a history of mathematics from ancient times to the present. As a consequence of the exponential growth of science, most mathematics has developed since the 15th century ce, and it is a historical fact that, from the 15th century to the late 20th century, new developments in mathematics were largely concentrated in Europe and North America. For these reasons, the bulk of this article is devoted to European developments since 1500.

This does not mean, however, that developments elsewhere have been unimportant. Indeed, to understand the history of mathematics in Europe, it is necessary to know its history at least in ancient Mesopotamia and Egypt, in ancient Greece, and in Islamic civilization from the 9th to the 15th century. The way in which these civilizations influenced one another and the important direct contributions Greece and Islam made to later developments are discussed in the first parts of this article.
