The European Union (EU) is a [political](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_union) and [economic union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_union) of 27 [member states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_state_of_the_European_Union) that are [located primarily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_the_European_Union) in Europe. [A](#cite_note-15) [supranational union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supranational_union) with a total area of 4,233,255km2 (1,634,469sqmi) and an estimated population of just under 452million as of 2026,[its member states generated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_the_European_Union) a nominal [gross domestic product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_domestic_product) (GDP) of around €18.802 trillion in 2025, accounting for approximately one sixth of [global economic output](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_economic_output). [The EU is often described as a](#cite_note-16) [sui generis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_generis) political entity combining characteristics of both a [federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation) and a [confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation).

Its cornerstone, the [Customs Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Customs_Union), paved the way to establishing [an internal single market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Single_Market) based on standardised [legal framework and legislation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_law) that apply in all member states in those matters, and only those matters, where the states have agreed to act as one. EU policies aim to ensure the free movement of people, goods, services and capital within the internal market; [enact legislation in justice and home affairs; and maintain common policies on](#cite_note-Europa_Internal_Market-19) [trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Commercial_Policy_(EU)),[agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Agricultural_Policy),[fisheries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Fisheries_Policy), and [regional development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_policy_of_the_European_Union).

Passport controls have been abolished for travel within the [Schengen Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schengen_Area). [The](#cite_note-Internal_borders-23) [eurozone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurozone) is a group composed of the 21 EU member states that have fully implemented the [EU's economic and monetary union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_and_Monetary_Union_of_the_European_Union) and use the [euro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro) currency. Through the [Common Foreign and Security Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Foreign_and_Security_Policy), the union has developed [a role in external relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_the_European_Union) and [defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_the_European_Union). It maintains permanent [diplomatic missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomatic_missions_of_the_European_Union) throughout the world and represents itself at the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations), the [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization), the [G7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_and_the_G7) and the [G20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G20). In 2012, the EU [was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Nobel_Peace_Prize).

The EU was established, along with [its citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_citizenship), when the [Maastricht Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_Treaty) came into force in 1993. The Union later acquired a single, explicit international legal personality with the entry into force of the [Treaty of Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lisbon) in 2009. [Its beginnings can be traced to the](#cite_note-25) [Inner Six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_Six) states (Belgium, France, Italy, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, and [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany)) at the start of modern [European integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_integration) in 1948, and to the [Western Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Union_(alliance)), the [International Authority for the Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Authority_for_the_Ruhr), the [European Coal and Steel Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Coal_and_Steel_Community), the [European Economic Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Economic_Community) and the [European Atomic Energy Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Atomic_Energy_Community), which were established by treaties. These increasingly amalgamated bodies grew, with their legal successor, the EU, both in size through [the accessions of a further 22 states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlargement_of_the_European_Union) from 1973 to 2013, and in power through acquisitions of policy areas. In 2020, [the United Kingdom became](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brexit) the only member state to [leave the EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Withdrawal_from_the_European_Union); ten countries are [aspiring or negotiating to join it](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_enlargement_of_the_European_Union).

## History

### Background: World Wars and aftermath

[Internationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internationalism_(politics)) and [visions of European unity had existed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideas_of_European_unity_before_1948) since well before the 19th century, but gained particularly as a reaction to [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) and its aftermath. In this light the first advances for the idea of [European integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_integration) were made. In 1920 [John Maynard Keynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Maynard_Keynes) proposed a [European customs union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_customs_union) for the struggling post-war European economies, [and in 1923 the oldest organisation for European integration, the](#cite_note-Taussig_Keynes_1920_p._381-26) [Paneuropean Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paneuropean_Union) was founded, led by [Richard von Coudenhove-Kalergi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_von_Coudenhove-Kalergi), who would later found in June 1947 the [European Parliamentary Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Parliamentary_Union) (EPU). [Aristide Briand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristide_Briand)—who was [Prime Minister of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_France), a follower of the Paneuropean Union, and [Nobel Peace Prize laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_Peace_Prize_laureates) for the [Locarno Treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locarno_Treaties)—delivered a widely recognised speech at the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations) in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva) on 5 September 1929 for a [federal Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Europe) to secure Europe and settle the historic [Franco-German enmity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-German_enmity).

With large-scale war being waged in Europe once again in the 1930s and becoming [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), the question of what to fight against and what for had to be agreed on. A first agreement was the [Declaration of St James's Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_St_James's_Palace) of 1941, when [Europe's resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_resistance) gathered in London. This was expanded on by the 1941 [Atlantic Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Charter), establishing the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) and their common goals, inciting a new wave of global international institutions like the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations) ([founded 1945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Conference_on_International_Organization)) or the [Bretton Woods System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bretton_Woods_System) (1944).

In 1943 at the [Moscow Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Conference_(1943)) and [Tehran Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_Conference), plans to establish joint institutions for a post-war world and Europe increasingly became a part of the agenda. This led to a decision at the [Yalta Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yalta_Conference) in 1944 to form a [European Advisory Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Advisory_Commission), later replaced by the [Council of Foreign Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Foreign_Ministers) and the [Allied Control Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_Control_Council), following the German surrender and the [Potsdam Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam_Agreement) in 1945.

By the end of the war, European integration became seen as an antidote to the [extreme nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_nationalism) that had caused the war. [On 19 September 1946, in a much recognised speech,](#cite_note-30) [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill), speaking at the [University of Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zürich), reiterated his calls since 1930 for a "European Union" and "Council of Europe", coincidentally[while the](#cite_note-Union_of_European_Federalists_(UEF)-31) [Hertenstein Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertensteiner_Cross) of the [Union of European Federalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_European_Federalists), [one of the constituent members of the](#cite_note-32) [European Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Movement_International), was laying out its principles. One month later, the [French Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Union) was installed by the new [Fourth French Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_French_Republic) to direct the [decolonisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonization) of [its colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonies) so that they would become parts of a European community.

By 1947, a growing rift between the western Allied Powers and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) became evident as a result of the rigged [1947 Polish legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1947_Polish_legislative_election), which constituted an open breach of the [Yalta Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yalta_Agreement). March of that year saw two important developments. First was the signing of the [Treaty of Dunkirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Dunkirk) between [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France) and the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom). The treaty assured mutual assistance in the event of future military aggression against either nation. Though it officially named Germany as a threat, in reality the actual concern was for the Soviet Union. A few days later came the announcement of the [Truman Doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truman_Doctrine) which pledged American support for democracies to counter the Soviets.

### Initial years and the Paris Treaty (1948‍–‍1957)

Immediately following the [February 1948 coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Czechoslovak_coup_d'état) by the [Communist Party of Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Czechoslovakia), the [London Six-Power Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Six-Power_Conference) was held, resulting in the [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) boycott of the Allied Control Council and its incapacitation, an event marking the beginning of the [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War). The year 1948 marked the beginning of the institutionalised modern [European integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_integration). In March 1948 the [Treaty of Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Brussels) was signed, establishing the [Western Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Union_(alliance)) (WU), followed by the [International Authority for the Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Authority_for_the_Ruhr). Furthermore, the [Organisation for European Economic Co-operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_for_European_Economic_Co-operation) (OEEC), the predecessor of the OECD, was also founded in 1948 to manage the [Marshall Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Plan), which led to the Soviets creating [Comecon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comecon) in response. The ensuing [Hague Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hague_Congress_(1948)) of May 1948 was a pivotal moment in European integration, as it led to the creation of the [European Movement International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Movement_International), the [College of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Europe), [and most importantly to the foundation of the](#cite_note-34) [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe) on 5 May 1949 (which is now [Europe Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe_Day)). The Council of Europe has since been a broad forum to further cooperation and shared issues, achieving, for example, the [European Convention on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Convention_on_Human_Rights) in 1950.

Essential for the actual birth of the institutions of the EU was the [Schuman Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schuman_Declaration) on 9 May 1950 (the day after the fifth [Victory in Europe Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_in_Europe_Day)) and the decision by six nations (France, Belgium, Netherlands, Luxembourg, West Germany, and Italy) to follow Schuman and draft the [Treaty of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_(1951)). This treaty was created in 1952 the [European Coal and Steel Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Coal_and_Steel_Community) (ECSC), which was built on the [International Authority for the Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Authority_for_the_Ruhr), installed by the Western Allies in 1949 to regulate the coal and steel industries of the Ruhr area in West Germany. [Backed by the](#cite_note-36) [Marshall Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Plan) with large funds coming from the United States since 1948, the ECSC became a milestone organisation, enabling European economic development and integration and being the origin of the main institutions of the EU such as the [European Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commission) and [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Parliament).

The [founding fathers of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founding_fathers_of_the_European_Union) understood that coal and steel were the two industries essential for waging war, and believed that by tying their national industries together, a future war between their nations became much less likely. [In parallel with Schuman, the](#cite_note-Europa_History_45-59-38) [Pleven Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleven_Plan) of 1951 tried but failed to tie the institutions of the developing European community under the [European Political Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Political_Community_(1952)), which was to include the also proposed [European Defence Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_establishing_the_European_Defence_Community), an alternative to [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany) joining [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO) which was established in 1949 under the [Truman Doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truman_Doctrine). In 1954 the [Modified Brussels Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modified_Brussels_Treaty) transformed the Western Union into the [Western European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_European_Union) (WEU). West Germany eventually joined both the WEU and NATO in 1955, prompting the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) to form the [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact) in 1955 as an institutional framework for its military domination in the countries of [Central and Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_and_Eastern_Europe). Assessing the progress of European integration the [Messina Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messina_Conference) was held in 1955, ordering the [Spaak Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaak_Report), which in 1956 recommended the next significant steps of European integration.

### Treaty of Rome (1958‍–‍1972)

In 1957, [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy), [Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxembourg), [the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands), and [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany) signed the [Treaty of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Rome), which created the [European Economic Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Economic_Community) (EEC) and established a [customs union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Customs_Union). [They also signed another pact creating the](#cite_note-39) [European Atomic Energy Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Atomic_Energy_Community) (Euratom) for cooperation in developing nuclear power. Both treaties came into force in 1958. [Although the EEC and Euratom were created separately from the ECSC, they shared the same courts and the Common Assembly. The EEC was headed by](#cite_note-Europa_History_45-59-38) [Walter Hallstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Hallstein) ([Hallstein Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallstein_Commission)) and Euratom was headed by [Louis Armand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Armand) ([Armand Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armand_Commission)) and then [Étienne Hirsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Étienne_Hirsch) ([Hirsch Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirsch_Commission)).

The OEEC was in turn reformed in 1961 into the [Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_for_Economic_Co-operation_and_Development) (OECD) and its membership was extended to states outside of Europe, the United States and Canada. During the 1960s, tensions began to show, with France seeking to limit supranational power. Nevertheless, in 1965 an agreement was reached, and on 1 July 1967 the [Merger Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merger_Treaty) created a single set of institutions for the three communities, which were collectively referred to as the [European Communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Communities).[Jean Rey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Rey_(politician)) [presided over](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_European_Commission) the first merged commission ([Rey Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rey_Commission)).

### First enlargement and European co-operation (1973‍–‍1993)

In 1973, the communities were enlarged to include [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark) (including Greenland), Ireland, and the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_the_United_Kingdom_to_the_European_Communities).[Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway) had negotiated to join at the same time, but Norwegian voters rejected membership in a [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Norwegian_European_Communities_membership_referendum). The [Ostpolitik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostpolitik) and the ensuing détente led to establishment of a first truly pan-European body, the [Conference on Security and Co-operation in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conference_on_Security_and_Co-operation_in_Europe) (CSCE), predecessor of the modern [Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_for_Security_and_Co-operation_in_Europe) (OSCE). In 1979, the [first direct elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_European_Parliament_election) to the European Parliament were held.[Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece) joined in 1981. In 1985, Greenland [left the Communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Greenlandic_European_Communities_membership_referendum), following a dispute over fishing rights. During the same year, the [Schengen Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schengen_Agreement) paved the way for the creation of open borders without passport controls between most member states and some non-member states. [In 1986, the](#cite_note-46) [Single European Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_European_Act) was signed. Portugal and [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_Treaty_of_Spain_to_the_European_Economic_Community) joined in 1986. [In 1990, after](#cite_note-ENA_Enlargement_negotiations-47) [the fall of the Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1989), the former [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany) became part of the communities as part of a [reunified Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification).

### Treaties of Maastricht, Amsterdam and Nice (1993‍–‍2004)

The European Union was formally established when the [Maastricht Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_Treaty)—whose main architects were [Horst Köhler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horst_Köhler),[Helmut Kohl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmut_Kohl) and [François Mitterrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/François_Mitterrand)—came into force on 1 November 1993. [The treaty also gave the name](#cite_note-50) [European Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Community) to the EEC, even if it was referred to as such before the treaty. With further enlargement planned to include the former [communist states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_state) of Central and Eastern Europe, as well as [Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus) and [Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta), the [Copenhagen criteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_criteria) for candidate members to join the EU were agreed upon in June 1993. The expansion of the EU introduced a new level of complexity and discord.

In 1995, Austria, Finland, and Sweden [joined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_enlargement_of_the_European_Union) the EU. In 2002, euro banknotes and coins replaced national currencies in 12 of the member states. Since then, the [eurozone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurozone) has increased to encompass 21 countries. The euro currency became the second-largest reserve currency in the world. In 2004, the EU saw [its biggest enlargement to date](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_enlargement_of_the_European_Union) when Cyprus, the Czech Republic, [Estonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonia), [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary), [Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvia), [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuania), [Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta), [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland), [Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovakia), and [Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovenia) joined the union.

### Treaty of Lisbon and Brexit (2004‍–‍present)

In 2007, Bulgaria and Romania became EU members. Later that year, Slovenia adopted the euro, [followed by Cyprus and Malta in 2008, Slovakia in 2009, Estonia in 2011, Latvia in 2014, Lithuania in 2015, and Croatia in 2023. On 1 December 2009, the](#cite_note-Europa_History_00-52) [Lisbon Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lisbon) entered into force and reformed many aspects of the EU. In particular, it changed the legal structure of the EU, merging the [EU three pillars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_pillars_of_the_European_Union) system into a single legal entity provisioned with a [legal personality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_personality), created a permanent [president of the European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_European_Council), the first of which was [Herman Van Rompuy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herman_Van_Rompuy), and strengthened the position of the [high representative of the union for foreign affairs and security policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Representative_of_the_Union_for_Foreign_Affairs_and_Security_Policy).

In 2012, the EU [received the Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Nobel_Peace_Prize) for having "contributed to the advancement of peace and reconciliation, democracy, and human rights in Europe". [In 2013,](#cite_note-56) [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia) became the 28th EU member. [From the beginning of the 2010s, the cohesion of the European Union has been tested by several issues, including the](#cite_note-57) [euro area crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_area_crisis), [2015 European migrant crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_European_migrant_crisis), and [Brexit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brexit). [A](#cite_note-BBCUKREFRESLT-58) [referendum in the UK on its membership of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_Kingdom_European_Union_membership_referendum) was held in 2016, with 51.9 per cent of participants voting to leave. [The UK formally notified the European Council of its decision to leave on 29 March 2017, initiating the](#cite_note-NYTEUBrexit-59) [formal withdrawal procedure for leaving the EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_50_of_the_Treaty_on_European_Union); following extensions to the process, the UK left the European Union on 31 January 2020, although most areas of EU law continued to apply to the UK for a transition period that lasted until 31 December 2020.

The early 2020s saw Denmark [abolishing one of its three opt-outs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Danish_European_Union_opt-out_referendum) and Croatia [adopting the euro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_and_the_euro). After [the economic crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_recession) caused by the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic), the EU leaders agreed for the first time to create common debt to finance the European Recovery Program called [Next Generation EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Generation_EU) (NGEU). [On 24 February 2022, after massing on the borders of Ukraine, the](#cite_note-61) [Russian Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Armed_Forces) undertook an attempt for a full-scale invasion of Ukraine. [The European Union imposed](#cite_note-63) [heavy sanctions on Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_sanctions_during_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War#EU_sanctions) and agreed on a pooled military aid package to Ukraine for lethal weapons funded via the [European Peace Facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Peace_Facility) off-budget instrument.

NGEU is a European Commission [economic recovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_recovery) package to support the EU member states to recover from the COVID-19 pandemic, in particular those that have been particularly hard hit. It is sometimes styled NextGenerationEU and Next Gen EU, and also called the European Union Recovery Instrument. [Agreed in principle by the](#cite_note-65) [European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Council) on 21 July 2020 and adopted on 14 December 2020, the instrument is worth [€](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro)750billion. NGEU will operate from 2021 to 2026, [and will be tied to the regular](#cite_note-66) [2021–2027 budget of the EU's Multiannual Financial Framework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiannual_Financial_Framework#2021–2027_Financial_Framework_and_recovery_package) (MFF). The comprehensive NGEU and MFF packages are projected to reach €1824.3billion.

Preparing the EU for a new great enlargement is one of its political priorities, with the goal of achieving 35 member states by 2030. Institutional and budgetary reforms are being discussed in order for the EU to be ready for the new members.

In December 2025, it was leaked that the [United States government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_government) would attempt to persuade "Austria, Hungary, Italy, and Poland" to depart the EU in a Brexit-type manner.

## Politics

The European Union operates through a hybrid system of [supranational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supranational_union) and [intergovernmental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intergovernmentalism) decision-making, [and according to the](#cite_note-CIA-80) [principle of conferral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principle_of_conferral) (which says that it should act only within the limits of the competences conferred on it by the [treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaties_of_the_European_Union)) and of [subsidiarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsidiarity_(European_Union)) (which says that it should act only where an objective cannot be sufficiently achieved by the member states acting alone). [Laws made by the EU institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_law) are passed in a variety of forms. [Generally speaking, they can be classified into two groups: those which come into force without the necessity for national implementation measures (regulations) and those which specifically require national implementation measures (directives).](#cite_note-81) [EU policy is in general promulgated by](#cite_note-82) [EU directives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directive_(European_Union)), which are then implemented in the [domestic legislation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereignty) of its [member states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_state_of_the_European_Union), and [EU regulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulation_(European_Union)), which are immediately enforceable in all member states. [Lobbying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobbying#European_Union) at the EU level by special interest groups is regulated to try to balance the aspirations of private initiatives with public interest decision-making process.

### Member states

Through successive [enlargements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlargement_of_the_European_Union), the EU and its predecessors have grown from the [six founding states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_Six) of the EEC to 27 members. Countries accede to the union by becoming a party to the founding [treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaties_of_the_European_Union), thereby subjecting themselves to the privileges and obligations of EU membership. This entails a partial delegation of sovereignty to the institutions in return for representation within those institutions, a practice often referred to as "pooling of sovereignty". [In some policies, there are several member states that ally with strategic partners within the union. Examples of such alliances include the](#cite_note-85) [Baltic Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Assembly), the [Benelux Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benelux_Union), the [Bucharest Nine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucharest_Nine), the [Craiova Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craiova_Group), the [EU Med Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU_Med_Group), the [Lublin Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lublin_Triangle), the [New Hanseatic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hanseatic_League), the [Three Seas Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Seas_Initiative), the [Visegrád Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visegrád_Group), and the [Weimar Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Triangle).

To become a member, a country must meet the [Copenhagen criteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_criteria), defined at the 1993 meeting of the European Council in Copenhagen. These require a stable democracy that respects human rights and the [rule of law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_law); a functioning [market economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_economy); and the acceptance of the obligations of membership, including EU law. Evaluation of a country's fulfilment of the criteria is the responsibility of the [European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Council).

The four countries forming the [European Free Trade Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Free_Trade_Association) (EFTA) are not EU members, but have partly committed to the EU's economy and regulations: Iceland, [Liechtenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liechtenstein) and Norway, which are a part of the [single market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_market) through the [European Economic Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Economic_Area), and [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland), which has similar ties through [bilateral treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland–European_Union_relations). [The relationships of the](#cite_note-CH-88) [European microstates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microstates_and_the_European_Union) [Andorra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andorra), [Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monaco), [San Marino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Marino), and [Vatican City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatican_City) include the use of the euro and other areas of co-operation.

#### Former members

[Article 50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_50_of_the_Treaty_on_European_Union) of the [Lisbon Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lisbon) provides the basis for a member to [leave the EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Withdrawal_from_the_European_Union). ### Governance

Member states retain in principle all powers except those that they have agreed collectively to delegate to the union as a whole, though the exact delimitation has on occasions become a subject of scholarly or legal disputes. [In certain fields, members have awarded](#cite_note-102) [exclusive competence and exclusive mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsidiarity_(European_Union)#EU_competences) to the union. These are areas in which member states have entirely renounced their own capacity to enact legislation. In other areas, the EU and its member states share the competence to legislate. While both can legislate, the member states can only legislate to the extent to which the EU has not. In other policy areas, the EU can only co-ordinate, support and supplement member state action but cannot enact legislation with the aim of harmonising national laws. [That a particular policy area falls into a certain category of competence is not necessarily indicative of what](#cite_note-103) [legislative procedure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature_of_the_European_Union) is used for enacting legislation within that policy area. Different legislative procedures are used within the same category of competence, and even with the same policy area. The European Union has seven principal decision-making bodies, its [institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institutions_of_the_European_Union): the [European Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Parliament), the [European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Council), the [Council of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_the_European_Union), the [European Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commission), the [Court of Justice of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Justice_of_the_European_Union), the [European Central Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Central_Bank) and the [European Court of Auditors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Court_of_Auditors). Competence in scrutinising and amending legislation is shared between the Council of the European Union and the European Parliament, while executive tasks are performed by the European Commission and in a limited capacity by the European Council (not to be confused with the aforementioned Council of the European Union). The [monetary policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monetary_policy) of the eurozone is determined by the European Central Bank. The interpretation and the application of EU law and the treaties are ensured by the Court of Justice of the European Union. The EU budget is scrutinised by the European Court of Auditors. There are also a number of ancillary bodies which advise the EU or operate in a specific area.

### Branches of power

#### Executive branch

The union's executive branch is organised as a [directorial system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directorial_system), where the executive power is jointly exercised by several people. The executive branch consists of the European Council and European Commission.

The European Council sets the broad political direction of the union. It convenes at least four times a year and comprises the [president of the European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_European_Council) (presently [António Costa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/António_Costa)), the [president of the European Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_European_Commission) and one representative per [member state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_state_of_the_European_Union) (either its [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) or [head of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_government)). The [high representative of the union for foreign affairs and security policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Representative_of_the_Union_for_Foreign_Affairs_and_Security_Policy) (presently [Kaja Kallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaja_Kallas)) also takes part in its meetings. Described by some as the union's "supreme political leadership", [it is actively involved in the negotiation of](#cite_note-How_work-105) [treaty changes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaties_of_the_European_Union) and defines the EU's policy agenda and strategies. Its leadership role involves solving disputes between member states and the institutions, and to resolving any political crises or disagreements over controversial issues and policies. It acts as a "collective head of state" and [ratifies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratification) important documents (for example, international agreements and treaties). [Tasks for the president of the European Council are ensuring the external representation of the EU,](#cite_note-Oxford_University_Press-106) [driving consensus and resolving divergences among member states, both during meetings of the European Council and over the periods between them. The European Council should not be mistaken for the](#cite_note-Council_Press_Release-107) [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe), an international organisation independent of the EU and based in Strasbourg.

The [European Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commission) acts both as the EU's [executive arm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_(government)), responsible for the day-to-day running of the EU, and also the [legislative initiator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_of_initiative_(legislative)), with the sole power to propose laws for debate. [The commission is 'guardian of the Treaties' and is responsible for their efficient operation and policing.](#cite_note-110) [It has 27](#cite_note-111) [European commissioners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commissioner) for different areas of policy, one from each member state, though commissioners are bound to represent the interests of the EU as a whole rather than their home state. The leader of the 27 is the [president of the European Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_European_Commission) (presently [Ursula von der Leyen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ursula_von_der_Leyen) for 2019–2024, reelected for the 2024–2029 term), [proposed by the European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_European_Commission#Appointment), following and taking into account the result of the European elections, and is then elected by the European Parliament. [The President retains, as the leader responsible for the entire cabinet, the final say in accepting or rejecting a candidate submitted for a given portfolio by a member state, and oversees the commission's permanent civil service. After the President, the most prominent commissioner is the high representative of the union for foreign affairs and security policy, who is ex-officio a](#cite_note-112) [vice-president of the European Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-President_of_the_European_Commission) and is also chosen by the European Council. [The other 25 commissioners are subsequently appointed by the](#cite_note-113) [Council of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_the_European_Union) in agreement with the nominated president. The 27 commissioners as a single body are subject to approval (or otherwise) by a vote of the [European Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Parliament). All commissioners are first nominated by the government of the respective member state.

#### Legislative branch

The council, as it is now simply called [(also called the Council of the European Union](#cite_note-115) [and the "Council of Ministers", its former title),](#cite_note-116) [forms one half of the EU's legislature. It consists of a representative from each member state's government and meets in](#cite_note-117) [different compositions depending on the policy area being addressed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_the_European_Union#Configurations). Notwithstanding its different configurations, it is considered to be one single body. In addition to the legislative functions, members of the council also have [executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_(government)) responsibilities, such as the development of a [Common Foreign and Security Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Foreign_and_Security_Policy) and the coordination of broad economic policies within the union. [The](#cite_note-Europa_Institutions_Council-118) [Presidency of the council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_the_Council_of_the_European_Union) rotates between member states, with each holding it for six months. Beginning on 1 July 2025, the position is held by Denmark.

The European Parliament is one of three [legislative institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature_of_the_European_Union) of the EU, which together with the [Council of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_the_European_Union) is tasked with amending and approving the European Commission's proposals. 705 [members of the European Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_European_Parliament) (MEPs) are directly [elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_the_European_Union) by [EU citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship_of_the_European_Union) every five years on the basis of [proportional representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proportional_representation). MEPs are elected on a national basis and they sit according to [political groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_groups_of_the_European_Parliament) rather than their nationality. Each country has a set number of seats and is divided into [sub-national constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Parliament_constituency) where this does not affect the proportional nature of the voting system. [In the](#cite_note-119) [ordinary legislative procedure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_legislative_procedure), the European Commission proposes legislation, which requires the joint approval of the European Parliament and the Council of the European Union to pass. This process applies to nearly all areas, including the [EU budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budget_of_the_European_Union). The parliament is the final body to approve or reject the proposed membership of the commission, and can attempt motions of censure on the commission by appeal to the [Court of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Court_of_Justice). The [president of the European Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_European_Parliament) carries out the role of speaker in Parliament and represents it externally. The president and [vice-presidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_European_Parliament) are elected by MEPs every two and a half years.

#### Judicial branch

The [judicial branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_branch) of the European Union is formally called the [Court of Justice of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Justice_of_the_European_Union) (CJEU) and consists of two courts: the [Court of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Court_of_Justice) and the [General Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Court_(European_Union)). [The Court of Justice is the](#cite_note-121) [supreme court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_court) of the European Union in matters of [European Union law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_law). As a part of the CJEU, it is tasked with interpreting EU law and ensuring its uniform application across all [EU member states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_state_of_the_European_Union) under Article 263 of the Treaty of the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). The Court was established in 1952, and is based in [Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxembourg_City). It is composed of one judge per member state – currently 27 – although it normally hears cases in panels of three, five or fifteen judges. The Court has been led by president [Koen Lenaerts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koen_Lenaerts) since 2015. The CJEU is the highest court of the European Union in matters of Union law. Its case-law provides that EU law has supremacy over any national law that is inconsistent with EU law. [It is not possible to appeal against the decisions of national courts in the CJEU, but rather national courts refer questions of EU law to the CJEU (a preliminary reference). However, it is ultimately for the national court to apply the resulting interpretation (the preliminary ruling) to the facts of any given case. Although, only courts of final appeal are bound to refer a question of EU law when one is addressed. The treaties give the CJEU the power for consistent application of EU law across the EU as a whole. The court also acts as an administrative and constitutional court between the other EU institutions and the Member States and can annul or invalidate unlawful acts of EU institutions, bodies, offices and agencies.](#cite_note-122)

The [General Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Court_(European_Union)) is a constituent court of the European Union. It hears actions taken against the [institutions of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institutions_of_the_European_Union) by individuals and member states, although certain matters are reserved for the Court of Justice. Decisions of the General Court can be appealed to the Court of Justice, but only on a point of law. Prior to the coming into force of the [Lisbon Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon_Treaty) on 1 December 2009, it was known as the Court of First Instance. Since the amendments to the [Statute of the CJEU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_the_Court_of_Justice_of_the_European_Union) in October 2024, the Court of Justice shares its jurisdiction over references for a preliminary ruling with the General Court. [The General Court is since then competent to handle preliminary references falling under one of the six following areas: the common system of](#cite_note-123) [value added tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_value_added_tax); excise duties; the Customs Code; the tariff classification of goods under the [Combined Nomenclature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_Nomenclature); compensation and assistance to passengers in the event of denied boarding or delay or cancellation of transport services; the scheme for greenhouse gas emission allowance trading.

#### Additional branches

The [European Central Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Central_Bank) (ECB) is one of the institutions of the [monetary branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_bank) of the European Union, the prime component of the [Eurosystem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurosystem) and the European System of Central Banks. It is one of the world's [most important central banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Four_(banking)#International_use), heading a system with a combined balance sheet of close to €7 trillion. [The](#cite_note-125) [ECB Governing Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governing_Council_of_the_European_Central_Bank) makes [monetary policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monetary_policy) for the [eurozone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurozone) and the European Union, administers the [foreign exchange reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_exchange_reserves) of EU member states, engages in foreign exchange operations, and defines the intermediate monetary objectives and key interest rate of the EU. [The](#cite_note-126) [ECB Executive Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Board_of_the_European_Central_Bank) enforces the policies and decisions of the Governing Council, and may direct the national central banks when doing so. [The ECB has the exclusive right to authorise the issuance of](#cite_note-127) [euro banknotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_banknotes). Member states can issue [euro coins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_coins), but the volume must be approved by the ECB beforehand. The bank also operates the [T2 (RTGS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T2_(RTGS)) payments system. The [European System of Central Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_System_of_Central_Banks) (ESCB) consists of the ECB and the national central banks (NCBs) of all 27 member states of the European Union. The ESCB is not the monetary authority of the eurozone, because not all EU member states have joined the euro. The ESCB's objective is price stability throughout the European Union. Secondarily, the ESCB's goal is to improve monetary and financial cooperation between the Eurosystem and member states outside the eurozone.

The [European Court of Auditors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Court_of_Auditors) (ECA) is the [auditory branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_audit_institution) of the European Union. It was established in 1975 in [Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxembourg_City) in order to improve EU financial management. [It has 27 members (1 from each EU member-state) supported by approximately 800 civil servants. The](#cite_note-ENA_ECA-129) [European Personnel Selection Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Personnel_Selection_Office) (EPSO) is the EU's [civil service recruitment body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_service_commission) and operates its selection of candidates via generalist and specialist competitions. Each institution is then able to recruit staff from among the pool of candidates selected by EPSO. Every year, the EU institutions recruit around 1,000 new permanent staff members for long-term careers from more than 50,000 applicants to EPSO's open competitions. [The](#cite_note-130) [European Ombudsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Ombudsman) is the [ombudsman branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ombudsman) of the European Union that holds the institutions, bodies and agencies of the EU to account, and promotes good administration. The Ombudsman helps people, businesses and organisations facing problems with the EU administration by investigating complaints, as well as by proactively looking into broader systemic issues. The current Ombudsman is [Teresa Anjinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teresa_Anjinho). [The](#cite_note-131) [European Public Prosecutor's Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Public_Prosecutor's_Office) (EPPO) is the [prosecutory branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney_general) of the union, established under the Treaty of Lisbon. It is based in [Kirchberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirchberg,_Luxembourg), Luxembourg City, alongside the Court of Justice of the European Union and the European Court of Auditors. Following Hungary's accession in 2026, Denmark and Ireland were the only member states that were not participating.

### EU ethical governance and Ethics Body

The European Union’s public ethics system is highly fragmented, with various bespoke public integrity regimes coexisting under EU law, each possessing its own ethics principles and obligations. [EU ethics standards are scattered across multiple legal sources, ranging from the EU Treaties and institutional Rules of Procedure to Codes of Conduct and the EU Staff Regulations.](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlemanno202515-133) [While public ethics instruments exist in almost all EU institutions, there is less evidence that ethics constitutes an integrated feature of day-to-day EU policymaking, a concept defined by scholars as "ethical governance".](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlemanno202516-134)

Historically, the system's enforcement mechanisms have faced severe structural limitations, notably high fragmentation, limited independence, and weak sanctioning powers. [Furthermore, virtually all EU institutions have traditionally entrusted the respect of these rules to themselves through internal self-assessment mechanisms rather than independent external oversight.](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlemanno202526-136) [The 2022](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlemanno2025278-137) [Qatargate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_corruption_scandal_at_the_European_Parliament) influence-buying scandal served as a critical juncture that accelerated the push for systemic reform across the institutions, [as public perception of the transparency and independence of EU institutions vis-à-vis private interests remained poor.](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlemanno2025186-138) [In 2021, the European Parliament had initially proposed the creation of a strong, independent ethics body equipped with its own investigative and enforcement powers.](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKotanidisFaucheux20242-139)

Instead, the main EU institutions concluded an agreement, formally signed in May 2024, to establish a comparatively constrained Interinstitutional Body for Ethical Standards (IBES). [The core task of this new entity is the development of "common minimum standards" for the conduct of the political members of the participating institutions, strictly excluding the](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKotanidisFaucheux20248-142) [EU civil service staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Civil_Service). [The new body adopts these minimum standards by consensus and is assisted by five independent experts.](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlemanno2025283,_272-143)[The Court of Justice of the EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Justice_of_the_European_Union) (ECJ) participates only as an observer to preserve its judicial impartiality.

The [Interinstitutional EU Ethics Body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Ethics_Body) (or Interinstitutional Body for Ethical Standards; IBES[) faces specific legal and constitutional constraints. Functioning through the "contractual" conjunction of each participating institution, the body operates primarily as a formal mechanism for coordination rather than a centralized enforcement agency.](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKotanidisFaucheux2024-145) [Curiously the body is not based on the Treaty legal basis for IIAs (](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlemanno202588,_94–95-146)[Inter Institutional Agreements](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/EN/legal-content/summary/interinstitutional-agreements.html)) (Article 295 TFEU[), as this article only refers to the three main EU institutions. It cannot take over decision-making powers for individual disciplinary cases.](#cite_note-147)[. Consequently, it does not replace existing ethical authorities but merely aims at complementing them, and its creation explicitly does not encroach upon the investigative competences of the](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKotanidisFaucheux20246-149) [European Anti-Fraud Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Anti-Fraud_Office) (OLAF) or the [European Ombudsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Ombudsman). [The](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartinez_Navarro2025239-151) [European Ombudsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Ombudsman) continues to play a parallel role in assessing ethical breaches and conflicts of interests under the broader concept of "maladministration", which goes beyond strict illegality.

Academic evaluations and civil society groups generally characterize the creation of the IBES as an example of "incremental change" rather than comprehensive reform. [Civil society organizations, such as](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlemanno2025192–193-153) [Transparency International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transparency_International), [have criticized the final agreement as "toothless" due to its inability to independently investigate or sanction the EU institutions.](#cite_note-154) [Similarly, legal scholars point out that the body lacks investigative powers and the capacity to apply ethical standards to concrete individual cases.](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKotanidisFaucheux202411-155) [Nevertheless, because the body requires each participating institution to conduct a public, written self-assessment of its internal rules, experts suggest that the ensuing public scrutiny and institutional peer pressure may induce institutions to align their standards more closely over time.](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartinez_Navarro2025239-151)

### Law

Constitutionally, the EU bears some resemblance to both a [confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation) and a [federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation), [but has not formally defined itself as either. (It does not have a formal constitution: its status is defined by the](#cite_note-164) [Treaty of European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_European_Union) and the [Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_the_Functioning_of_the_European_Union)). It is more integrated than a traditional confederation of states because the general level of government widely employs [qualified majority voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qualified_majority_voting) in some decision-making among the member states, rather than relying exclusively on unanimity.

The EU is less integrated than a federal state because it is not a state in its own right: sovereignty continues to flow 'from the bottom up', from the several peoples of the separate member states, rather than from a single undifferentiated whole. This is reflected in the fact that the member states remain the 'masters of the Treaties', retaining control over the allocation of competences to the union through constitutional change (thus retaining so-called [Kompetenz-kompetenz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kompetenz-kompetenz)); in that they retain control of the use of armed force; they retain control of taxation; and in that they retain a right of unilateral withdrawal under Article 50 of the Treaty on European Union. In addition, the principle of [subsidiarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsidiarity) requires that only those matters that need to be determined collectively are so determined.

Under the principle of [supremacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremacy_(European_Union_law)), national courts are required to enforce the treaties that their member states have ratified, even if doing so requires them to ignore conflicting national law, and (within limits) even constitutional provisions. [The](#cite_note-Supremacy-167) [direct effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_effect) and supremacy doctrines were not explicitly set out in the European Treaties but were developed by the Court of Justice itself over the 1960s, apparently under the influence of its then most influential judge, Frenchman [Robert Lecourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Lecourt). [The question whether the secondary law enacted by the EU has a comparable status in relation to national legislation, has been a matter of debate among legal scholars.](#cite_note-168)

#### Primary law

The European Union is based on a series of [treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaties_of_the_European_Union). These first established the European Community and the EU, and then made amendments to those founding treaties. [These are power-giving treaties which set broad policy goals and establish institutions with the necessary legal powers to implement those goals. These legal powers include the ability to enact legislation](#cite_note-169) [which can directly affect all member states and their inhabitants.](#cite_note-art249-170) [The EU has](#cite_note-Direct_Effect-171) [legal personality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_personality), with the right to sign agreements and international treaties.

#### Secondary law

The main legal acts of the European Union come in three forms: [regulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulation_(European_Union)), [directives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directive_(European_Union)), and [decisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decision_(European_Union)). Regulations become law in all member states the moment they come into force, without the requirement for any implementing measures, [and automatically override conflicting domestic provisions.](#cite_note-173) [Directives require member states to achieve a certain result while leaving them discretion as to how to achieve the result. The details of how they are to be implemented are left to member states.](#cite_note-art249-170) [When the time limit for implementing directives passes, they may, under certain conditions, have](#cite_note-174) [direct effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_effect) in national law against member states. Decisions offer an alternative to the two above modes of legislation. They are legal acts which only apply to specified individuals, companies or a particular member state. They are most often used in [competition law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competition_law), or on rulings on [State Aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_aid_(European_Union)), but are also frequently used for procedural or administrative matters within the institutions. Regulations, directives, and decisions are of equal legal value and apply without any formal hierarchy.

### Foreign relations

Foreign policy co-operation between member states dates from the establishment of the community in 1957, when member states negotiated as a bloc in international trade negotiations under the [EU's common commercial policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU's_common_commercial_policy). [Steps for more wide-ranging co-ordination in foreign relations began in 1970 with the establishment of](#cite_note-176) [European Political Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Political_Cooperation) which created an informal consultation process between member states with the aim of forming common foreign policies. In 1987 the European Political Cooperation was introduced on a formal basis by the [Single European Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_European_Act). EPC was renamed as the [Common Foreign and Security Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Foreign_and_Security_Policy) (CFSP) by the [Maastricht Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_Treaty).

The stated aims of the CFSP are to promote both the EU's own interests and those of the [international community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_community) as a whole, including the furtherance of international co-operation, respect for human rights, democracy, and the rule of law. [The CFSP requires unanimity among the member states on the appropriate policy to follow on any particular issue. The unanimity and difficult issues treated under the CFSP sometimes lead to disagreements, such as those which occurred over the](#cite_note-art11_Union-178) [war in Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_War).

The coordinator and representative of the CFSP within the EU is the [high representative of the union for foreign affairs and security policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Representative_of_the_Union_for_Foreign_Affairs_and_Security_Policy) who speaks on behalf of the EU in foreign policy and defence matters, and has the task of articulating the positions expressed by the member states on these fields of policy into a common alignment. The high representative heads up the [European External Action Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_External_Action_Service) (EEAS), a unique EU department [that has been officially implemented and operational since 1 December 2010 on the occasion of the first anniversary of the entry into force of the](#cite_note-23Oct-180) [Treaty of Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lisbon). [The EEAS serves as a foreign ministry and](#cite_note-181) [diplomatic corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomatic_corps) for the European Union.

Besides the emerging international policy of the European Union, the international influence of the EU is also felt through [enlargement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlargement_of_the_European_Union). The perceived benefits of becoming a member of the EU act as an incentive for both political and economic reform in states wishing to fulfil the EU's accession criteria, and are considered an important factor contributing to the reform of European formerly Communist countries.[:762 This influence on the internal affairs of other countries is generally referred to as "](#cite_note-183)[soft power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_power)", as opposed to military "hard power".

#### Humanitarian aid

The [European Commission's Humanitarian Aid and Civil Protection department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECHO_(European_Commission)), or "ECHO", provides [humanitarian aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanitarian_aid) from the EU to [developing countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_country). In 2012, its budget amounted to €874million, 51 per cent of the budget went to Africa and 20 per cent to Asia, Latin America, the Caribbean and Pacific, and 20 per cent to the Middle East and Mediterranean. [Humanitarian aid is financed directly by the budget (70 per cent) as part of the financial instruments for external action and also by the](#cite_note-185) [European Development Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Development_Fund) (30 per cent). [The EU's external action financing is divided into geographic instruments and thematic instruments.](#cite_note-ODI-186) [The geographic instruments provide aid through the](#cite_note-ODI-186) [Development Cooperation Instrument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_Cooperation_Instrument) (DCI, €16.9billion, 2007–2013), which must spend 95 per cent of its budget on [official development assistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_development_assistance) (ODA), and from the [European Neighbourhood and Partnership Instrument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Neighbourhood_and_Partnership_Instrument) (ENPI), which contains some relevant programmes.

The European Development Fund (EDF, €22.7billion for the period 2008–2013 and €30.5billion for the period 2014–2020) is made up of voluntary contributions by member states, but there is pressure to merge the EDF into the budget-financed instruments to encourage increased contributions to match the 0.7 per cent target and allow the [European Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Parliament) greater oversight. [In 2016, the average among EU countries was 0.4 per cent and five had met or exceeded the 0.7 per cent target: Denmark, Germany, Luxembourg, Sweden, and the United Kingdom.](#cite_note-187)

#### International cooperation and development partnerships

The European Union uses foreign relations instruments like the [European Neighbourhood Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Neighbourhood_Policy) which seeks to tie those countries to the east and south of the European territory of the EU to the union. These countries, primarily developing countries, include some who seek to one day become either a [member state of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_state_of_the_European_Union), or more closely integrated with the European Union. The EU offers financial assistance to countries within the European Neighbourhood, so long as they meet the strict conditions of government reform, economic reform and other issues surrounding positive transformation. This process is normally underpinned by an Action Plan, as agreed by both Brussels and the target country.

There is also the worldwide [European Union Global Strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Global_Strategy). International recognition of sustainable development as a key element is growing steadily. Its role was recognised in three major UN summits on sustainable development: the 1992 [UN Conference on Environment and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_Summit) (UNCED) in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil; the 2002 World Summit on Sustainable Development (WSSD) in Johannesburg, [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa); and the 2012 [UN Conference on Sustainable Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Conference_on_Sustainable_Development) (UNCSD) in Rio de Janeiro. Other key global agreements are the [Paris Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Agreement) and the [2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_Development_Goals) (United Nations, 2015). The SDGs recognise that all countries must stimulate action in the following key areas – people, [planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet), prosperity, [peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace) and [partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partnership) – in order to tackle the global challenges that are crucial for the survival of [humanity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human).

EU development action is based on the European Consensus on Development, which was endorsed on 20 December 2005 by EU Member States, the council, the European Parliament and the commission. [It is applied from the principles of](#cite_note-189) [capability approach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capability_approach) and [rights-based approach to development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rights-based_approach_to_development). Funding is provided by the [Instrument for Pre-Accession Assistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_for_Pre-Accession_Assistance) and the [Global Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Europe) programmes. Partnership and cooperation agreements are bilateral agreements with non-member nations.

### Defence

The predecessors of the European Union were not devised as a military alliance because [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO) was largely seen as appropriate and sufficient for defence purposes. [In 2025, Europe initiated the](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilkinson2007100-191) [ReArm program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ReArm_Europe), a strategic breakthrough aimed at mobilising local industrial capabilities and bolstering European equipment production, involves a financial investment of €800 billion to support the development and procurement, simultaneously enhancing the continent's overall military readiness and self-sufficiency.

Twenty-three EU members are members of NATO while the remaining member states follow policies of [neutrality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrality_(international_relations)). [The](#cite_note-195) [Western European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_European_Union), a military alliance with a mutual defence clause, closed in 2011 [as its role had been transferred to the EU.](#cite_note-196) [Following the](#cite_note-197) [Kosovo War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_War) in 1999, the [European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Council) agreed that "the Union must have the capacity for autonomous action, backed by credible military forces, the means to decide to use them, and the readiness to do so, in order to respond to international crises without prejudice to actions by NATO". To that end, a number of efforts were made to increase the EU's military capability, notably the [Helsinki Headline Goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki_Headline_Goal) process. After much discussion, the most concrete result was the [EU Battlegroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU_Battlegroup) initiative, each of which is planned to be able to deploy quickly about 1500personnel. [The EU Strategic Compass adopted in 2022 reaffirmed the bloc's partnership with NATO, committed to increased military mobility and formation of a 5,000-strong EU Rapid Deployment Capacity](#cite_note-Council_Capabilities-198)

Since the withdrawal of the United Kingdom, France is the only member officially recognised as a [nuclear weapon state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_with_nuclear_weapons) and the sole holder of a [permanent seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_members_of_the_United_Nations_Security_Council) on the [United Nations Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council). France and Italy are also the only EU countries that have [power projection capabilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_projection) outside of Europe. [Italy, Germany, the Netherlands and Belgium participate in NATO](#cite_note-200) [nuclear sharing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_sharing). [Most EU member states opposed the](#cite_note-201) [Nuclear Weapon Ban Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_the_Prohibition_of_Nuclear_Weapons).

[EU forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EUFOR) have been deployed on peacekeeping missions from middle and northern Africa to the [western Balkans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Balkans) and western Asia. [EU military operations are supported by a number of bodies, including the](#cite_note-Council_Operations-203) [European Defence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Defence_Agency), [European Union Satellite Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Satellite_Centre) and the [European Union Military Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Military_Staff). [The European Union Military Staff is the highest military institution of the European Union, established within the framework of the European Council, and follows on from the decisions of the Helsinki European Council (10–11 December 1999), which called for the establishment of permanent political-military institutions. The European Union Military Staff is under the authority of the](#cite_note-Council_Structures-204) [High Representative of the Union for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Representative_of_the_Union_for_Foreign_Affairs_and_Security_Policy) and the Political and Security Committee. It directs all military activities in the EU context, including planning and conducting military missions and operations in the framework of the [Common Security and Defence Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Security_and_Defence_Policy) and the development of military capabilities, and provides the Political and Security Committee with military advice and recommendations on military issues. In an EU consisting of 27 members, substantial security and defence co-operation is increasingly relying on collaboration among all member states.

The European Border and Coast Guard Agency ([Frontex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontex)) is an [agency of the EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agencies_of_the_European_Union) aiming to detect and stop illegal immigration, [human trafficking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_trafficking) and terrorist infiltration. [The EU also operates the](#cite_note-206) [European Travel Information and Authorisation System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Travel_Information_and_Authorisation_System), the [Entry/Exit System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entry/Exit_System), the [Schengen Information System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schengen_Information_System), the [Visa Information System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visa_Information_System) and the [Common European Asylum System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_European_Asylum_System) which provide common databases for police and immigration authorities. The impetus for the development of this co-operation was the advent of open borders in the Schengen Area and the associated cross-border crime.

## Geography

The EU's member states cover an area of 4,233,262 square kilometres (1,634,472sqmi), [and therefore a large part of the](#cite_note-Area.and.population.figure-207) [European continent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe). The EU's highest peak is [Mont Blanc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mont_Blanc) in the [Graian Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graian_Alps), 4,810.45 metres (15,782ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level). [The lowest points in the EU are](#cite_note-208) [Lammefjorden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lammefjorden), Denmark, and [Zuidplaspolder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuidplaspolder), Netherlands, at 7m (23ft) below sea level. The landscape, climate, and economy of the EU are influenced by its coastline, which is 53,563 kilometres (33,283mi) long. [In addition to national territories in Europe, there are 32](#cite_note-209) [special territories of members of the European Economic Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_territories_of_members_of_the_European_Economic_Area), not all of which are part of the EU. The largest by area is [Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenland), which is [not part of the EU but whose citizens are EU citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenland_and_the_European_Union), while the largest by population are the [Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Islands) off Africa, which are part of the EU and the [Schengen area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schengen_area). [French Guiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Guiana) in South America is part of the EU and the eurozone, as is [Mayotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayotte), north of [Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar).

### Climate

The climate of the European Union is of a [temperate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_climate), [continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_climate) nature, with a [maritime climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_climate) prevailing on the western coasts and a [mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) in the south. The climate is strongly conditioned by the [Gulf Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Stream), which warms the western region to levels unattainable at similar latitudes on other continents. Western Europe is oceanic, while eastern Europe is continental and dry. Four seasons occur in western Europe, while southern Europe experiences a [wet season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_season) and a [dry season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_season). Southern Europe is hot and dry during the summer months. The heaviest precipitation occurs downwind of water bodies due to the prevailing [westerlies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westerlies), with higher amounts also seen in the [Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alps).

### Environment

In 1957, when the European Economic Community was founded, it had no environmental policy. [Over the past 50 years, an increasingly dense network of legislation has been created, extending to all areas of environmental protection, including air pollution, water quality, waste management, nature conservation, and the control of chemicals, industrial hazards, and biotechnology.](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJordanAdelle2012-213) [According to the](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJordanAdelle2012-213) [Institute for European Environmental Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_European_Environmental_Policy), environmental law comprises over 500 Directives, Regulations and Decisions, making environmental policy a core area of European politics.

European policy-makers originally increased the EU's capacity to act on environmental issues by defining it as a trade problem.[Trade barriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_barrier) and competitive distortions in the Common Market could emerge due to the different environmental standards in each member state. [In subsequent years, the environment became a formal policy area, with its own policy actors, principles and procedures. The legal basis for EU environmental policy was established with the introduction of the Single European Act in 1987.](#cite_note-215)

Initially, EU environmental policy focused on Europe. More recently, the EU has demonstrated leadership in global environmental governance, e.g. the role of the EU in securing the ratification and coming into force of the [Kyoto Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_Protocol) despite opposition from the United States. This international dimension is reflected in the EU's Sixth Environmental Action Programme, [which recognises that its objectives can only be achieved if key international agreements are actively supported and properly implemented both at EU level and worldwide. The Lisbon Treaty further strengthened the leadership ambitions.](#cite_note-216) [EU law has played a significant role in improving habitat and species protection in Europe, as well as contributing to improvements in air and water quality and waste management.](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJordanAdelle2012-213)

Mitigating [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change) is one of the top priorities of EU environmental policy. In 2007, member states agreed that, in the future, 20 per cent of the energy used across the EU must be [renewable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy), and carbon dioxide emissions have to be lower in 2020 by at least 20 per cent compared to 1990 levels. [In 2017, the EU emitted 9.1 per cent of global](#cite_note-EUO_energy-217) [greenhouse-gas emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse-gas_emissions). [The European Union claims that already in 2018, its GHG emissions were 23% lower than in 1990.](#cite_note-Global_Emissions-218)

The EU has adopted an [emissions trading system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emissions_trading) to incorporate [carbon emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_emissions) into the economy. [The](#cite_note-220) [European Green Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Green_Capital) is an annual award given to cities that focuses on the environment, energy efficiency, and quality of life in urban areas to create [smart city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_city). In the [2019 elections to the European Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_European_Parliament_election), the green parties increased their power, possibly because of the rise of post materialist values. [Proposals to reach a zero carbon economy in the European Union by 2050 were suggested in 2018–2019. Almost all member states supported that goal at an EU summit in June 2019. The Czech Republic, Estonia, Hungary, and Poland disagreed.](#cite_note-221) [In June 2021, the European Union passed a](#cite_note-222) [European Climate Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Climate_Law?action=edit&redlink=1) with targets of 55% GHG emissions reduction by 2030 and [carbon neutrality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_neutrality) by 2050. [Also in the same year, the European Union and the United States pledged to cut](#cite_note-223) [methane emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methane_emissions) by 30% by 2030. The pledge is considered as a big achievement for climate change mitigation. [A research report from November 2024 declared that the](#cite_note-224) [Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Republic) is the EU's most toxic country in Europe for car emissions.

In 2025, The European Union together with [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China), [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil) and others, joined the [Global carbon market coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_Coalition_(COP30_proposal)). [According to some calculations, a global carbon market can speed up emission reduction seven-fold.](#cite_note-226)

## Economy

The [gross domestic product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_domestic_product) (GDP), a measure of economic activity, of EU member states was US$16.64 trillion in 2022, around 16.6 per cent of the world GDP. [There is a significant variation in GDP per capita between and within individual EU states. The difference between the richest and poorest regions (281 NUTS-2 regions of the](#cite_note-228) [Nomenclature of Territorial Units for Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomenclature_of_Territorial_Units_for_Statistics)) ranged, in 2017, from 31 per cent (Severozapaden, Bulgaria) of the EU28 average (€30,000) to 253 per cent (Luxembourg), or from €4,600 to €92,600.

EU member states own the estimated third largest after the United States (US$140trillion) and [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China) (US$84trillion) [net wealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_wealth) in the world, equal to around one sixth (US$76trillion) of the US$454trillion global wealth. [Of the top](#cite_note-databook2023-230) [500 largest corporations in the world measured by revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_Global_500) in 2024, 90 had their headquarters in the EU. [In 2016, unemployment in the EU stood at 8.9 per cent](#cite_note-231) [while inflation was at 2.2 per cent, and the account balance at −0.9 per cent of GDP. The average annual net earnings in the European Union was around €25,000](#cite_note-UNEMP-232) [in 2021.](#cite_note-233)

### Economic and monetary union

The [euro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro) is the official currency in 21 member states of the EU. The creation of a [European single currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Currency_Unit) became an official objective of the European Economic Community in 1969. In 1992, having negotiated the structure and procedures of a currency union, the member states signed the [Maastricht Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_Treaty) and were legally bound to fulfil the agreed-on rules including the [convergence criteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_convergence_criteria) if they wanted to join the [monetary union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency_union). The states wanting to participate had first to join the [European Exchange Rate Mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Exchange_Rate_Mechanism). To prevent the joining states from getting into financial trouble or crisis after entering the monetary union, they were obliged in the Maastricht treaty to fulfil important financial obligations and procedures, especially to show budgetary discipline and a high degree of sustainable economic convergence, as well as to avoid excessive government deficits and limit the government debt to a sustainable level, as agreed in the [European Fiscal Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Fiscal_Pact).

#### Capital Markets Union and financial institutions

Free movement of capital is intended to permit movement of investments such as property purchases and buying of shares between countries. [Until the drive towards](#cite_note-Europa_Single_Market_C-234) [economic and monetary union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_and_monetary_union) the development of the capital provisions had been slow. Post-Maastricht there has been a rapidly developing corpus of ECJ judgements regarding this initially neglected freedom. The free movement of capital is unique insofar as it is granted equally to non-member states.

The [European System of Financial Supervision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_System_of_Financial_Supervision) is an institutional architecture of the EU's framework of financial supervision composed by three authorities: the [European Banking Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Banking_Authority), the [European Insurance and Occupational Pensions Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Insurance_and_Occupational_Pensions_Authority) and the [European Securities and Markets Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Securities_and_Markets_Authority). To complement this framework, there is also a [European Systemic Risk Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Systemic_Risk_Board) under the responsibility of the central bank. The aim of this financial control system is to ensure the economic stability of the EU.

#### Eurozone and banking union

In 1999, the currency union started to materialise through introducing a common accounting (virtual) currency in [eleven of the member states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_euro). In 2002, it was turned into a fully-fledged conventible currency, when [euro notes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_banknotes) and coins were issued, while the phaseout of national currencies in the eurozone (consisting by then of 12 member states) was initiated. The eurozone (constituted by the EU member states which have adopted the euro) has since grown to 20 countries.

The 20 EU member states known collectively as the [eurozone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurozone) have fully implemented the currency union by superseding their national currencies with the [euro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro). The currency union represents 345million EU citizens. [The euro is the second largest](#cite_note-Europa_Internal_Market-19) [reserve currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_currency) as well as the second most traded currency in the world after the [United States dollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_dollar).

The euro, and the monetary policies of those who have adopted it in agreement with the EU, are under the control of the ECB. [The ECB is the central bank for the eurozone, and thus controls](#cite_note-ECB_org-241) [monetary policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monetary_policy) in that area with an agenda to maintain [price stability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price_stability). It is at the centre of the [Eurosystem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurosystem), which comprehends all the eurozone national central banks. [The ECB is also the central institution of the](#cite_note-ECB_org-241) [Banking Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banking_Union) established within the eurozone, as the hub of [European Banking Supervision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Banking_Supervision). There is also a [Single Resolution Mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_Resolution_Mechanism) in case of a bank default.

### Trade

As a political entity, the European Union is represented in the [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization) (WTO). Two of the original core objectives of the European Economic Community were the development of a common market, subsequently becoming a [single market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_market), and a [customs union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Customs_Union) between its member states.

#### Single market

The single market involves [the free circulation of goods, capital, people, and services within the EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Single_Market), [The free movement of services and of establishment allows self-employed persons to move between member states to provide services on a temporary or permanent basis. While services account for 60 per cent to 70 per cent of GDP, legislation in the area is not as developed as in other areas. This lacuna has been addressed by the](#cite_note-Europa_Internal_Market-19) [Services in the Internal Market Directive 2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Services_in_the_Internal_Market_Directive_2006) which aims to liberalise the cross border provision of services. [According to the treaty the provision of services is a residual freedom that only applies if no other freedom is being exercised.](#cite_note-Europa_Single_Market_S-242)

#### Customs union

The customs union involves the application of a [common external tariff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_external_tariff) on all goods entering the market. Once goods have been admitted into the market they cannot be subjected to customs duties, discriminatory taxes or [import quotas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Import_quota), as they travel internally. The non-EU member states of [Iceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iceland), [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway), [Liechtenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liechtenstein) and [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland) participate in the single market but not in the customs union. [Half the trade in the EU is covered by legislation harmonised by the EU.](#cite_note-EEA-87)

The [European Union Association Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Association_Agreement) does something similar for a much larger range of countries, partly as a so-called soft approach ('a carrot instead of a stick') to influence the politics in those countries.
The European Union represents all its members at the [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization) (WTO), and acts on behalf of member states in any disputes. When the EU negotiates trade related agreement outside the WTO framework, the subsequent agreement must be approved by each individual EU member state government.

#### Competition and consumer protection

The EU operates a [competition policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_competition_law) intended to ensure undistorted competition within the single market. [In 2001 the commission for the first time prevented a merger between two companies based in the United States (](#cite_note-245)[General Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric) and [Honeywell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honeywell)) which had already been approved by their national authority. [Another high-profile case,](#cite_note-246) [against Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Microsoft_competition_case), resulted in the commission fining [Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft) over €777million following nine years of legal action.

#### External trade

The European Union has concluded [free trade agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_free_trade_agreements) (FTAs) [and other agreements with a trade component with many countries worldwide and is negotiating with many others.](#cite_note-248) [The European Union's services trade surplus rose from $16 billion in 2000 to more than $250 billion in 2018.](#cite_note-249) [In 2020, in part due to the COVID-19 pandemic, China became the EU's largest trading partner, displacing the United States.](#cite_note-250) [The European Union is the largest exporter in the world](#cite_note-251) [and in 2008 was the largest importer of goods and services.](#cite_note-252) [Internal trade between the member states is aided by the removal of barriers to trade such as](#cite_note-254) [tariffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariff) and [border controls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_control). In the [eurozone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurozone), trade is helped by not having any currency differences to deal with amongst most members. [Externally, the EU's free-trade agreement with](#cite_note-Se-jeong-244) [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan) is perhaps its most notable one. The [EU-Japan Economic Partnership Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan–European_Union_relations#Political_relations_and_agreements) was officially signed on 17 July 2018, becoming the world's largest bilateral free trade deal when it went into effect on 1 February 2019, creating an open trade zone covering nearly one-third of global GDP.

### Energy

- Oil (31.7%)
- Natural gas (24.7%)
- Coal (10.9%)
- Nuclear (13.2%)
- Biofuels, waste, electricity, heat (19.4%)

The total energy supply of the EU was 59 billion [GJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_(unit)) in 2019, about 10.2 per cent of the world total. Approximately three fifths of the energy available in the EU came from imports (mostly of fossil fuels). [Renewable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy) contributed 18.1 per cent of the EU's total energy supply in 2019, and 11.1 per cent of the final energy consumption. [The EU has had legislative power in the area of energy policy for most of its existence; this has its roots in the original](#cite_note-ec.europa.eu-257) [European Coal and Steel Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Coal_and_Steel_Community). The introduction of a mandatory and comprehensive European energy policy was approved at the meeting of the European Council in October 2005, and the first draft policy was published in January 2007.

The EU has five key points in its energy policy: increase competition in the [internal market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Single_Market), encourage investment and boost interconnections between electricity grids; diversify energy resources with better systems to respond to a crisis; establish a new treaty framework for energy co-operation with Russia while improving relations with energy-rich states in Central Asia [and North Africa; use existing energy supplies more efficiently while increasing](#cite_note-oies-259) [renewable energy commercialisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_commercialisation); and finally increase funding for new energy technologies.

In 2007, EU countries as a whole imported 82 per cent of their oil, 57 per cent of their natural gas [and 97.48 per cent of their uranium](#cite_note-low_carb_prop-260) [demands. The three largest suppliers of natural gas to the European Union are Russia, Norway and](#cite_note-Euratom2007-261) [Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeria), that amounted for about three quarters of the imports in 2019. [There is a strong](#cite_note-262) [dependence on Russian energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_in_the_European_energy_sector) that the EU has been attempting to reduce.

In May 2022, it was reported that the European Union was preparing another sanction against Russia over its invasion of Ukraine. It is expected to target Russian oil, Russian and Belarusian banks, as well as individuals and companies. According to an article by Reuters, two diplomats stated that the European Union may impose a ban on imports of Russian oil by the end of 2022. [Also in May 2022, the](#cite_note-264) [European Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commission) published the RePowerEU initiative, a €300 billion plan outlining the path towards the end of EU dependence on Russian fossil fuels by 2030 and the acceleration on the clean energy transition.

### Transport

The European Union manages cross-border road, railway, airport and water infrastructure through the [Trans-European Transport Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-European_Transport_Network) (TEN-T), created in 1990, [and the](#cite_note-Axes_page_7-266) [Trans-European Combined Transport network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-European_Combined_Transport_network). TEN-T comprises two network layers: the Core Network, which is to be completed by 2030; and the Comprehensive Network, which is to be completed by 2050. The network is currently made up of 9 core corridors: the [Baltic–Adriatic Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic–Adriatic_Corridor), the [North Sea–Baltic Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea–Baltic_Corridor), the [Mediterranean Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Corridor), the [Orient/East–Med Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orient/East–Med_Corridor), the [Scandinavian–Mediterranean Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavian–Mediterranean_Corridor), the [Rhine–Alpine Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine–Alpine_Corridor), the Atlantic Corridor, the [North Sea–Mediterranean Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea–Mediterranean_Corridor), and the [Rhine–Danube Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine–Danube_Corridor). Road transportation was organised under the TEN-T by the [Trans-European road network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-European_road_network). [Bundesautobahn 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_7) is the longest national [motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorway) in the EU at 963km (598mi).

Maritime transportation is organised under the TEN-T by the [Trans-European Inland Waterway network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-European_Inland_Waterway_network), and the [Trans-European Seaport network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-European_Seaport_network). European [seaports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaport) are categorised as international, community, or regional. The [Port of Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Rotterdam) is the busiest in the EU, and the world's largest seaport outside of [East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia), located in and near the city of [Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam), in the province of [South Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Holland) in the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands). [The](#cite_note-267) [European Maritime Safety Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Maritime_Safety_Agency) (EMSA), founded in 2002 in [Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon), Portugal, is charged with reducing the risk of [maritime accidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipwreck), [marine pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_pollution) from ships and the [loss of human lives at sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_and_rescue) by helping to enforce the pertinent EU legislation.

Air transportation is organised under the TEN-T by the [Trans-European Airport network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-European_Airport_network). European [airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport) are categorised as international, community, or regional. The [Charles de Gaulle Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle_Airport) is the busiest in the EU, located in and near the city of [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris), in France. [The](#cite_note-:FR2021-268) [European Common Aviation Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Common_Aviation_Area) (ECAA) is a [single market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_market) in [aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation). ECAA agreements were signed on 5 May 2006 in [Salzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salzburg), Austria between the EU and some third countries. The ECAA liberalises the air transport industry by allowing any company from any ECAA member state to fly between any ECAA member states airports, thereby allowing a "foreign" airline to provide domestic flights. The [Single European Sky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_European_Sky) (SES) is an initiative that seeks to reform the European [air traffic management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_traffic_management) system through a series of actions carried out in four different levels (institutional, operational, technological and control and supervision) with the aim of satisfying the needs of the European airspace in terms of capacity, safety, efficiency and environmental impact. Civil [aviation safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_safety) is under the responsibility of the [European Union Aviation Safety Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Aviation_Safety_Agency) (EASA). It carries out [certification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_certificate), regulation and standardisation and also performs investigation and monitoring. The idea of a European-level aviation safety authority goes back to 1996, but the agency was only legally established in 2002, and began operating in 2003.

Rail transportation is organised under the TEN-T by the [Trans-European Rail network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-European_Rail_network), made up of the [high-speed rail network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-European_high-speed_rail_network) and the [conventional rail network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-European_conventional_rail_network). The [Gare du Nord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gare_du_Nord) railway station is the busiest in the EU, located in and near the city of [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris), in France.[Rail transport in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Europe) is being synchronised with the [European Rail Traffic Management System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Rail_Traffic_Management_System) (ERTMS) with the goal of greatly enhancing safety, increase efficiency of train transports and enhance cross-border interoperability. This is done by replacing former national [signalling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_signal) equipment and operational procedures with a single new Europe-wide standard for train control and command systems. This system is conducted by the [European Union Agency for Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Agency_for_Railways) (ERA).

#### Schengen Area

The Schengen Area is an area comprising 29 European countries that have officially abolished all passport and all other types of [border control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_control) at their mutual borders. Being an element within the wider [area of freedom, security and justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_of_freedom,_security_and_justice) policy of the EU, it mostly functions as a single jurisdiction under [a common visa policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visa_policies_in_the_European_Union) for international travel purposes. The area is named after the 1985 [Schengen Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schengen_Agreement) and the 1990 [Schengen Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schengen_Convention), both signed in [Schengen, Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schengen,_Luxembourg). Of the 27 EU member states, 25 participate in the Schengen Area. Of the EU members that are not part of the Schengen Area, one—[Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_and_the_European_Union)—is legally obligated to join the area in the future; [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ireland_and_the_European_Union) maintains an [opt-out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opt-outs_in_the_European_Union), and instead operates [its own visa policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visa_policy_of_Ireland). The four [European Free Trade Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Free_Trade_Association) (EFTA) member states, [Iceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iceland–European_Union_relations), [Liechtenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liechtenstein-European_Union_relations), [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway-European_Union_relations), and [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland-European_Union_relations), are not members of the EU, but have signed agreements in association with the Schengen Agreement. Also, three [European microstates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_microstates)—[Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monaco), [San Marino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Marino) and the [Vatican City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatican_City)—maintain open borders for passenger traffic with their neighbours, and are therefore considered de facto members of the Schengen Area due to the practical impossibility of travelling to or from them without transiting through at least one Schengen member country.

### Electronic communications and space

Mobile communication [roaming charges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_roaming_regulations) are abolished throughout the EU, Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway.

The [European Union Agency for the Space Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Agency_for_the_Space_Programme) (EUSPA), headquartered in [Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague), Czech Republic, was established in 2021 to manage the [European Union Space Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Space_Programme) in order to implement the pre-existing European Space Policy, established on 22 May 2007 between the EU and the [European Space Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Space_Agency) (ESA), known collectively as the European Space Council. This was the first common political framework for space activities established by the EU. Each member state has pursued to some extent their own national space policy, though often co-ordinating through the ESA. [Günter Verheugen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Günter_Verheugen), the [European Commissioner for Enterprise and Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commissioner_for_Enterprise_and_Industry), has stated that even though the EU is "a world leader in the technology", it was "being put on the defensive by the United States and Russia and that it only has about a 10-year technological advantage on China and India, which are racing to catch up".

[Galileo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo_(satellite_navigation)) is a [global navigation satellite system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_navigation) (GNSS) that went live in 2016, created by the EU through the ESA, operated by the EUSPA, with two ground operations centres in [Fucino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fucine_Lake), Italy, and [Oberpfaffenhofen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberpfaffenhofen), Germany. The €10 billion project is named after the Italian astronomer [Galileo Galilei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo_Galilei). One of the aims of Galileo is to provide an independent high-precision positioning system so European political and military authorities do not have to rely on the US [GPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Positioning_System), or the Russian [GLONASS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GLONASS) systems, which could be disabled or degraded by their operators at any time. The [European Geostationary Navigation Overlay Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Geostationary_Navigation_Overlay_Service) (EGNOS) is a [satellite-based augmentation system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite-based_augmentation_system) (SBAS) developed by the ESA and [EUROCONTROL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EUROCONTROL). Currently, it supplements the GPS by reporting on the reliability and accuracy of their positioning data and sending out corrections. The system will supplement Galileo in a future version. The [Copernicus Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copernicus_Programme) is the EU's Earth observation programme coordinated and managed by EUSPA in partnership with ESA. It aims at achieving a global, continuous, autonomous, high quality, wide range Earth observation capacity, providing accurate, timely and easily accessible information to, among other things, improve the management of the environment, understand and [mitigate the effects of climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_mitigation), and ensure civil security.

### Agriculture and fisheries

The Common Agricultural Policy (CAP) is the [agricultural policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_policy) of the European Union. It implements a system of [agricultural subsidies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_subsidies) and other programmes. It was introduced in 1962 and has since then undergone several changes to reduce the EEC budget cost (from 73% in 1985 to 37% in 2017) and consider rural development in its aims. It has, however, been criticised on the grounds of its cost and its environmental and humanitarian effects.

Likewise, the Common Fisheries Policy (CFP) is the [fisheries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisheries) policy of the European Union. It sets quotas for which member states are allowed to catch each type of fish, as well as encouraging the [fishing industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_industry) by various market interventions and [fishing subsidies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_subsidies). It was introduced in 2009 with the Treaty of Lisbon, which formally enshrined fisheries conservation policy as one of the handful of "exclusive competences" reserved for the European Union.

### Labour

The free movement of persons means that [EU citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship_of_the_European_Union) can move freely between member states to live, work, study or retire in another country. This required the lowering of administrative formalities and recognition of professional qualifications of other states. [The EU seasonally adjusted unemployment rate was 6.7 per cent in September 2018.](#cite_note-Europa_Single_Market_P-272) [The euro area unemployment rate was 8.1 per cent.](#cite_note-unemployment-273) [Among the member states, the lowest unemployment rates were recorded in the Czech Republic (2.3 per cent), Germany and Poland (both 3.4 per cent), and the highest in Spain (10.61 per cent in 2024)](#cite_note-unemployment-273) [and Greece (9.6 per cent in November 2024).](#cite_note-274) [The European Union has long sought to prevent](#cite_note-275) [social](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_dumping) and [environmental dumping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_dumping). [To this end, it has adopted laws establishing minimum employment and environmental standards. These included the](#cite_note-276) [Working Time Directive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_Time_Directive) and the [Environmental Impact Assessment Directive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_Impact_Assessment_Directive_2011). The European Directive about Minimum Wage, which looks to lift minimum wages and strengthen collective bargaining was approved by the European Parliament in September 2022.

### Regional development

The five [European Structural and Investment Funds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Structural_and_Investment_Funds) are supporting the development of the EU regions, primarily the underdeveloped ones, located mostly in the states of [central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Europe) and southern Europe. [Another fund (the](#cite_note-Business2000_Funds-279) [Instrument for Pre-Accession Assistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_for_Pre-Accession_Assistance)) provides support for candidate members to transform their country to conform to the EU's standard. Demographic transition to a society of ageing population, low fertility-rates and depopulation of non-metropolitan regions is tackled within this policies.

## Demographics

### Population

The population of the EU in 2026 was just under 452million people, corresponding to 5.4 per cent of the world population. [The](#cite_note-280) [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) across the EU was 106 inhabitants per square kilometre, which is more than the world average. [It is highest in areas in central and western Europe, sometimes referred to as the "](#cite_note-ec.europa.eu-257)[blue banana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_banana)", while [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden) and [Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland) in the north are much more sparsely populated. The total population of the EU has been slightly decreasing for several years, contracting by 0.04 per cent in 2021. [This is due to a low birth rate of about 1.5 children per woman, less than the world average of 2.3.](#cite_note-ec.europa.eu-257) [In total, 4.1 million babies were born in the EU in 2021.](#cite_note-ec.europa.eu-257)

Of the people residing in the EU, 5.3 per cent are not [EU citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_citizenship). [There were 31 non-EU citizenships that each accounted for at least 1 per cent of non-EU citizens living in the EU, of which the largest were](#cite_note-ec.europa.eu-257) [Moroccan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco), [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey), [Syrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria) and [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China). [Around 1.9 million people](#cite_note-ec.europa.eu-257) [immigrated to one of the EU member states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Europe) from a non-EU country during 2020, and a total of 956,000 people emigrated from a member state to go to a non-EU country during the same year.

#### Urbanisation

More than two thirds (68.2%) of EU inhabitants lived in [urban areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area) in 2020, which is slightly less than the world average. [Cities are largely spread out across the EU with a large grouping in and around the](#cite_note-ec.europa.eu-257) [Benelux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benelux). [The EU contains about 40 urban areas with populations of over 1million. With a population of over 13 million,](#cite_note-282)[Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris) is the largest metropolitan area and the only [megacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megacity) in the EU.

Paris is followed by [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid), [Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona), [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin), the [Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr), [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan), and [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome), all with a metropolitan population of over 4million. The EU also has numerous [polycentric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conurbation) urbanised regions like [Rhine-Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine-Ruhr) ([Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne), [Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund), [Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Düsseldorf) et al.), [Randstad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randstad) ([Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam), [Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam), [The Hague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hague), [Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht) et al.), [Frankfurt Rhine-Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Rhine-Main) ([Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt), [Wiesbaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiesbaden), [Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainz) et al.), the [Flemish Diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flemish_Diamond) ([Antwerp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antwerp), [Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels), [Leuven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leuven), [Ghent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghent) et al.) and [Upper Silesian-Moravian area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Silesian-Moravian_metropolitan_area) ([Katowice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katowice), [Ostrava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrava) et al.).

The EU has 24 official languages: [Bulgarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_language), [Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_language), [Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_language), [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language), [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language), [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language), [Estonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonian_language), [Finnish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_language), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language), [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language), [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language), [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_language), [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_language), [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_language), [Latvian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_language), [Lithuanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_language), [Maltese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_language), [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language), [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language), [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language), [Slovak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovak_language), [Slovene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovene_language), [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_language), and [Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_language). Important documents, such as legislation, are translated into every official language and the [European Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Parliament) provides translation for documents and plenary sessions. [Most EU institutions use only a handful of working languages: the European Commission conducts its internal business in three procedural languages: English, French, and German;](#cite_note-291) [the](#cite_note-procedural-292) [Court of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Justice_of_the_European_Union) uses French as the working language, [and the](#cite_note-293) [European Central Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Central_Bank) conducts its business primarily in English. [Even though](#cite_note-295) [language policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_policy) is the responsibility of member states, EU institutions promote multilingualism among its citizens.

The most widely spoken language in the EU is [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language); the language is spoken by 50 per cent of the population [and studied by 95 per cent of school students,](#cite_note-EU-Lang-286) [although following the](#cite_note-297) [withdrawal of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brexit) only 2% of the population speak it natively.[German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) are spoken by 29 per cent and 25 per cent of the population. More than half (59 per cent) of EU citizens are able to engage in a conversation in a language other than their mother tongue, and more than a tenth (11%) are quadrilingual.

[Luxembourgish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxembourgish) (in Luxembourg) and [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language) (in Cyprus) are the only two national languages that are not official languages of the EU. [Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_language), [Galician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galician_language) and [Basque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_language) are not recognised official languages of the EU but have official status in Spain. Therefore, official translations of the treaties are made into them and citizens have the right to correspond with the institutions in these languages. [There are about 150](#cite_note-299) [regional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_language) and [minority languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_language) in the EU, spoken by up to 50million people. [The](#cite_note-Many_tongues,_one_family-300) [European Charter for Regional or Minority Languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Charter_for_Regional_or_Minority_Languages) ratified by most EU states provides general guidelines that states can follow to protect their linguistic heritage. The [European Day of Languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Day_of_Languages) is held annually on 26 September and is aimed at encouraging language learning across Europe.

The EU has no formal connection to any religion. Article 17 of the [Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_the_Functioning_of_the_European_Union) [recognises the "status under national law of churches and religious associations" as well as that of "philosophical and non-confessional organisations".](#cite_note-302) [The preamble to the](#cite_note-Consolidated_Treaties-303) [Treaty on European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_Treaty) mentions the "cultural, religious and humanist inheritance of Europe". [Discussion over the draft texts of the European Constitution and later the](#cite_note-304) [Treaty of Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lisbon) included proposals to mention [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity) or a [god](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God), or both, in the preamble of the text, but the idea faced opposition and was dropped.

Christians in the EU include [Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church) of both [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church) and [Eastern Rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Catholic_Churches), numerous [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) denominations with [Lutherans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheranism), [Anglicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicans), and [Reformed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformed_tradition) forming the majority of Protestant affiliations, and the [Eastern Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church). In 2009, the EU had an estimated [Muslim population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_Muslim_population) of 13million, [and an estimated](#cite_note-mgmpPRC-306) [Jewish population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_population) of over a million. [The other world religions of](#cite_note-307) [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism), [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism), and [Sikhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism) are also represented in the EU population. [Eurostat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurostat)'s [Eurobarometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurobarometer) opinion polls showed in 2005 that 52 per cent of EU citizens believed in a god, 27 per cent in "some sort of spirit or life force", and 18 per cent had no form of belief.

Into the 21st century, many countries have experienced falling church attendance and membership. [The countries where the fewest people reported a religious belief were Estonia (16 per cent) and the Czech Republic (19 per cent).](#cite_note-About_SecE-309) [The most religious countries were Malta (95 per cent, predominantly Catholic) as well as Cyprus and Romania (both predominantly Orthodox) each with about 90 per cent of citizens professing a belief in God. Across the EU, belief was higher among women, older people, those with religious upbringing, those who left school at 15 or 16, and those "positioning themselves on the right of the political scale".](#cite_note-Eurostat_Religion-308)

### Education and research

Basic education is an area where the EU's role is limited to supporting national governments. In higher education, the policy was developed in the 1980s in programmes supporting exchanges and mobility. The most visible of these has been the [Erasmus Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_Programme), a university exchange programme which began in 1987. In its first 20years, it supported international exchange opportunities for well over 1.5million university and college students and became a symbol of European student life.

There are similar programmes for school pupils and teachers, for trainees in [vocational education and training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocational_education), and for adult learners in the [Lifelong Learning Programme 2007–2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifelong_Learning_Programme_2007–2013). These programmes are designed to encourage a wider knowledge of other countries and to spread good practices in the education and training fields across the EU. [Through its support of the](#cite_note-312) [Bologna Process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna_Process), the EU is supporting comparable standards and compatible degrees across Europe. Scientific development is facilitated through the EU's [Framework Programmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framework_Programmes_for_Research_and_Technological_Development), the first of which started in 1984. The aims of EU policy in this area are to co-ordinate and stimulate research. The independent [European Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Research_Council) allocates EU funds to European or national research projects. [EU research and technological framework programmes deal in a number of areas, for example energy where the aim is to develop a diverse mix of](#cite_note-313) [renewable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy) to help the environment and to reduce dependence on imported fuels.

### Health

Article 35 of the [Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_of_Fundamental_Rights_of_the_European_Union) affirms that "A high level of human health protection shall be ensured in the definition and implementation of all Union policies and activities". The [European Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commission)'s [Directorate-General for Health and Consumers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directorate-General_for_Health_and_Consumers_(European_Commission)) seeks to align national laws on the protection of people's health, on the consumers' rights, on the safety of food and other products.

All EU and many other European countries offer their citizens a free [European Health Insurance Card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Health_Insurance_Card) which, on a reciprocal basis, provides insurance for emergency medical treatment insurance when visiting other participating European countries. [A directive on cross-border healthcare aims at promoting co-operation on health care between member states and facilitating access to safe and high-quality cross-border healthcare for European patients.](#cite_note-318)

The life expectancy in the EU was 80.1 year at birth in 2021, among the highest in the world and around nine years higher than the world average. [In general, life expectancy is lower in](#cite_note-ec.europa.eu-257) [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe) than in [Western Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Europe). [In 2018, the EU region with the highest life expectancy was](#cite_note-322) [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comunidad_de_Madrid), Spain at 85.2 years, followed by the Spanish regions of [La Rioja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Rioja_(Spain)) and [Castilla y León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castilla_y_León) both at 84.3 years, [Trentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trentino) in Italy at 84.3 years and [Île-de-France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Île-de-France) in France at 84.2 years.

### Social rights and equality

The EU has also sought to coordinate the social security and health systems of member states to facilitate individuals exercising free movement rights and to ensure they maintain their ability to access social security and health services in other member states. Since 2019 there has been a European commissioner for equality and the [European Institute for Gender Equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Institute_for_Gender_Equality) has existed since 2007. A Directive on countering gender-based violence has been proposed. [In September 2022, a European Care strategy was approved in order to provide "quality, affordable and accessible care services".](#cite_note-325) [The](#cite_note-326) [European Social Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Social_Charter) is the main body that recognises the social rights of European citizens. In 2020, the first ever European Union Strategy on LGBTIQ equality was approved under [Helena Dalli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helena_Dalli) mandate. [In December 2021, the commission announced the intention of codifying a union-wide law against LGBT hate crimes.](#cite_note-327)

### Freedom, security and justice

Since the creation of the European Union in 1993, it has developed its competencies in the area of justice and home affairs; initially at an intergovernmental level and later by supranationalism. Accordingly, the union has legislated in areas such as [extradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Arrest_Warrant), [family law,](#cite_note-329) [asylum law,](#cite_note-330) [and criminal justice.](#cite_note-331)

The EU has also established agencies to co-ordinate police, prosecution and civil litigations across the member states: [Europol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europol) for police co-operation, [CEPOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CEPOL) for training of police forces [and the](#cite_note-333) [Eurojust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurojust) for co-operation between prosecutors and courts. [It also operates the](#cite_note-334) [EUCARIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EUCARIS) database of vehicles and drivers, the [Eurodac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurodac), the [European Criminal Records Information System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Criminal_Records_Information_System), the [European Cybercrime Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Cybercrime_Centre), [FADO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FADO), [PRADO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Register_of_Travel_and_Identity_Documents_Online) and others.

Prohibitions against discrimination have a long standing in the treaties. In more recent years, these have been supplemented by powers to legislate against discrimination based on race, religion, disability, age, and sexual orientation. [The treaties declare that the European Union itself is "founded on the values of respect for](#cite_note-art2(7)_Amsterdam-335) [human dignity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_dignity), [freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty), democracy, [equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equality_before_the_law), the [rule of law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_law) and respect for human rights, including the rights of persons belonging to [minorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_group)... in a society in which pluralism, non-discrimination, tolerance, justice, solidarity and equality between women and men prevail." [By virtue of these powers, the EU has enacted legislation on](#cite_note-336) [sexism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexism) in the work-place, [age discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ageism), and [racial discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism).

In 2009, the [Lisbon Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon_Treaty) gave legal effect to the [Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_of_Fundamental_Rights_of_the_European_Union). The charter is a codified catalogue of [fundamental rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_right) against which the EU's legal acts can be judged. It consolidates many rights which were previously recognised by the Court of Justice and derived from the "constitutional traditions common to the member states". [The Court of Justice has long recognised fundamental rights and has, on occasion, invalidated EU legislation based on its failure to adhere to those fundamental rights.](#cite_note-338) [Signing the](#cite_note-europarl-rights-339) [European Convention on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Convention_on_Human_Rights) (ECHR) is a condition for EU membership. [Previously, the EU itself could not accede to the convention as it is neither a state](#cite_note-340) [nor had the competence to accede.](#cite_note-341) [The Lisbon Treaty and Protocol 14 to the ECHR have changed this: the former binds the EU to accede to the convention while the latter formally permits it.](#cite_note-342)

The EU is independent from the Council of Europe, although they share purpose and ideas, especially on the rule of law, human rights and democracy. Furthermore, the [European Convention on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Convention_on_Human_Rights) and [European Social Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Social_Charter), as well as the [source of law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Source_of_law) for the [Charter of Fundamental Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_of_Fundamental_Rights_of_the_European_Union) are created by the Council of Europe. The EU has also promoted human rights issues in the wider world. The EU opposes the death penalty and has proposed its worldwide abolition. Abolition of the death penalty is a condition for EU membership. [On 19 October 2020, the European Union revealed new plans to create a legal structure to act against](#cite_note-343) [human rights violations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_violations) worldwide. The new plan was expected to provide the European Union with greater flexibility to target and sanction those responsible for serious human rights violations and abuses around the world.

## Culture

Cultural co-operation between member states has been an interest of the European Union since its inclusion as a community competency in the Maastricht Treaty. [Actions taken in the cultural area by the EU include the](#cite_note-Bozoki-345) [Culture 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_2000) seven-year programme, [the](#cite_note-Bozoki-345) [European Cultural Month](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Cultural_Month) event, [and orchestras such as the](#cite_note-Culture_Month-346) [European Union Youth Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Youth_Orchestra). [The](#cite_note-347) [European Capital of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Capital_of_Culture) programme selects one or more cities in every year to assist the [cultural development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociocultural_evolution) of that city.

### Sport

Sport is mainly the responsibility of the member states or other international organisations, rather than of the EU. There are some EU policies that have affected sport, such as the free movement of workers, which was at the core of the [Bosman ruling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosman_ruling) that prohibited national football leagues from imposing quotas on foreign players with EU member state citizenship. [The](#cite_note-BBC_Boseman-349) [Treaty of Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lisbon) requires any application of economic rules to take into account the specific nature of sport and its structures based on voluntary activity. [This followed lobbying by governing organisations such as the](#cite_note-350) [International Olympic Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Olympic_Committee) and [FIFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA), due to objections over the application of free market principles to sport, which led to an increasing gap between rich and poor clubs. [The EU does fund a programme for Israeli, Jordanian, Irish, and British football coaches, as part of the Football 4 Peace project.](#cite_note-IHT_RT-351)

### Symbols

The [flag of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Europe) consists of a [circle of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_of_stars) 12 golden stars on a blue background. Originally designed in 1955 for the Council of Europe, the flag was adopted by the [European Communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Communities), the predecessors of the present European Union, in 1986. The Council of Europe gave the flag a symbolic description in the following terms, [although the official symbolic description adopted by the EU omits the reference to the "Western world".](#cite_note-36th-353)

> Against the blue sky of the Western world, the stars symbolise the peoples of Europe in a form of a circle, the sign of union. The number of stars is invariably
>
> [twelve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12_(number)#Religion), the figure twelve being the symbol of perfection and entirety.

—Council of Europe. Paris, 7–9 December 1955.

[United in Diversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motto_of_the_European_Union) was adopted as the motto of the union in 2000, having been selected from [proposals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_motto_for_Europe) submitted by school pupils. [Since 1985, the](#cite_note-FOOTNOTESimons2002110-355) [flag day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_day) of the union has been [Europe Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe_Day), on 9 May (the date of the 1950 [Schuman declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schuman_declaration)). The [anthem of the EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthem_of_Europe) is an instrumental version of the prelude to the [Ode to Joy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ode_to_Joy), the 4th movement of [Ludwig van Beethoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven)'s [ninth symphony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._9_(Beethoven)). The anthem was adopted by European Community leaders in 1985 and has since been played on official occasions.[Besides naming the continent, the](#cite_note-356) [Greek mythological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mythology) figure of [Europa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europa_(consort_of_Zeus)) has frequently been employed as a [personification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_personification) of Europe. Known from the myth in which [Zeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeus) seduces her in the guise of a white bull, Europa has also been referred to in relation to the present union. Statues of Europa and the bull decorate several of the EU's institutions and a portrait of her is seen on the 2013 series of [euro banknotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_banknotes). The bull is, for its part, depicted on all residence permit cards.

[Charles the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne), also known as Charlemagne ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): Carolus Magnus) and later recognised as Pater Europae ("Father of Europe"), [has a symbolic relevance to Europe. The commission has named](#cite_note-360) [one of its central buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne_building) in Brussels after Charlemagne and the city of [Aachen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aachen) has since 1949 awarded the [Charlemagne Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne_Prize) to champions of European unification. [Since 2008, the organisers of this prize, in conjunction with the European Parliament, have awarded the](#cite_note-361) [Charlemagne Youth Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Charlemagne_Youth_Prize) in recognition of similar efforts led by young people.

### Media

[Media freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_freedom) is a [fundamental right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_rights) that applies to all [member states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_state_of_the_European_Union) of the European Union and its [citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU_citizens), as defined in the [EU Charter of Fundamental Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU_Charter_of_Fundamental_Rights) as well as the [European Convention on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Convention_on_Human_Rights).[:1 Within the](#cite_note-eprs-363) [EU enlargement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU_enlargement) process, guaranteeing media freedom is named a "key indicator of a country's readiness to become part of the EU".

The majority of media in the European Union are national-orientated, although some EU-wide media focusing on European affairs have emerged since the early 1990s, such as [Euronews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euronews), [Eurosport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurosport), [EUobserver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EUobserver), [EURACTIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EURACTIV) or [Politico Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politico_Europe).[Arte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arte) is a public Franco-German TV network that promotes programming in the areas of culture and the arts. 80 per cent of its programming are provided in equal proportion by the two member companies, while the remainder is being provided by the [European Economic Interest Grouping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Economic_Interest_Grouping) ARTE GEIE and the channel's European partners. [The](#cite_note-367) [MEDIA Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MEDIA_Programme) of the European Union has supported the European popular film and audiovisual industries since 1991. It provides support for the development, promotion and distribution of European works within Europe and beyond.

### Influence

The European Union has had a significant positive economic effect on most member states. [According to a 2019 study of the member states who joined from 1973 to 2004, "without European integration, per capita incomes would have been, on average, approximately 10% lower in the first ten years after joining the EU".](#cite_note-JoME-369) [Greece was the exception reported by the study, which analysed up to 2008, "to avoid confounding effects from the global financial crisis".](#cite_note-JoME-369) [A 2021 study in the Journal of Political Economy found that the 2004 enlargement had aggregate beneficial economic effects on all groups in both the old and new member states. The largest winners were the new member states, in particular unskilled labour in the new member states.](#cite_note-JoME-369)

The European Union is frequently cited as having made a major contribution to peace in Europe, in particular by pacifying border disputes, [and to the spread of democracy, especially by encouraging democratic reforms in aspiring Eastern European member states after the collapse of the USSR.](#cite_note-372) [Scholar](#cite_note-Risse-2009-374) [Thomas Risse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Risse) wrote in 2009, "there is a consensus in the literature on Eastern Europe that the EU membership perspective had a huge anchoring effects for the new democracies." [However,](#cite_note-Risse-2009-374) [R. Daniel Kelemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._Daniel_Kelemen) argues that the EU has proved beneficial to leaders who are overseeing [democratic backsliding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_backsliding), as the EU is reluctant to intervene in domestic politics, gives authoritarian governments funds which they can use to strengthen their regimes, and because [freedom of movement within the EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_single_market#People) allows dissenting citizens to leave their backsliding countries. At the same time, the union might provide through [Article 7 of the Treaty on European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_7_of_the_Treaty_on_European_Union) an external constraint that prevents [electoral autocracies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_autocracy), currently Hungary, [from progressing into](#cite_note-375) [closed autocracies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autocracy).

## Opt-outs

Three member states have permanent opt-outs from certain aspects of the union:

- Denmark has an opt-out from the [eurozone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurozone)and the[area of freedom, security and justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_of_freedom,_security_and_justice)(AFSJ);
- Ireland has an opt-out of the [Schengen area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schengen_area), due to its[open border with the UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Travel_Area), and a partial opt-out from the AFSJ
- Poland has a partial opt-out of the [Charter of Fundamental Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_of_Fundamental_Rights).
