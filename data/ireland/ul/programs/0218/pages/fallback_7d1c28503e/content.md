The Masters Tournament (usually referred to as simply the Masters, or as the U.S. Masters outside North America) [is one of the four](#cite_note-golfdigest-openname-4) [men's major championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men's_major_golf_championships) in [professional golf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_golf_tours). Scheduled for the first full week in April, the Masters is the first major golf tournament of the year. Unlike the other major tournaments which change host venues with each tournament, the Masters is always held at the same host venue: [Augusta National Golf Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusta_National_Golf_Club), a private course in the city of [Augusta, Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusta,_Georgia).

[Amateur golf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur_sports#Golf) champion [Bobby Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Jones_(golfer)) and [investment banker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment_banking) [Clifford Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_Roberts) founded the tournament. [After his](#cite_note-Hist-5) [grand slam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Slam_(golf)) in 1930, Jones acquired a former [plant nursery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_nursery) and co-designed Augusta National with course architect [Alister MacKenzie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alister_MacKenzie). [First played in](#cite_note-itcpgat11-1) [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_Masters_Tournament) as the "Augusta National Invitation Tournament", [the Masters is an official money event](#cite_note-7) [on the](#cite_note-8) [PGA Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PGA_Tour), the [European Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Tour), and the [Japan Golf Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Golf_Tour). The field of players is smaller than those of the other major championships because it is an invitational event, held by the Augusta National Golf Club.

The tournament has a number of traditions. Since the [1949 Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Masters_Tournament), a green jacket (specifically [Pantone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantone) 342C, "Augusta Green") [has been awarded to the champion, who must return it to the clubhouse one year after his victory, although it remains his personal property and is stored with other champions' jackets in a specially designated cloakroom. In most instances, only a first-time and reigning champion may remove his jacket from the club grounds. A golfer who wins the event multiple times uses the same green jacket awarded upon his initial win unless he needs to be re-fitted with a new jacket.](#cite_note-9) [The Champions Dinner, inaugurated by](#cite_note-10) [Ben Hogan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Hogan) at the [1952 Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1952_Masters_Tournament), is held on the Tuesday before each Masters and is open only to past champions and certain board members of the Augusta National Golf Club. Beginning in 1963, distinguished golfers, usually past champions, have hit an honorary tee shot on the morning of the first round to commence play. These have included [Fred McLeod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_McLeod_(golfer)), [Jock Hutchinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jock_Hutchinson), [Gene Sarazen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_Sarazen), [Sam Snead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Snead), [Byron Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byron_Nelson), [Arnold Palmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Palmer), [Jack Nicklaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Nicklaus), [Gary Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Player), [Lee Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Elder), and [Tom Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Watson_(golfer)). Since 1960, a [semi-social contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masters_Tournament_Par_3_Contest) on the Par3 course has been played on Wednesday, the day before the first round.

Nicklaus has the most Masters wins, with six between 1963 and 1986. [Tiger Woods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger_Woods) won five between 1997 and 2019. Palmer won four between 1958 and 1964. Five have won three titles at Augusta: [Jimmy Demaret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Demaret), [Sam Snead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Snead), [Gary Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Player), [Nick Faldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Faldo), and [Phil Mickelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Mickelson). Gary Player, from [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa), was the first non-American player to win the tournament, in [1961](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1961_Masters_Tournament); the second was [Seve Ballesteros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seve_Ballesteros) of [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain), the champion in [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Masters_Tournament) and [1983](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Masters_Tournament).

The Augusta National course first opened in 1933 and has been modified many times by different architects. Among the changes: greens have been reshaped and, on occasion, entirely re-designed, bunkers have been added, water hazards have been extended, new tee boxes have been built, hundreds of trees have been planted, and several mounds have been installed.

The idea for Augusta National originated with [Bobby Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Jones_(golfer)), who wanted to build a golf course after his retirement from the game. He sought advice from Clifford Roberts, who later became the chairman of the club. They came across a piece of land in [Augusta, Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusta,_Georgia), of which Jones said: "Perfect! And to think this ground has been lying here all these years waiting for someone to come along and lay a golf course upon it."[The land had been an](#cite_note-Jonesquote-12) [indigo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigofera) plantation in the early nineteenth century and a [plant nursery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_nursery) since 1857. [Jones hired](#cite_note-nursbeaut-13) [Alister MacKenzie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alister_MacKenzie) to help design the course, and work began in 1931. The course formally opened in 1933, but MacKenzie died before the first Masters Tournament was played.

After [Clifford Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_Roberts) invited then-USGA tournament committee chairman [Prescott Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prescott_Bush) to ANGC in early 1933, Jones and Roberts strenuously petitioned the [USGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USGA) to hold the [U.S.Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Open_(golf)) at Augusta in 1934, but the USGA denied the petition, noting that the hot Georgia summers would create difficult playing conditions. [Indeed, the professional golf schedule would have needed to change, and to date no golf major had been held in the south. The club was deeply disappointed and had been seeking publicity as it was struggling financially and in debt, but Roberts took the rejection as an opportunity to found his own annual tournament.](#cite_note-15)

Roberts told the PGA of his plan for a new tournament in late 1933. The official PGA schedule, in a small endnote, mentioned Augusta's tournament alongside three others: the 1934 [Tournament of the Gardens Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tournament_of_the_Gardens_Open), the 1934 [North and South Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_and_South_Open), and a tournament in [Columbus, Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus,_Georgia). [The fact that the new tournament would bring Jones out of retirement made the idea attractive to both Augusta and the PGA, which itself was facing hard times financially;](#cite_note-Owen_1999-11) [Grantland Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grantland_Rice) announced the news in a syndicated column.

Due to Augusta National's financial hardships, the tournament was set to feature a small contingent of players. Initially the Augusta National Invitation field was composed largely of Bobby Jones' close associates. Due to the early calendar date of the Masters, it was not uncommon for professional golfers to be unable to attend because of jobs and prior commitments.

The first "Augusta National Invitation Tournament", as the Masters was originally known, began on March22, 1934, and was won by [Horton Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horton_Smith), who took the first prize of $1,500. (The club could not afford to pay the winnings, so 17Augusta National members personally chipped in instead.[) Roberts had borrowed chairs for spectators to use from two local funeral homes, and the printed program included advertisements given for free to the club's creditors. Tournament passes cost $2.00.](#cite_note-Owen_1999-11) [The tournament was played with current holes 10 through 18 played as the first nine, and 1 through 9 as the second nine,](#cite_note-golf-com-prices-26-16) [then reversed permanently to its present layout for the 1935 tournament.](#cite_note-17) [After the first tournament, around 20 new members joined ANGC, giving the club a much-needed financial lifeline.](#cite_note-Hist-5)

The present name, Masters Tournament, was officially adopted in 1939, but had been in use since the first tournament (newspapers sometimes called it the "Masters Open").

[Gene Sarazen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_Sarazen) hit the "shot heard 'round the world" in 1935, holing a shot from the [fairway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairway_(golf)) on the [par](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Par_(score)) 5 15th for a [double eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_eagle_(golf)) (albatross). [This tied Sarazen with](#cite_note-18) [Craig Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Wood_(golfer)), and in the ensuing 36-hole playoff, Sarazen was the victor by five strokes. [Roberts would later contend that Sarazen's shot had "put the Masters in business", but attendance fell in 1935 and 1936. Throughout the rest of the 1930s, the numbers of players who accepted tournament invitations fell despite an increase in sent invitations, falling to a nadir of 42 golfers at the 1938 Masters. The club was in an inconvenient location and the effects of the Depression were still lingering.](#cite_note-past-results-19)

The gallery for Sunday's final two rounds at the [1939Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1939_Masters_Tournament) was estimated at 10,000spectators.

In 1940, Roberts stated that the Masters was one of the top tournaments in the United States, if not the biggest. He stated, "I am told that the Masters has outdistanced in attendance both the [U.S. Amateur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Amateur) and the [PGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PGA_Championship)." [The tournament was not played from 1943 to 1945, due to](#cite_note-Maco400317-21) [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II). To assist the war effort, cattle and turkeys were raised on the Augusta National grounds.

After the war, ticket sales were still a struggle: club member Jerry Franklin would plead with Augusta Chronicle owner William MorrisJr. to buy a group of tickets.

[Byron Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byron_Nelson) won the first of two Masters titles in 1937. [Jimmy Demaret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Demaret) won three times as did [Sam Snead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Snead) in the 1940s and 1950s. [Ben Hogan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Hogan) won the 1951 and 1953 Masters and was runner-up on four occasions.

The club raised ticket prices to $10 for the [1951tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1951_Masters_Tournament); spectators were not pleased.

Through the 50s and into the 60s, the Masters was still not widely attended. Locals were able to buy tickets at the gate on the day of each round. The city held a "Masters hospitality week", featuring a parade, from 1957 through 1967.

In 1967, series badge prices rose to $15; by 1975, they were $30.

The "Big Three" of Arnold Palmer, Gary Player, and Jack Nicklaus dominated the Masters from 1960 through 1978, winning the event 11 times between them during that span. After winning by one stroke in 1958, [Palmer won by one stroke again in 1960 in memorable circumstances. Trailing](#cite_note-past-results-19) [Ken Venturi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Venturi) by one shot in the 1960 event, Palmer made birdies on the last two holes to prevail. Palmer would go on to win another two Masters in 1962 and 1964. [Palmer's caddie at Augusta each time was](#cite_note-past-results-19) [Nathaniel "Iron Man" Avery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathaniel_"Iron_Man"_Avery).

Nicklaus emerged in the early 1960s and served as a rival to the popular Palmer. Nicklaus won his first green jacket in 1963, defeating [Tony Lema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Lema) by one stroke. [Two years later, he shot a then-course record of 271 (17 under par) for his second Masters win, leading Bobby Jones to say that Nicklaus played "a game with which I am not familiar."](#cite_note-24) [The next year, Nicklaus won his third green jacket in a grueling 18-hole playoff against](#cite_note-25) [Tommy Jacobs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Jacobs) and [Gay Brewer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay_Brewer). [This made Nicklaus the first player to win consecutive Masters. He won again in 1972 by three strokes.](#cite_note-nick1966-26) [In 1975, Nicklaus won by one stroke in a close contest with](#cite_note-past-results-19) [Tom Weiskopf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Weiskopf) and [Johnny Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Miller) in one of the most exciting Masters to date.

Player became the first non-American to win the Masters in [1961](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1961_Masters_Tournament), beating Palmer, the defending champion, by one stroke when Palmer double-bogeyed the final hole. [In 1974, he won again by two strokes.](#cite_note-past-results-19) [After not winning a tournament on the U.S. PGA tour for nearly four years, and at the age of 42, Player won his third and final Masters in 1978 by one stroke over three players.](#cite_note-past-results-19) [Player is second in consecutive cuts made with 23 straight (tied with](#cite_note-past-results-19) [Fred Couples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Couples)), and has played in a record 52 Masters.

A controversial ending to the Masters occurred in 1968. Argentine champion [Roberto De Vicenzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_De_Vicenzo) signed his scorecard (attested by playing partner [Tommy Aaron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Aaron)) incorrectly recording him as making a par 4 instead of a birdie 3 on the 17th hole of the final round. According to the rules of golf, if a player signs a scorecard (thereby attesting to its veracity) that records a score on a hole higher than what he actually made on the hole, the player receives the higher score for that hole. This extra stroke cost De Vicenzo a chance to be in an 18-hole Monday playoff with [Bob Goalby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Goalby), who won the green jacket. De Vicenzo's mistake led to the famous quote, "What a stupid I am."

In [1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Masters_Tournament), [Lee Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Elder) became the first [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American) to play in the Masters, [doing so 15 years before Augusta National admitted its first black member, Ron Townsend, as a result of the](#cite_note-31) [Shoal Creek Controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoal_Creek_Club#Membership).

Non-Americans collected 11 victories in 20years in the 1980s and 1990s, by far the strongest run they have had in any of the three majors played in the United States since the early days of the U.S. Open. The first European to win the Masters was [Seve Ballesteros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seve_Ballesteros) in 1980; badges at that point were $48. [Nicklaus became the oldest player to win the Masters in 1986 when he won for the sixth time at age 46.](#cite_note-golf-michaux-21-22)

During this period, no golfer suffered more disappointment at the Masters than [Greg Norman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Norman). In his first appearance at Augusta in 1981, he led during the second nine but ended up finishing fourth. In 1986, after birdieing holes 14 through 17 to tie Nicklaus for the lead, he badly pushed his 4-iron approach on 18 into the patrons surrounding the green and missed his par putt for a closing bogey. In 1987, Norman lost a sudden-death playoff when [Larry Mize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Mize) holed out a remarkable 45-yard pitch shot to birdie the second playoff hole. Mize thus became the first Augusta native to win the Masters. [In 1996, Norman tied the course record with an opening-round 63 and had a six-stroke lead over](#cite_note-34) [Nick Faldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Faldo) entering the final round. However, he stumbled to a closing 78 while Faldo, his playing partner that day, carded a 67 to win by five shots for his third Masters championship. [Norman also led the 1999 Masters on the second nine of the final round, only to falter again and finish third behind winner](#cite_note-35) [José María Olazábal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/José_María_Olazábal), who won his second green jacket. Norman finished in the top five at the Masters eight times, but never won.

In 1993, the price of a four-day badge was raised to $100.

Two-time champion [Ben Crenshaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Crenshaw) captured an emotional Masters win in 1995, just days after the death of his lifelong teacher and mentor [Harvey Penick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Penick). After making his final putt to win, he broke down sobbing at the hole and was consoled and embraced by his caddie Carl Jackson. In the post-tournament interview, Crenshaw said: "I had a 15th club in my bag," a reference to Penick. (The "15th club" reference is based on the golf rule that limits a player to carrying 14 clubs during a round.) Crenshaw first won at Augusta in 1984.

In 1997, 21-year-old Tiger Woods became the youngest champion in Masters history, winning by 12 shots with an 18-under par 270 which broke the 72-hole record that had stood for 32 years. [In 2001, Woods completed his "Tiger Slam" by winning his fourth straight major championship at the Masters by two shots over](#cite_note-Hist-5) [David Duval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Duval). [He won again the following year, making him only the third player in history (after Nicklaus and Faldo) to win the tournament in consecutive years,](#cite_note-past-results-19) [as well as in 2005 when he defeated](#cite_note-past-results-19) [Chris DiMarco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_DiMarco) in a playoff for his first major championship win in almost three years.

The cost of a four-day badge rose to $125 for the [2001 tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Masters_Tournament).

In 2003, the Augusta National Golf Club was targeted by [Martha Burk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_Burk), who organized a failed protest at that year's Masters to pressure the club into accepting female members. Burk planned to protest at the front gates of Augusta National during the third day of the tournament, but her application for a permit to do so was denied. [A court appeal was dismissed.](#cite_note-37) [In 2004, Burk stated that she had no further plans to protest against the club.](#cite_note-38) [The club admitted its first two women members,](#cite_note-39) [Condoleezza Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condoleezza_Rice) and [Darla Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darla_Moore), in 2012.

Augusta National chairman [Billy Payne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Payne) himself made headlines in April 2010 when he commented at the annual pre-Masters press conference on Tiger Woods' [off-the-course behavior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger_Woods#Infidelity_scandal_and_fallout). "It's not simply the degree of his conduct that is so egregious here," Payne said, in his opening speech. "It is the fact he disappointed all of us and more importantly our kids and grandkids."

In 2003, [Mike Weir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Weir) became the first Canadian to win a [men's major championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men's_major_golf_championships) and the first left-hander to win the Masters when he defeated [Len Mattiace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Len_Mattiace) in a playoff. [The following year another left-hander,](#cite_note-past-results-19) [Phil Mickelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Mickelson), won his first major championship by making a birdie on the final hole to beat [Ernie Els](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernie_Els) by a stroke. [Mickelson also won the tournament in 2006 and 2010. In 2011, unheralded South African](#cite_note-past-results-19) [Charl Schwartzel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charl_Schwartzel) birdied the final four holes to win by two strokes. In 2012, [Bubba Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubba_Watson) won the tournament on the second playoff hole over [Louis Oosthuizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Oosthuizen). In 2013 [Adam Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Scott_(golfer)) won the Masters in a playoff over 2009 champion [Ángel Cabrera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ángel_Cabrera), making him the first Australian to win the tournament. [Watson won the 2014 Masters by three strokes over](#cite_note-43) [Jordan Spieth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Spieth) and [Jonas Blixt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonas_Blixt), his second Masters title in three years and the sixth for a left-hander in 12 years. In 2015, Spieth would become the second-youngest winner (behind Woods) in just his second Masters, equaling Woods' 72-hole scoring record. [In 2017,](#cite_note-44) [Sergio García](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_García) beat [Justin Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Rose) in a playoff for his long-awaited first major title. In 2019, Tiger Woods captured his fifth Masters, his first win at Augusta National in 14 years and his first major title since 2008.

In 2009, the price for a four-day badge rose to $200; it hit $250 in 2012, and $325 in 2015.

The [2020 Masters Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Masters_Tournament), originally scheduled to be played April 9–12, was postponed until November due to the [coronavirus outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic). [Dustin Johnson won the tournament by five strokes.](#cite_note-45)

In 2025, a monument in [Augusta, Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusta,_Georgia) was erected by artist [Baruti Tucker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baruti_Tucker?action=edit&redlink=1) to honor the black caddies at [Augusta National Golf Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusta_National_Golf_Club) for the Masters Tournament.[Rory McIlroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rory_McIlroy) won the 2025 Masters and completed the sixth [grand slam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Slam_(golf)) in a playoff over [Justin Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Rose). [The](#cite_note-47) [next year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Masters_Tournament), McIlroy won again, becoming the fourth player to go back-to-back at the Masters.

The price for a four-day badge to the Masters rose to $450 in 2023, and reached $525 for 2026.

The [Augusta National Women's Amateur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusta_National_Women's_Amateur) (ANWA) is held the week before the Masters. The ANWA was established in 2019 by Augusta National chairman [Fred Ridley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Ridley). The winner of the Augusta National Women's Amateur is exempt from two women's golf majors.

In 2013, Augusta National partnered with the [USGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USGA) and the [PGA of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PGA_of_America) to establish "Drive, Chip and Putt" (DCP), a youth golf skills competition which was first held in 2014. The event was established as part of an effort to help promote the sport of golf among youth; the winners of local qualifiers in different age groups advance to the national finals, which have been held at Augusta National on the Sunday immediately preceding the Masters. The driving and chipping portions of the event are held on the course's practice range, and the putting portion has been played on the 18th hole.

Practice rounds are held on the Monday, Tuesday, and Wednesday before the tournament begins on Thursday. [During the practice rounds, Redbud (hole16) is well-known for spectators shouting "Skipit!" at players, encouraging the golfers to attempt to](#cite_note-melton-practice-25-53) [skip the ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_skipping) across the pond.

The three days of practice are the only times during which attendees are allowed to bring cameras onto the course.

First established in 1948, the Amateur Dinner is held on the Monday before the tournament, on the same day practice rounds begin. [The amateurs competing in the Masters are invited.](#cite_note-ghs-ad-davis-57)

The Champions' Dinner is held each year on the Tuesday evening preceding Thursday's first round. The dinner was first held in 1952, hosted by defending champion Ben Hogan, to honor the past champions of the tournament. [At that time 15 tournaments had been played, and the number of past champions was 11. Officially known as the "Masters Club", it includes only past winners of the Masters, although selected members of the Augusta National Golf Club have been included as honorary members, usually the chairman.](#cite_note-59)

The defending champion, as host, selects the menu for the dinner. Frequently, Masters champions have served cuisine from their home regions prepared by the Masters chef. Notable examples have included [haggis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haggis), served by [Scotsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland) [Sandy Lyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandy_Lyle) in 1989, [and](#cite_note-60) [bobotie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobotie), a South African dish, served at the behest of 2008 champion [Trevor Immelman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trevor_Immelman). Other examples include German [Bernhard Langer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernhard_Langer)'s 1986 [Wiener schnitzel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiener_schnitzel), Britain's [Nick Faldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Faldo)'s [fish and chips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_and_chips), Canadian [Mike Weir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Weir)'s [elk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elk) and [wild boar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_boar), and [Vijay Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijay_Singh)'s [tom kha thale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_kha_kai) and chicken [phanaeng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phanaeng). The 2011 dinner of [Phil Mickelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Mickelson) was a Spanish-themed menu in hopes that [Seve Ballesteros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seve_Ballesteros) would attend, but he was too sick and died weeks later.

In 1998, Tiger Woods served [cheeseburgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheeseburger), [chicken sandwiches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken_sandwich), [french fries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_fries) and [milkshakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milkshake). Woods was the youngest winner, and when asked about his food choices, he responded with "They said you could pick anything you want... Hey, it's part of being young, that's what I eat."[Fuzzy Zoeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzzy_Zoeller), the 1979 champion, created a media storm when he suggested that Woods refrain from serving [collard greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collard_greens) and [fried chicken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fried_chicken), dishes commonly associated with [African-American culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African-American_culture).

The [Par3 Contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masters_Tournament_Par_3_Contest) was first introduced in 1960, and was won that year by Snead. Since then it has traditionally been played on the Wednesday before the tournament starts. The Par3 Course was built in 1958. It is a nine-hole course, with a par of27, and measures 1,060 yards (970m) in length.

There have been 94holes-in-one in the history of the contest, with a record nine occurring in 2016, during which [Rickie Fowler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rickie_Fowler) and [Justin Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Thomas) scored back-to-back holes in one on the 4thhole, while playing in a group with reigning champion [Jordan Spieth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Spieth).[Camilo Villegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camilo_Villegas) became the first player to card two holes-in-one in the same round during the 2015 Par3 Contest. This achievement was duplicated by [Séamus Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Séamus_Power_(golfer)), who scored back-to-back holes in one on holes8 and 9 during the 2023 Par3 Contest. [As of 2026](#cite_note-67)[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Masters_Tournament&action=edit), no Par3 Contest winner has also won the Masters in the same year. [There have been several repeat winners, including](#cite_note-69) [Pádraig Harrington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pádraig_Harrington), Sandy Lyle, Sam Snead, and Tom Watson.[[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)]

In this event, golfers may use their children as caddies, which helps to create a family-friendly atmosphere. In 2008, the event was televised for the first time by [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN).

The winner of the Par3 Contest, which is played the day before the tournament begins, wins a crystal bowl.

Since 1963, the custom in most years has been to start the tournament with an honorary opening tee shot at the first hole, [typically by one or more older players. For a number of years before 1963,](#cite_note-71) [Jock Hutchison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jock_Hutchison) and [Fred McLeod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_McLeod_(golfer)) had been the first pair to tee off, both being able to play as past major championship winners. In 1963 the eligibility rules were changed and they were no longer able to compete. The idea of honorary starters was introduced with Hutchison and McLeod being the first two. This twosome led off every tournament from 1963 until 1973 when poor health prevented Hutchison from swinging a club. McLeod continued on until his death in 1976. Byron Nelson and Gene Sarazen started in 1981 and were then joined by Sam Snead in 1984. This trio continued until 1999 when Sarazen died, while Nelson stopped in 2001. Snead hit his final opening tee shot in 2002, a little over a month before he died.

In 2007, Arnold Palmer took over as the honorary starter. Palmer also had the honor in 2008 and 2009. [At the](#cite_note-72) [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Masters_Tournament) and [2011 Masters Tournaments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Masters_Tournament), [Jack Nicklaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Nicklaus) joined Palmer as an honorary starter for the event. [In 2012,](#cite_note-73) [Gary Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Player) joined them. Palmer announced in March 2016 that a lingering shoulder issue would prevent him from partaking in the 2016 tee shot. [Palmer was still in attendance for the ceremony.](#cite_note-74)

Following Palmer's death in 2016, the [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Masters_Tournament) ceremony featured tributes; his green jacket was draped over an empty white chair, while everyone in attendance wore "Arnie's Army" badges.

In 2021 [Lee Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Elder) joined Nicklaus and Player as an honorary starter. He was invited to join them as he was the first African-American to take part in the Masters in [1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Masters_Tournament). Despite bad health preventing Elder from hitting a shot, he was still present and received a standing ovation from the crowd.

Two-time Masters champion [Tom Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Watson_(golfer)) joined Nicklaus and Player, starting in 2022.

Until [1983](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Masters_Tournament), all players in the Masters were required to use the services of an Augusta National Club [caddie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caddie), [who by club tradition was always an African-American man.](#cite_note-mcadcol-81) [Club co-founder](#cite_note-nyt1990-32) [Clifford Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_Roberts) is reputed to have said, "As long as I'm alive, golfers will be white, and caddies will be black." [Since 1983—six years after Roberts's death in 1977—players have been allowed the option of bringing their own caddie to the tournament.](#cite_note-82)

The Masters requires caddies to wear a uniform consisting of a white jumpsuit, a green Masters cap, and white tennis shoes. The surname, and sometimes first initial, of each player is found on the back of his caddie's uniform. The defending champion always receives caddie number "1": other golfers get their caddie numbers from the order in which they register for the tournament. The other majors and some PGA Tour events formerly had a similar policy concerning caddies well into the 1970s; [the U.S. Open first allowed players to use their own caddies in](#cite_note-tgprfocd-85) [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_U.S._Open_(golf)).

The total prize money for the [2025 Masters Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Masters_Tournament) was $21,000,000, with $4,200,000 going to the winner. In the inaugural year of 1934, the winner [Horton Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horton_Smith) received $1,500 out of a $5,000 purse. [After Nicklaus's first win in 1963, he received $20,000, while after his final victory in 1986 he won $144,000.](#cite_note-Purse-88) [In recent years the purse has grown quickly. Between 2001 and 2014, the winner's share grew by $612,000, and the purse grew by $3,400,000.](#cite_note-90)

In addition to a cash prize, the winner of the tournament is presented with a distinctive green jacket, formally awarded since 1949 and informally awarded to the champions from the years prior. The green sport coat is the official attire worn by members of Augusta National while on the club grounds; each Masters winner becomes an honorary member of the club. The recipient of the green jacket has it presented to him inside the Butler Cabin soon after the end of the tournament in a televised ceremony, and the presentation is then repeated outside near the 18th green in front of the patrons. Winners keep their jacket for the year after their victory, then return it to the club to wear whenever they are present on the club grounds. [Sam Snead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Snead) was the first Masters champion to be awarded the green jacket after he took his first Masters title in 1949.

The green jacket is only allowed to be removed from the Augusta National grounds by the reigning champion, after which it must remain at the club. Exceptions to this rule include Gary Player, who in his joy of winning mistakenly took his jacket home to South Africa after his 1961 victory;[Seve Ballesteros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seve_Ballesteros) who, in an interview with [Peter Alliss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Alliss) from his home in Pedreña, showed one of his two green jackets in his trophy room;[[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)] and [Henry Picard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Picard), whose jacket was removed from the club before the tradition was well established, remained in his closet for a number of years, and is now on display at Canterbury Golf Club in [Beachwood, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beachwood,_Ohio), where he was the club professional for many years.

By tradition, the winner of the previous year's Masters Tournament puts the jacket on the winner at the end of the tournament. In [1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_Masters_Tournament), Jack Nicklaus became the first player to win in consecutive years and he donned the jacket himself. [When](#cite_note-nick1966-26) [Nick Faldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Faldo) (in 1990), Tiger Woods (in 2002), and [Rory McIlroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rory_McIlroy) (in 2026) won in consecutive years, the chairman of Augusta National put the jacket on them.

In addition to the green jacket, winners of the tournament receive a gold medal. In 2017, a green jacket that was found at a thrift store in 1994 was sold at auction for $139,000.

Winners also have their names engraved on the actual silver Masters trophy. The runner-up receives a silver medal, introduced in 1951. Beginning in 1978, a silver [salver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salver) was added as an award for the runner-up.

In 1952, the Masters began presenting an award, known as the Silver Cup, to the lowest scoring amateur to make the cut. In 1954, they began presenting an amateur silver medal to the low amateur runner-up.

The original trophy weighs over 130 pounds (59kg) and sits on a 4-foot-wide (1.2m) base. It resides permanently at Augusta National and depicts the clubhouse. Winners instead receive a replica, which is significantly smaller, stands just 6.5 inches (17cm) tall, and weighs 20 pounds (9.1kg), which they get to keep. The champion and the runner-up both have their names engraved on the permanent trophy.

There are several awards presented to players who perform exceptional feats during the tournament. The player who has the daily lowest score receives a crystal vase, while players who score a [hole-in-one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hole-in-one) or a [double eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_eagle_(golf)) win a large crystal bowl. [For each](#cite_note-98) [eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle_(golf)) a player makes, they receive a pair of crystal goblets.

The Double Eagle trophy was introduced in 1967 when [Bruce Devlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Devlin) holed out for double eagle on number8. He was only the second to do so, and the first in 32years, following [Gene Sarazen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_Sarazen) on hole15 in 1935. The trophy is a large crystal bowl with "Masters Tournament" engraved around the top.

As with the other majors, winning the Masters gives a golfer several privileges which make his career more secure. Masters champions are automatically invited to play in the other three majors (the [U.S. Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Open_(golf)), [The Open Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Open_Championship), and the [PGA Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PGA_Championship)) for the next five years (except for amateur winners, unless they turn pro within the five-year period), and earn a lifetime invitation to the Masters. They also receive membership on the PGA Tour for the following five seasons and invitations to [The Players Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Players_Championship) for five years.

Because the tournament was established by an amateur champion, [Bobby Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Jones_(golfer)), the Masters has a tradition of honoring [amateur golf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur_golf). It invites winners of the most prestigious amateur tournaments in the world. Also, the current [U.S. Amateur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Amateur) champion always plays in the same group as the defending Masters champion for the first two days of the tournament.

Amateurs in the field are welcome to stay in the "Crow's Nest" atop the Augusta National clubhouse during the tournament. The Crow's Nest is 1,200 square feet (110m2) with lodging space for five during the competition.

While the tournament now has a wide range of qualifying criteria for international golfers, until 1987 the only methods of qualification for non-Americans were via "special international invitation", or by winning the tournament in a previous year.

Merchandise sold at the tournament has become a large business for Augusta National, bringing in an estimated $70million in revenue. All items are only available in-person at the Masters, making the merchandise into a status symbol and creating a secondhand market.

Although tickets (referred to as "badges") for the Masters are not expensive at face value compared with other high-level sporting events (as of 2026[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Masters_Tournament&action=edit), $525 for a four-day pass[), they are very difficult to come by. Masters tickets are considered the second-hardest to obtain in sports, trailing only the](#cite_note-masters-prices-gw-26-36) [Super Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl). [Even the practice rounds can be difficult to gain entrance into. Practice rounds and daily tournament passes are sold in advance, through a selection process, only after receipt of an online application. All tickets are sold in advance and there are none sold at the gates.](#cite_note-129) [Additionally, Georgia state law prohibits tickets from being bought, sold, or handed off within a 2,700-foot (820m) boundary around the club.](#cite_note-130)

Since the first Masters, badges have displayed the ticket price, tournament dates, and ANGC logo. They were made from cardboard until 1953, when they switched to paper; all badges have been made of plastic since 1961. [In 2017, the club began including a color bar encoding the ticket number printed on the front.](#cite_note-badges-kelly-23-133) [Badges each contain an](#cite_note-badges-kelly-23-133) [RFID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RFID) chip.

Open applications for practice rounds and individual daily tournament tickets have to be made nearly a year in advance and the successful applicants are chosen by random selection. Series badges for the actual tournament, that is a badge valid for all four tournament rounds, are made available and sold only to individuals of a patrons list, which is closed. A waiting list for the patrons list was opened in 1972 and closed in 1978. It was reopened in 2000 and subsequently closed once again. [Individuals who are on the patron list are given the recurring opportunity to purchase series badges each year for life. According to Augusta National, after the death of a badge holder, a series badge account is transferable only to a surviving spouse and no other family members.](#cite_note-137)

In 2008, as part of their Junior Pass Program, the Masters also began allowing children (between the ages of 8 and 16) to enter on tournament days for free if they are accompanied by the patron who is the original applicant of his or her series badge. The Junior Pass Program does not apply to individual daily tournament tickets, only to series badge patrons.

The difficulty in acquiring Masters badges has made the tournament one of the largest events on the secondary resale ticket market. [Since a majority of the badges for the Masters are made available to the same group of patrons each year, these perennial ticket holders sometimes decide to sell their badges through large ticket marketplaces or third party ticket brokers, although they do so at their own detriment as this action is strictly prohibited in the ticket purchase agreement and ticket policy; those caught are banned from future tournaments.](#cite_note-141) [Since 2025, ANGC has more strictly enforced its no-resale policy.](#cite_note-142)

Augusta National has for decades made 100 series badges available to [Fort Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Gordon), which holds a lottery for service members to buy tickets for a day.

> "I just don't think [cell phones are] appropriate. The noise is an irritation to not only the players, the dialing, the conversation; it's a distraction. And that's the way we have chosen to deal with it."

Patrons who have obtained a ticket to access Augusta National must comply with a strict policy regarding the use of electronic devices during the tournament. The organization clearly states that "the use of any device for phone calls, emails, text messaging, or to record and/or transmit voice, video or data is strictly prohibited," and that cameras are "strictly prohibited on Tournament days" but "are permitted for still photography and personal use only" on practice days, at the start of the competition week. Noise and music-producing devices (radios, TVs, speakers), flags, posters, and weapons are also forbidden. Breaking these rules may lead to ejection from the venue and permanent loss of tickets. [Complimentary phones are available throughout the course for spectators needing to make a call. These policies were established to reduce distractions and encourage patrons to carpe diem, ensuring smooth play and calm on the course while upholding the tournament's etiquette, atmosphere, and tradition.](#cite_note-146)

Augusta National holds a lottery for members of the press pool to play the course the Monday after the end of the Masters; the event is called the Monday Golf Outing.

The Masters is the first [major championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men's_major_golf_championships) of the year. Since [1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Masters_Tournament), its final round has been scheduled for the second Sunday of April, with several exceptions. It ended on the first Sunday four times ([1952](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1952_Masters_Tournament), [1957](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1957_Masters_Tournament), [1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_Masters_Tournament), [1959](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1959_Masters_Tournament)) and the [1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Masters_Tournament) and [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Masters_Tournament) tournaments ended on April15, the month's third Sunday. [The first edition in](#cite_note-Hist-5) [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_Masters_Tournament) was held in late March and the next ten were in early April, with only the [1942](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1942_Masters_Tournament) event scheduled to end on the second Sunday. The [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Masters_Tournament) event, postponed by the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic), was held from November12 to 15, thus being the last major of the year.

Similar to the other majors, the tournament consists of four rounds at 18holes each, Thursday through Sunday (when there are no delays). The Masters has a relatively small field of contenders when compared with other golf tournaments, so the competitors play in groups of three for the first two rounds (36holes) and the field is not split to start on the 1stand 10thtees unless weather shortens the available playing time. The tournament is unique in that it is the only major tournament conducted by a private club rather than a national golf organization like the PGA.

Originally, the Masters was the only tournament to use two-man pairings during the first two rounds. It was also the only event to re-pair based on the leaderboard before Friday's round, as most tournaments only do this on the weekend. This practice ended in the early 2000s when the Masters switched to the more standard three-man groups and the groups are now kept intact on Friday, with players sharing the same playing partners in both of the first two rounds.[[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)]

After 36holes of play, a cut-off score is calculated to reduce the size of the field for the weekend rounds. In 2020, to "make the cut", players must be in the top50 places (ties counting). [Before](#cite_note-151) [1957](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1957_Masters_Tournament), there was no 36-hole cut and all of the invitees played four rounds, if desired. From 1957 to 1961, the top40 scores (including ties) made the cut. From 1962 to 2012, it was the top44 (and ties) or within 10strokes of the lead. [From 2013 to 2019, it was the top50 (and ties) or within 10strokes of the lead.](#cite_note-Cut-29)

Following the cut, an additional 36holes are played over the final two days. Should the fourth round fail to produce a winner, all players tied for the lead enter a [sudden-death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudden_death_(sport)#Golf) playoff. Play begins on the 18thhole, followed by the adjacent10th, repeating until one player wins. Adopted in 1976, the sudden-death playoff was originally formatted to start on the first hole, [but was not needed for the first three years. It was changed for](#cite_note-shtsd76-153) [1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Masters_Tournament) to the inward (final) nine holes, starting at the tenth tee, where the television coverage began. [First employed that same year, the Masters' first sudden-death playoff, won by](#cite_note-mposttn-154) [Fuzzy Zoeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzzy_Zoeller), ended on the 11thgreen. The current arrangement, beginning at the 18thtee, was amended for 2004 and first used the following year. Through 2017, the eleven sudden-death playoffs have yet to advance past the second extra hole. The Masters is the only major tournament that currently uses a sudden-death playoff, which is used on most professional tour tournaments. [Earlier playoffs were 18holes on the following day, except for the first in](#cite_note-155) [1935](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1935_Masters_Tournament), which was 36holes ([Gene Sarazen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_Sarazen) defeated [Craig Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Wood_(golfer))); the last 18-hole playoff was in [1970](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_Masters_Tournament) when [Billy Casper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Casper) defeated [Gene Littler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_Littler), and none of the full-round playoffs went to additional holes.

Lengths of the course for the Masters at the start of each decade:

As with many other courses, Augusta National's championship setup was lengthened in recent years. In [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Masters_Tournament), the course measured 6,925 yards (6,332m) and was extended to 7,270 yards (6,648m) for 2002, and again in [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Masters_Tournament) to 7,445 yards (6,808m); 520 yards (475m) longer than the 2001 course. [The changes attracted many critics, including the most successful players in Masters history, Jack Nicklaus, Arnold Palmer, Gary Player and Tiger Woods. Woods claimed that the "shorter hitters are going to struggle". Augusta National chairman](#cite_note-163) [Hootie Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_"Hootie"_Johnson) was unperturbed, stating, "We are comfortable with what we are doing with the golf course." After a practice round, Gary Player defended the changes, saying, "There have been a lot of criticisms, but I think unjustly so, now I've played it... The guys are basically having to hit the same second shots that Jack Nicklaus had to hit (in his prime)".

The first hole was shortened by 10 yards (9m) for the [2009 Masters Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Masters_Tournament). For the [2019 Masters Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Masters_Tournament), the fifth hole was lengthened by 40 yards (37m) from 455yards to 495yards, with two new gaping bunkers on the left side of the fairway. [The length of the course is 7,475 yards (6,835m).](#cite_note-165)

Originally, the grass on the putting greens was wide-bladed [Bermuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda_grass). The greens lost speed, especially during the late 1970s, after the introduction of a healthier strain of narrow-bladed Bermuda, which thrived and grew thicker. In 1978, the greens on the Par3 course were reconstructed with [bentgrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bentgrass), a narrow-bladed species that could be mowed shorter, eliminating grain. [After this test run, the greens on the main course were replaced with bentgrass in time for the 1981 Masters. The bentgrass resulted in significantly faster putting surfaces, which has required a reduction in some of the contours of the greens over time.](#cite_note-bent-166)

Just before the 1975 tournament, the common beige sand in the bunkers was replaced with the now-signature white feldspar. It is a [quartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartz) derivative of the mining of [feldspar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feldspar) and is shipped in from [North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina).

The Masters has the smallest field of the major championships, with 85–100 players. Unlike other majors, there are no alternates or qualifying tournaments. It is an invitational event, with invitations largely issued on an automatic basis to players who meet published criteria. The top 50 players in the [Official World Golf Ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_World_Golf_Ranking) are all invited.

Past champions are always eligible, but since 2002 the Augusta National Golf Club has discouraged them from continuing to participate at an advanced age. Some will later become honorary starters.

In 1952, the Masters began presenting an award, known as the Silver Cup, to the lowest-scoring amateur to make the cut. In 1954 they began presenting an amateur silver medal to the low amateur runner-up. There have been seven players to win low amateur and then go on to win the Masters as a professional. These players are [Cary Middlecoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cary_Middlecoff), [Jack Nicklaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Nicklaus), [Ben Crenshaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Crenshaw), [Phil Mickelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Mickelson), [Tiger Woods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger_Woods), [Sergio García](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_García), and [Hideki Matsuyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hideki_Matsuyama).

[Justin Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Rose) holds the record for the most times having led the tournament field after the first round, having done it 5times. [In 2023,](#cite_note-182) [Fred Couples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Couples) became the oldest player to make the cut, doing so at age 63years, 186days.

[Nick Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Price) and [Greg Norman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Norman) share the course record of63, with their rounds coming in 1986 and 1996 respectively.

The highest winning score of 289(+1) has occurred three times: [Sam Snead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Snead) in 1954, [Jack BurkeJr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Burke_Jr.) in 1956, and [Zach Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zach_Johnson) in 2007. [Anthony Kim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Kim) holds the record for most birdies in a round with11 in 2009 during his second round.

There have been only four [double eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Par_(score)#Albatross) carded in the history of the Masters; the latest was by a contender in the fourth round in 2012. In the penultimate pairing with eventual champion [Bubba Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubba_Watson), [Louis Oosthuizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Oosthuizen)'s 260-yard (238m) downhill [4 iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4_iron) from the fairway made the left side of the green at the par-5 second hole, called Pink Dogwood, rolled downhill, and in. [The other two occurrences of this feat after Sarazen's double eagle on the course's Fire Thorn hole in 1935:](#cite_note-184) [Bruce Devlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Devlin) made double eagle from 248 yards (227m) out with a 4-wood at the eighth hole (Yellow Jasmine) in the first round in 1967, while [Jeff Maggert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Maggert) hit a 3-iron 222 yards (203m) at the 13thhole (Azalea) in the fourth round in 1994.

Three players share the record for most runner-up finishes with four – [Ben Hogan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Hogan) (1942, 1946, 1954, 1955), [Tom Weiskopf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Weiskopf) (1969, 1972, 1974, 1975), and [Jack Nicklaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Nicklaus) (1964, 1971, 1977, 1981). Nicklaus and Tiger Woods are the only golfers to have won the Masters in three separate decades.

The highest official score in a round was95 by Charles Kunkle in 1956 and the highest unofficial score was106 by Billy Casper in 2005 (he refused to hand in his scorecard to avoid holding the record).

In Ireland, [Setanta Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Setanta_Sports) previously showed all four rounds, and now since 2017 [Eir Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eir_Sport) broadcast all four rounds live having previously broadcast the opening two rounds with [RTÉ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RTÉ) broadcasting the weekend coverage. [After Eir Sport's closure in 2021, Sky Sports will broadcast the event exclusively in Ireland for the first time, like in the UK.](#cite_note-216)

In [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada), broadcast rights to the Masters are held by [Bell Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Media), with coverage divided between [TSN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sports_Network) (cable), which carries live simulcasts and primetime encores of CBS and ESPN coverage for all four rounds, [CTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CTV_Television) (broadcast), which simulcasts CBS's coverage of the weekend rounds, and [RDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Réseau_des_sports), which carries French-language coverage. Prior to 2013, Canadian broadcast rights were held by a marketing company, Graham Sanborn Media, [which in turn bought time on the](#cite_note-218) [Global Television Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Television_Network), TSN, and RDS (except for 2012 when French-language coverage aired on [TVA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TVA_(Canada)) and [TVA Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TVA_Sports)) to air the broadcasts, also selling all of the advertising for the Canadian broadcasts. This was an unusual arrangement in Canadian sports broadcasting, as in most cases broadcasters acquire their rights directly from the event organizers or through partnerships with international rightsholders, such as [ESPN International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN_International) (ESPN owns a minority stake in TSN). In 2013, Global and TSN began selling advertising directly, and co-produced supplemental programs covering the tournament (while still carrying U.S. coverage for the tournament itself).

On December15, 2015, TSN parent company Bell Media announced that it had acquired exclusive Canadian rights to the tournament beginning 2016 under a multi-year deal. Broadcast television coverage moved to co-owned broadcast network CTV, while TSN uses its expanded five-channel service to carry supplemental feeds (including the Amen Corner feed and early coverage of each round) that were previously exclusive to digital platforms.

In [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France), the Masters is broadcast live on [Canal+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal+_(French_TV_channel)) and [Canal+ Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal+_Sport_(France)).

In 53countries, including much of Latin America, broadcast rights for the entire tournament are held by the [ESPN International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN_International) networks.

The Masters has an [app](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphone_app) developed by IBM as a "365-day operation" for its development team.

In 2025, ANGC introduced Mornings@ TheMasters, a 45-minute daily live-streamed show focused on culture and lifestyle, featuring influencers Hally Leadbetter and Roger Steele, co-produced with CBSSports, and broadcast on [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube).
