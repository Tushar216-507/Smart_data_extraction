Electrical engineering is an [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering) discipline concerned with the study, design, and application of equipment, devices, and systems that use [electricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity), [electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics), and [electromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetism). It emerged as an identifiable occupation in the latter half of the 19th century after the [commercialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercialization) of the [electric telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_telegraph), the telephone, and [electrical power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_power) generation, distribution, and use.

Electrical engineering emcompasses many different fields, including [computer engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_engineering), [systems engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_engineering), [power engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_engineering), [telecommunications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications), [radio-frequency engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio-frequency_engineering), [signal processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_processing), [instrumentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrumentation), [control engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_engineering), [photovoltaic cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photovoltaic_cell), [electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics), and [optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optics) and [photonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photonics). Many of these disciplines overlap with other engineering branches, spanning a huge number of specializations including hardware engineering, [power electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_electronics), [electromagnetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetics) and waves, [microwave engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave_engineering), [nanotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanotechnology), [electrochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrochemistry), renewable energies, mechatronics/control, and electrical materials science. [Machine learning and computer science techniques are also further studied due to being historically developed as fields of electrical engineering.](#cite_note-1)

Electrical engineers typically hold a [degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_degree) in electrical engineering, electronic or electrical and electronic engineering. Practicing engineers may have [professional certification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_certification) and be members of a [professional body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_body) or an international standards organization. These include the [International Electrotechnical Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Electrotechnical_Commission) (IEC), the [National Society of Professional Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Society_of_Professional_Engineers) (NSPE), the [Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Electrical_and_Electronics_Engineers) (IEEE) and the [Institution of Engineering and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institution_of_Engineering_and_Technology) (IET, formerly the IEE).

Electrical engineers work in a very wide range of industries and the skills required are likewise variable. These range from [circuit theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_theory) to the management skills of a [project manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_manager). The tools and equipment that an individual engineer may need are similarly variable, ranging from a simple [voltmeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltmeter) to sophisticated design and manufacturing software.

Electricity has been a subject of scientific interest since at least the early 17th century. [William Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Gilbert_(astronomer)) was a prominent early electrical scientist, and was the first to draw a clear distinction between [magnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetism) and [static electricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Static_electricity). He is credited with establishing the term "electricity". [He also designed the](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartinsenGrimnes2011411-2) [versorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versorium): a device that detects the presence of statically charged objects. In 1762 Swedish professor [Johan Wilcke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_Wilcke) invented a device later named [electrophorus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrophorus) that produced a static electric charge. [By 1800](#cite_note-3) [Alessandro Volta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Volta) had developed the [voltaic pile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltaic_pile), a forerunner of the electric battery.

In the 19th century, research into the subject started to intensify. Notable developments in this century include the work of [Hans Christian Ørsted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Christian_Ørsted), who discovered in 1820 that an electric current produces a magnetic field that will deflect a compass needle; of [William Sturgeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Sturgeon), who in 1825 invented the [electromagnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnet); of [Joseph Henry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Henry) and [Edward Davy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Davy), who invented the [electrical relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_relay) in 1835; of [Georg Ohm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Ohm), who in 1827 quantified the relationship between the [electric current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_current) and [potential difference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_difference) in a [conductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_conductor); of [Michael Faraday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Faraday), the discoverer of [electromagnetic induction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_induction) in 1831; and of [James Clerk Maxwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Clerk_Maxwell), who in 1873 published a unified [theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxwell's_equations) of electricity and [magnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetism) in his treatise Electricity and Magnetism.

In 1782, [Georges-Louis Le Sage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges-Louis_Le_Sage) developed and presented in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) probably the world's first form of [electric telegraphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_telegraph), using 24 different wires, one for each letter of the alphabet. This telegraph connected two rooms. It was an electrostatic telegraph that moved gold leaf through electrical conduction.

In 1795, [Francisco Salva Campillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Salva_Campillo) proposed an electrostatic telegraph system. Between 1803 and 1804, he worked on electrical telegraphy, and in 1804, he presented his report at the Royal Academy of Natural Sciences and Arts of Barcelona. Salva's electrolyte telegraph system was very innovative though it was greatly influenced by and based upon two discoveries made in Europe in 1800—Alessandro Volta's electric battery for generating an electric current and William Nicholson and Anthony Carlyle's electrolysis of water.[Electrical telegraphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_telegraph) may be considered the first example of electrical engineering. [Electrical engineering became a profession in the later 19th century. Practitioners had created a global](#cite_note-6) [electric telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_telegraph) network, and the first professional electrical engineering institutions were founded in the UK and the US to support the new discipline. [Francis Ronalds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Ronalds) created an electric telegraph system in 1816 and documented his vision of how the world could be transformed by electricity. [Over 50 years later, he joined the new Society of Telegraph Engineers (soon to be renamed the](#cite_note-8) [Institution of Electrical Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institution_of_Electrical_Engineers)) where he was regarded by other members as the first of their cohort. [By the end of the 19th century, the world had been forever changed by the rapid communication made possible by the engineering development of land-lines,](#cite_note-9) [submarine cables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_communications_cable), and, from about 1890, [wireless telegraphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wireless_telegraphy).

Practical applications and advances in such fields created an increasing need for standardized [units of measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Units_of_measure). They led to the international standardization of the units [volt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volt), [ampere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ampere), [coulomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coulomb), [ohm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohm), [farad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farad), and [henry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_(unit)). This was achieved at an international conference in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago) in 1893. [The publication of these standards formed the basis of future advances in standardization in various industries, and in many countries, the definitions were immediately recognized in relevant legislation.](#cite_note-FOOTNOTERosenberg20089-10)

During these years, the study of electricity was largely considered to be a subfield of [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics) since early electrical technology was considered [electromechanical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromechanical) in nature. The [Technische Universität Darmstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universität_Darmstadt) founded the world's first department of electrical engineering in 1882 and introduced the first-degree course in electrical engineering in 1883. [The first electrical engineering degree program in the United States was started at](#cite_note-12) [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) (MIT) in the physics department under Professor Charles Cross, [though it was](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWildesLindgren198519-13) [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) to produce the world's first electrical engineering graduates in 1885. [The first course in electrical engineering was taught in 1883 in Cornell's](#cite_note-14) [Sibley College of Mechanical Engineering and Mechanic Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sibley_College_of_Mechanical_Engineering_and_Mechanic_Arts).

In about 1885, Cornell President [Andrew Dickson White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Dickson_White) established the first Department of Electrical Engineering in the United States. [In the same year,](#cite_note-16) [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London) founded the first chair of electrical engineering in Great Britain. [Professor Mendell P. Weinbach at](#cite_note-17) [University of Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Missouri) established the electrical engineering department in 1886. [Afterwards, universities and](#cite_note-18) [institutes of technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institutes_of_technology) gradually started to offer electrical engineering programs to their students all over the world.

During these decades the use of electrical engineering increased dramatically. In 1882, [Thomas Edison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Edison) switched on the world's first large-scale electric power network that provided 110 volts—[direct current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_current) (DC)—to 59 customers on [Manhattan Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Island) in New York City. In 1884, [Sir Charles Parsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Charles_Parsons) invented the [steam turbine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_turbine) allowing for more efficient electric power generation. [Alternating current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternating_current), with its ability to transmit power more efficiently over long distances via the use of [transformers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformer), developed rapidly in the 1880s and 1890s with transformer designs by [Károly Zipernowsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Károly_Zipernowsky), [Ottó Bláthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottó_Bláthy) and [Miksa Déri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miksa_Déri) (later called ZBD transformers), [Lucien Gaulard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucien_Gaulard), [John Dixon Gibbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dixon_Gibbs) and [William Stanley Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Stanley_Jr.) Practical [AC motor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC_motor) designs including [induction motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induction_motor) were independently invented by [Galileo Ferraris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo_Ferraris) and [Nikola Tesla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikola_Tesla) and further developed into a practical [three-phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-phase) form by [Mikhail Dolivo-Dobrovolsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Dolivo-Dobrovolsky) and [Charles Eugene Lancelot Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Eugene_Lancelot_Brown).[Charles Steinmetz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Steinmetz) and [Oliver Heaviside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Heaviside) contributed to the theoretical basis of alternating current engineering. [The spread in the use of AC set off in the United States what has been called the](#cite_note-21) [war of the currents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_currents) between a [George Westinghouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Westinghouse) backed AC system and a Thomas Edison backed DC power system, with AC being adopted as the overall standard.

During the [development of radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invention_of_radio), many scientists and inventors contributed to [radio technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_communications) and electronics. The mathematical work of [James Clerk Maxwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Clerk_Maxwell) during the 1850s had shown the relationship of different forms of [electromagnetic radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_radiation), including the possibility of invisible airborne waves (later called "radio waves"). In his classic physics experiments of 1888, [Heinrich Hertz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Hertz) proved Maxwell's theory by transmitting [radio waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_wave) with a [spark-gap transmitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spark-gap_transmitter), and detected them by using simple electrical devices. Other physicists experimented with these new waves and, in the process, developed devices for transmitting and detecting them. In 1895, [Guglielmo Marconi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guglielmo_Marconi) began work on a way to adapt the known methods of transmitting and detecting these "Hertzian waves" into a purpose-built commercial [wireless telegraphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wireless_telegraphy) system. Early on, he sent wireless signals over a distance of one and a half miles. In December 1901, he sent wireless waves that were not affected by the curvature of the Earth. Marconi later transmitted the wireless signals across the Atlantic between Poldhu, [Cornwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornwall), and St. John's, [Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newfoundland), a distance of 2,100 miles (3,400km).

[Millimetre wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millimetre_wave) communication was first investigated by [Jagadish Chandra Bose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagadish_Chandra_Bose) during 1894–1896, when he reached an [extremely high frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremely_high_frequency) of up to 60[GHz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GHz) in his experiments. [He also introduced the use of](#cite_note-24) [semiconductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor) junctions to detect radio waves, [when he patented the radio](#cite_note-emerson-25) [crystal detector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_detector) in 1901.

In 1897, [Karl Ferdinand Braun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Ferdinand_Braun) introduced the [cathode-ray tube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathode-ray_tube) as part of an [oscilloscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscilloscope), a crucial enabling technology for [electronic television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television).[John Fleming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Ambrose_Fleming) invented the first radio tube, the [diode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diode), in 1904. Two years later, [Robert von Lieben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_von_Lieben) and [Lee De Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_De_Forest) independently developed the amplifier tube, called the [triode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triode).

In 1920, [Albert Hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Hull) developed the [magnetron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetron) which would eventually lead to the development of the [microwave oven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave_oven) in 1946 by [Percy Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percy_Spencer). [In 1934, the](#cite_note-31) [British military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_military) began to make strides toward [radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar) (which also uses the magnetron) under the direction of Dr Wimperis, culminating in the operation of the first radar station at [Bawdsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bawdsey) in August 1936.

In 1941, [Konrad Zuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konrad_Zuse) presented the [Z3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z3_(computer)), the world's first fully functional and programmable computer using electromechanical parts. In 1943, [Tommy Flowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Flowers) designed and built the [Colossus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus_(computer)), the world's first fully functional, electronic, digital and programmable computer. [In 1946, the](#cite_note-34) [ENIAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ENIAC) (Electronic Numerical Integrator and Computer) of [John Presper Eckert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Presper_Eckert) and [John Mauchly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Mauchly) followed, beginning the computing era. The arithmetic performance of these machines allowed engineers to develop completely new technologies and achieve new objectives.

In 1948, [Claude Shannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Shannon) published "A Mathematical Theory of Communication" which mathematically describes the passage of information with uncertainty ([electrical noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_noise)).

The first working [transistor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transistor) was a [point-contact transistor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point-contact_transistor) invented by [John Bardeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bardeen) and [Walter Houser Brattain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Houser_Brattain) while working under [William Shockley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Shockley) at the [Bell Telephone Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Telephone_Laboratories) (BTL) in 1947. [They then invented the](#cite_note-36) [bipolar junction transistor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipolar_junction_transistor) in 1948. [While early](#cite_note-37) [junction transistors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junction_transistor) were relatively bulky devices that were difficult to manufacture on a [mass-production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass-production) basis, [they opened the door for more compact devices.](#cite_note-Moskowitz-38)

The first [integrated circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_circuit) were the [hybrid integrated circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_integrated_circuit) invented by [Jack Kilby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Kilby) at [Texas Instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Instruments) in 1958 and the monolithic integrated circuit chip invented by [Robert Noyce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Noyce) at [Fairchild Semiconductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairchild_Semiconductor) in 1959.

The [MOSFET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MOSFET) (metal–oxide–semiconductor field-effect transistor, or MOS transistor) was invented by [Mohamed Atalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Atalla) and [Dawon Kahng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawon_Kahng) at BTL in 1959. [It was the first truly compact transistor that could be miniaturised and mass-produced for a wide range of uses.](#cite_note-triumph-43) [It revolutionized the](#cite_note-Moskowitz-38) [electronics industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics_industry), [becoming the most widely used electronic device in the world.](#cite_note-Grant-45)

The MOSFET made it possible to build [high-density integrated circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Very_large-scale_integration) chips. [The earliest experimental MOS IC chip to be fabricated was built by Fred Heiman and Steven Hofstein at](#cite_note-computerhistory-transistor-42) [RCA Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCA_Laboratories) in 1962. [MOS technology enabled](#cite_note-computerhistory-digital-48) [Moore's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moore's_law), the [doubling of transistors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transistor_count) on an IC chip every two years, predicted by [Gordon Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Moore) in 1965.[Silicon-gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon-gate) MOS technology was developed by [Federico Faggin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_Faggin) at Fairchild in 1968. [Since then, the MOSFET has been the basic building block of modern electronics.](#cite_note-50) [The mass-production of silicon MOSFETs and MOS integrated circuit chips, along with continuous](#cite_note-nytimes.com-52) [MOSFET scaling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MOSFET_scaling) miniaturization at an exponential pace (as predicted by Moore's law), has since led to revolutionary changes in technology, economy, culture and thinking.

The [Apollo program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_program) which culminated in [landing astronauts on the Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon_landing) with [Apollo 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_11) in 1969 was enabled by [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA)'s adoption of advances in [semiconductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor) [electronic technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_technology), including MOSFETs in the [Interplanetary Monitoring Platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interplanetary_Monitoring_Platform) (IMP) [and silicon integrated circuit chips in the](#cite_note-55) [Apollo Guidance Computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_Guidance_Computer) (AGC).

The development of MOS integrated circuit technology in the 1960s led to the invention of the [microprocessor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microprocessor) in the early 1970s. [The first single-chip microprocessor was the](#cite_note-Colinge2016-58) [Intel 4004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel_4004), released in 1971. [The Intel 4004 was designed and realized by Federico Faggin at Intel with his silicon-gate MOS technology,](#cite_note-computerhistory1971-57) [along with Intel's](#cite_note-computerhistory1971-57) [Marcian Hoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcian_Hoff) and [Stanley Mazor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Mazor) and Busicom's Masatoshi Shima. [The microprocessor led to the development of](#cite_note-ieee-59) [microcomputers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microcomputer) and personal computers, and the [microcomputer revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microcomputer_revolution).

In recent times, the subject of [machine learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_learning) (including speech systems, [computer vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_vision) and [reinforcement learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinforcement_learning)) has had significant overlap with electrical engineering fields such as signal processing, image processing and control engineering, and is, as such, studied often by electrical engineers. Machine learning techniques are also used in electrical engineering systems in subfields such as electronic design automation, stochastic and adaptive control, smart grids, adaptive signal processing, etc.

One of the properties of electricity is that it is very useful for energy transmission as well as for information transmission. These were also the first areas in which electrical engineering was developed. Today, electrical engineering has many subdisciplines, the most common of which are listed below. Although there are electrical engineers who focus exclusively on one of these subdisciplines, many deal with a combination of them. Sometimes, certain fields, such as [electronic engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_engineering) and [computer engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_engineering), are considered disciplines in their own right.

Power & Energy engineering deals with the [generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_generation), [transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_power_transmission), and [distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_power_distribution) of electricity as well as the design of a range of related devices. [These include](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrigsby2012-60) [transformers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformer), [electric generators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_generator), [electric motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_motor), high voltage engineering, and [power electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_electronics). In many regions of the world, governments maintain an electrical network called a [power grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_grid) that connects a variety of generators together with users of their energy. Users purchase electrical energy from the grid, avoiding the costly exercise of having to generate their own. Power engineers may work on the design and maintenance of the power grid as well as the power systems that connect to it. [Such systems are called on-grid power systems and may supply the grid with additional power, draw power from the grid, or do both. Power engineers may also work on systems that do not connect to the grid, called off-grid power systems, which in some cases are preferable to on-grid systems.](#cite_note-UNESCO-61)

Telecommunications engineering focuses on the [transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmission_(telecommunications)) of information across a [communication channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communication_channel) such as a [coax cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coax_cable), [optical fiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_fiber) or [free space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-space_optical_communication). [Transmissions across free space require information to be encoded in a](#cite_note-FOOTNOTETobin200715-62) [carrier signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_signal) to shift the information to a carrier frequency suitable for transmission; this is known as [modulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modulation). Popular analog modulation techniques include [amplitude modulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amplitude_modulation) and [frequency modulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency_modulation). [The choice of modulation affects the cost and performance of a system and these two factors must be balanced carefully by the engineer.](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChandrasekhar200621-63)

Once the transmission characteristics of a system are determined, telecommunication engineers design the [transmitters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmitter) and [receivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Receiver_(radio)) needed for such systems. These two are sometimes combined to form a two-way communication device known as a [transceiver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transceiver). A key consideration in the design of transmitters is their [power consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_consumption) as this is closely related to their [signal strength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_strength). [Typically, if the power of the transmitted signal is insufficient once the signal arrives at the receiver's antenna(s), the information contained in the signal will be corrupted by](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEZhangHuLuo2007448-65) [noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_noise), specifically static.

[Control engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_engineering) focuses on the [modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_model) of a diverse range of [dynamic systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_system) and the design of [controllers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controller_(control_theory)) that will cause these systems to behave in the desired manner. [To implement such controllers, electronics control engineers may use](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBissell199617-66) [electronic circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_circuit), [digital signal processors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_signal_processor), [microcontrollers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microcontroller), and [programmable logic controllers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programmable_logic_controller) (PLCs). Control engineering has a wide range of applications from the flight and propulsion systems of [commercial airliners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_airliner) to the [cruise control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruise_control) present in many modern [automobiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile). [It also plays an important role in](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcDavidEchaore-McDavid200995-67) [industrial automation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_automation).

Control engineers often use [feedback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feedback) when designing [control systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_system). For example, in an [automobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile) with [cruise control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruise_control), the vehicle's [speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed) is continuously monitored and fed back to the system, which adjusts the [motor's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_combustion_engine) [power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_(physics)) output accordingly. [Where there is regular feedback,](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEÅströmMurray2021108-68) [control theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_theory) can be used to determine how the system responds to such feedback.

Control engineers also work in [robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotics) to design autonomous systems using control algorithms which interpret sensory feedback to control actuators that move robots such as [autonomous vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_vehicle), autonomous drones and others used in a variety of industries.

Electronic engineering involves the design and testing of [electronic circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_circuit) that use the properties of [components](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_element) such as [resistors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resistor), [capacitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacitor), [inductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inductor), [diodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diode), and [transistors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transistor) to achieve a particular functionality. [The](#cite_note-UNESCO-61) [tuned circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuned_circuit), which allows the user of a radio to [filter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_filter) out all but a single station, is just one example of such a circuit. Another example to research is a pneumatic signal conditioner.

Prior to the Second World War, the subject was commonly known as radio engineering and basically was restricted to aspects of communications and [radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar), [commercial radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio), and [early television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television). [Later, in post-war years, as consumer devices began to be developed, the field grew to include modern television, audio systems, computers, and](#cite_note-UNESCO-61) [microprocessors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microprocessor). In the mid-to-late 1950s, the term radio engineering gradually gave way to the name electronic engineering.

Before the invention of the [integrated circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_circuit) in 1959, [electronic circuits were constructed from discrete components that could be manipulated by humans. These discrete circuits consumed much space and](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThompson20064-70) [power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_power) and were limited in speed, although they are still common in some applications. By contrast, integrated circuits packed a large number—often millions—of tiny electrical components, mainly [transistors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transistor), [into a small chip around the size of a](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMerhari2009233-71) [coin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coin). This allowed for the powerful computers and other electronic devices we see today.

[Microelectronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microelectronics) engineering deals with the design and [microfabrication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microfabrication) of very small electronic circuit components for use in an [integrated circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_circuit) or sometimes for use on their own as a general electronic component. [The most common microelectronic components are](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBhushan1997581-72) [semiconductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor) [transistors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transistor), although all main electronic components ([resistors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resistor), [capacitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacitor), etc.) can be created at a microscopic level.

[Nanoelectronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanoelectronics) is the further scaling of devices down to [nanometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanometer) levels. Modern devices are already in the nanometer regime, with below 100nm processing having been standard since around 2002.

Microelectronic components are created by chemically fabricating wafers of semiconductors such as silicon (at higher frequencies, [compound semiconductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compound_semiconductor) like gallium arsenide and indium phosphide can be used) to obtain the desired transport of electric charge and current. The field of microelectronics involves a significant amount of chemistry and material science and requires the electronic engineer to have a working knowledge of the effects of [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics).

[Signal processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_processing) deals with the analysis and manipulation of [signals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal). [Signals can be either](#cite_note-FOOTNOTETuzlukov201020-75) [analog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_signal), in which case the signal varies continuously according to the information, or [digital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_signal_(signal_processing)), in which case the signal varies according to a series of discrete values representing the information. For analog signals, signal processing may involve the [amplification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amplifier) and [filtering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filter_(signal_processing)) of audio signals for audio equipment or the [modulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modulation) and [demodulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demodulation) of signals for telecommunications. For digital signals, signal processing may involve the [compression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_compression), [error detection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Error_detection) and [error correction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Error_correction) of digitally sampled signals.

Signal processing is a mathematically oriented area forming the core of [digital signal processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_signal_processing) and is rapidly expanding with new applications in multiple fields of electrical engineering such as communications, control, radar, [audio engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_engineer), [broadcast engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_engineering), power electronics, and [biomedical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomedical_engineering) as many already existing analog systems are replaced with their digital counterparts. [Analog signal processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_signal_processing) is still important in the design of many [control systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_system).

DSP processor ICs are found in many types of modern electronic devices, such as digital [television sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_set), [radios,](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBayoumiSwartzlander199425-77) [hi-fi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hi-fi) audio equipment, mobile phones, [multimedia players](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portable_Media_Player), camcorders and digital cameras, automobile control systems, [noise cancelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_cancelling) headphones, digital [spectrum analyzers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum_analyzer), missile guidance systems, [radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar) systems, and [telematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telematics) systems. In such products, DSP may be responsible for [noise reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_reduction), [speech recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech_recognition) or [synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech_synthesis), [encoding or decoding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codec) digital media, wirelessly [transmitting or receiving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transceiver) data, triangulating positions using [GPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPS), and other kinds of [image processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_processing), [video processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_processing), [audio processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_signal_processing), and [speech processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech_processing).

[Instrumentation engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrumentation_engineering) deals with the design of devices to measure physical quantities such as [pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressure), [flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volumetric_flow_rate), and temperature. [The design of such instruments requires a good understanding of](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrantBixley2011159-79) [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics) that often extends beyond [electromagnetic theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_theory). For example, [flight instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_instrument) measure variables such as [wind speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_speed) and altitude to enable pilots to control the aircraft analytically. Similarly, [thermocouples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermocouple) use the [Peltier-Seebeck effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peltier-Seebeck_effect) to measure the temperature difference between two points.

Often instrumentation is not used by itself, but instead as the [sensors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensor) of larger electrical systems. For example, a thermocouple might be used to help ensure a furnace's temperature remains constant. [For this reason, instrumentation engineering is often viewed as the counterpart of control.](#cite_note-81)

Computer engineering deals with the design of computers and [computer systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_system). This may involve the design of new [hardware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_hardware). Computer engineers may also work on a system's software. However, the design of complex software systems is often the domain of software engineering, which is usually considered a separate discipline.[Desktop computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desktop_computer) represent a tiny fraction of the devices a computer engineer might work on, as computer-like architectures are now found in a range of [embedded devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embedded_device) including [video game consoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_console) and [DVD players](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD_player). Computer engineers are involved in many hardware and software aspects of computing.[Robots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robot) are one of the applications of computer engineering.

[Photonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photonics) and [optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optics) deal with the generation, transmission, amplification, modulation, detection, and analysis of [electromagnetic radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_radiation). The application of optics deals with design of optical instruments such as [lenses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lens), [microscopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microscope), [telescopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telescope), and other equipment that uses the properties of electromagnetic radiation. Other prominent applications of optics include [electro-optical sensors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electro-optical_sensor) and measurement systems, [lasers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser), [fiber-optic communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiber-optic_communication) systems, and optical disc systems (e.g., CD and DVD). Photonics builds heavily on optical technology, supplemented with modern developments such as [optoelectronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optoelectronics) (mostly involving [semiconductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor)), laser systems, [optical amplifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_amplifier) and novel materials (e.g. [metamaterials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metamaterial)).

[Mechatronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechatronics) is an engineering discipline that deals with the convergence of electrical and [mechanical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine) systems. Such combined systems are known as [electromechanical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromechanical) systems and have widespread adoption. Examples include [automated manufacturing systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automation),[heating, ventilation and air-conditioning systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HVAC), [and various subsystems of aircraft and](#cite_note-FOOTNOTELeondes2000199-85) [automobiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile).[Electronic systems design is the subject within electrical engineering that deals with the multi-disciplinary design issues of complex electrical and mechanical systems.](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShettyKolk201036-86)

The term mechatronics is typically used to refer to [macroscopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroscopic) systems, but [futurists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futures_studies) have predicted the emergence of very small electromechanical devices. Already, such small devices, known as [microelectromechanical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microelectromechanical_system) (MEMS), are used in automobiles to tell [airbags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbag) when to deploy, [in](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalufWilliams20043-88) [digital projectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_projector) to create sharper images, and in [inkjet printers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inkjet_printer) to create nozzles for high definition printing. In the future, it is hoped the devices will help build tiny implantable medical devices and improve [optical communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_communication).

In [aerospace engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace_engineering) and [robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotics), an example is the most recent [electric propulsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_propulsion) and [ion propulsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_propulsion).

Electrical engineers typically possess an [academic degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_degree) with a major in electrical engineering, [electronics engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics_engineering), [electronics and computer engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics_and_Computer_Engineering?action=edit&redlink=1), [electrical engineering technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering_technology), [or electrical and electronic engineering.](#cite_note-BLS3-90) [The same fundamental principles are taught in all programs, though emphasis may vary according to title. The length of study for such a degree is usually four or five years and the completed degree may be designated as a Bachelor of Science in Electrical/Electronics Engineering Technology,](#cite_note-92) [Bachelor of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Engineering), Bachelor of Science, [Bachelor of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Technology), or [Bachelor of Applied Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Applied_Science), depending on the university. The [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) generally includes units covering [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics), mathematics, [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), [project management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_management), and a [variety of topics in electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_electrical_engineering_topics). [Initially, such topics cover most, if not all, of the subdisciplines of electrical engineering.](#cite_note-Enterprise1986-93)

At many schools, electronic engineering is included as part of an electrical award, sometimes explicitly, such as a Bachelor of Engineering (Electrical and Electronic), but in others, electrical and electronic engineering are both considered to be sufficiently broad and complex that separate degrees are offered.

Some electrical engineers choose to study for a postgraduate degree such as a [Master of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Engineering)/Master of Science (MEng/MSc), a Master of [Engineering Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_Management), a Doctor of Philosophy (PhD) in Engineering, an [Engineering Doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_Doctorate) (Eng.D.), or an [Engineer's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer's_degree). The master's and engineer's degrees may consist of either research, [coursework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coursework) or a mixture of the two. The Doctor of Philosophy and Engineering Doctorate degrees consist of a significant research component and are often viewed as the entry point to academia. In the United Kingdom and some other European countries, Master of Engineering is often considered to be an undergraduate degree of slightly longer duration than the Bachelor of Engineering, rather than a standalone postgraduate degree.

In most countries, a bachelor's degree in engineering represents the first step towards [professional certification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_certification) and the degree program itself is certified by a [professional body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_body). [After completing a certified degree program the engineer must satisfy a range of requirements (including work experience requirements) before being certified. Once certified the engineer is designated the title of](#cite_note-Labor2008-96) [Professional Engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_Engineer) (in the United States, Canada and South Africa), [Chartered engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chartered_engineer) or [Incorporated Engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incorporated_Engineer) (in India, Pakistan, the United Kingdom, Ireland and [Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe)), Chartered Professional Engineer (in Australia and New Zealand) or [European Engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Engineer) (in much of the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union)).

The advantages of licensure vary depending on location. For example, in the United States and Canada, "only a licensed engineer may seal engineering work for public and private clients". [This requirement is enforced by state and provincial legislation such as](#cite_note-97) [Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec)'s Engineers Act. [In other countries, no such legislation exists. Practically all certifying bodies maintain a](#cite_note-98) [code of ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethical_code) that they expect all members to abide by or risk expulsion. [In this way these organizations play an important role in maintaining ethical standards for the profession. Even in jurisdictions where certification has little or no legal bearing on work, engineers are subject to](#cite_note-99) [contract law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contract_law). In cases where an engineer's work fails he or she may be subject to the [tort of negligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tort_of_negligence) and, in extreme cases, the charge of [criminal negligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_negligence). An engineer's work must also comply with numerous other rules and regulations, such as [building codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building_code) and legislation pertaining to [environmental law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_law).

Professional bodies of note for electrical engineers include the [Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Electrical_and_Electronics_Engineers) (IEEE) and the [Institution of Engineering and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institution_of_Engineering_and_Technology) (IET). The IEEE, which claims to produce 30% of the world's literature in electrical engineering, has over 360,000 members worldwide and holds over 3,000 conferences annually. [The IET publishes 21 journals, has a worldwide membership of over 150,000, and claims to be the largest professional engineering society in Europe.](#cite_note-100) [Obsolescence of technical skills is a serious concern for electrical engineers. Membership and participation in technical societies, regular reviews of periodicals in the field and a habit of continued learning are therefore essential to maintaining proficiency. An MIET(Member of the Institution of Engineering and Technology) is recognised in Europe as an Electrical and computer (technology) engineer.](#cite_note-102)

In Australia, Canada, and the United States, electrical engineers make up around 0.25% of the labor force.

From the [Global Positioning System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Positioning_System) to [electric power generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_power_generation), electrical engineers have contributed to the development of a wide range of technologies. They design, develop, test, and supervise the deployment of electrical systems and electronic devices. For example, they may work on the design of telecommunications systems, the operation of [electric power stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_power_station), the [lighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lighting) and [wiring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiring) of buildings, the design of [household appliances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Household_appliance), or the electrical [control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_theory) of industrial machinery.

Fundamental to the discipline are the sciences of [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics) and mathematics, as these help to obtain both a [qualitative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qualitative_data) and [quantitative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantity) description of how such systems will work. Today, most engineering work involves the use of [computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer), and it is commonplace to use [computer-aided design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer-aided_design) programs when designing electrical systems. Nevertheless, the ability to sketch ideas is still invaluable for quickly communicating with others.

Although most electrical engineers will understand basic [circuit theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_theory) (that is, the interactions of elements such as [resistors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resistor), [capacitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacitor), [diodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diode), [transistors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transistor), and [inductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inductor) in a circuit), the theories employed by engineers generally depend upon the work they do. For example, [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics) and [solid state physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid_state_physics) might be relevant to an engineer working on [VLSI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VLSI) (the design of integrated circuits), but are largely irrelevant to engineers working with macroscopic electrical systems. Even [circuit theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_theory) may not be relevant to a person designing telecommunications systems that use [off-the-shelf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_off-the-shelf) components. Perhaps the most important technical skills for electrical engineers are reflected in university programs, which emphasize [strong numerical skills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numeracy), [computer literacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_literacy), and the ability to understand the [technical language and concepts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_terminology) that relate to electrical engineering.

A wide range of instrumentation is used by electrical engineers. For simple control circuits and alarms, a basic [multimeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multimeter) measuring [voltage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltage), [current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_current), and [resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_resistance) may suffice. Where time-varying signals need to be studied, the [oscilloscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscilloscope) is also an ubiquitous instrument. In [RF engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RF_engineering) and high-frequency telecommunications, [spectrum analyzers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum_analyzer) and [network analyzers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_analyzer_(electrical)) are used. In some disciplines, safety can be a particular concern with instrumentation. For instance, medical electronics designers must take into account that much lower voltages than normal can be dangerous when electrodes are directly in contact with internal body fluids. [Power transmission engineering also has great safety concerns due to the high voltages used; although](#cite_note-FOOTNOTELeitgeb2010122-110) [voltmeters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltmeter) may in principle be similar to their low voltage equivalents, safety and calibration issues make them very different. [Many disciplines of electrical engineering use tests specific to their discipline. Audio electronics engineers use](#cite_note-111) [audio test sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_system_measurements) consisting of a signal generator and a meter, principally to measure level but also other parameters such as [harmonic distortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic_distortion) and [noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_(electronics)). Likewise, information technology has its own test sets, often specific to a particular data format, and the same is true of television broadcasting.

For many engineers, technical work accounts for only a fraction of the work they do. A lot of time may also be spent on tasks such as discussing proposals with clients, preparing [budgets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budget) and determining [project schedules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schedule_(project_management)). [Many senior engineers manage a team of](#cite_note-112) [technicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technician) or other engineers and for this reason [project management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_management) skills are important. Most engineering projects involve some form of documentation and [strong written communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_writing) skills are therefore very important.

The [workplaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office) of engineers are just as varied as the types of work they do. Electrical engineers may be found in the pristine lab environment of a [fabrication plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabrication_plant), on board a [Naval ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_ship), the offices of a [consulting firm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consulting_firm) or on site at a mine. During their working life, electrical engineers may find themselves supervising a wide range of individuals, including scientists, [electricians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrician), [computer programmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_programmer), and other engineers.

Electrical engineering has an intimate relationship with the physical sciences. For instance, the physicist [Lord Kelvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Kelvin) played a major role in the engineering of the first [transatlantic telegraph cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transatlantic_telegraph_cable). [Conversely, the engineer](#cite_note-114) [Oliver Heaviside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Heaviside) produced major work on the mathematics of transmission on telegraph cables. [Electrical engineers are often required on major science projects. For instance, large](#cite_note-115) [particle accelerators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_accelerator) such as [CERN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CERN) need electrical engineers to deal with many aspects of the project including the power distribution, the instrumentation, and the manufacture and installation of the [superconducting electromagnets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_electromagnet).
