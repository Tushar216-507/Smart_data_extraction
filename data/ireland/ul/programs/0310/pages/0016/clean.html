Áine Hennessy

I graduated from UL with the Bachelor of Science in Environmental Science in August 2023. The reason I chose this course was the variety of career opportunities that graduates can avail of in a wide range of sectors. From food & beverage, pharmaceuticals, construction, consultancy, and environmental agencies and authorities, the possibilities are endless.  

I have always had an interest in science, specifically biology and chemistry, but the Environmental Science course really gives a blend of all science subjects and illustrates their presence in the “real-world”. While I have always had an interest in science, I have often found that I needed help in certain aspects of the laboratory and calculations aspects of science subjects. My biology and chemistry teachers in secondary school were both UL graduates and, having discussed my concerns with them and other UL graduates, I was assured that help was always around in the form of UL’s many free learning centres and its comprehensive library facilities. The tutors and staff in the learning centres and the library really aided in my understanding of coursework and allowed me to progress confidently throughout my degree.  

My Cooperative Education experience was a key highlight and turning point in my career path. I completed my eight-month placement at Janssen Pharmaceutical Sciences in Little Island, Co. Cork. On my placement I worked primarily in the Wastewater Treatment Plant Laboratory as a student laboratory scientist, but I was also given many more opportunities to visit other areas of the site and partake in projects. That showed me how many aspects of my course were needed throughout the business, from waste management to health and safety, etc. My placement in the environmental laboratory at Janssen really sparked my interest in water treatment technologies and stewardship, both at an industrial level and a catchment level. I further pursued this interest in my final year project looking at the effects of agricultural activities on two separate catchments in Ireland. Using Geographical Information System (GIS) mapping software and applying possible mitigation measures studied throughout my coursework, I proposed solutions to aid issues of excess nutrients in waterbodies throughout these catchments.  

I am currently employed at 20FIFTY Partners in Limerick. 20FIFTY Partners designs and delivers sustainability programmes that help governments, agencies and individual organisations build competencies and skills to transition to more sustainable outcomes. My key areas of focus are on water stewardship, contemporary wastewater treatments and technologies, and on assessing the current state of our clients’ water risk and catchments. Aligned with my job at 20FIFTY Partners, I am also currently pursuing a two-year research masters at UL. My project addresses the EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive requirements and investigates how organisations can track the impact of their water and wastewater-related activities on their catchments.  

UL gave me the opportunity to further explore and apply my environmental and scientific interests by introducing me to people knowledgeable in these fields and by providing me with the access to opportunities to further both my education and my career.
