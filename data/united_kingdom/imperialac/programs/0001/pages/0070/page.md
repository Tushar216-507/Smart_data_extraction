Your personal statement is a really important part of your UCAS application.

It helps our admissions tutors get an insight into who you are and whether you'd be a good fit for the course you're applying to.

You can only submit one personal statement for the five courses and universities you apply for. We do not allow the submission of an alternative personal statement.

## What do you need to cover in your personal statement?

## Personal statement for 2027 entry

For 2027 entry, personal statements will be structured into three distinct sections, each addressing a specific question:

- Why do you want to study this course or subject?
- How have your qualifications and studies prepared you for this course?
- What experiences outside of education have helped you prepare, and why are they valuable?

Each section must be at least 350 characters, with an overall character limit of 4,000 characters. A built-in character counter will help you stay on track.

For additional help, subject specific guides have been made available on the [UCAS website](https://www.ucas.com/applying/applying-university/writing-your-personal-statement/2026-personal-statement-guides).

## What we're looking for

We want to see a genuine passion for the subject(s) you’re applying for. Be specific about the experiences, skills, and insights that have shaped your decision to pursue this course.

Since your academic achievements are covered elsewhere in your application, focus this space on:

- [Your enthusiasm for the subject – What excites you about studying it?](#subject)
- [Your skills and aptitude – What strengths make you a good fit for the course?](#skills)
- [Relevant extracurricular activities – How have these experiences helped you develop?](#develop)

## Your enthusiasm for the subject

### What excites you about studying it?

We want to see a genuine passion for the subject and your understanding of the course you are applying for.

It's useful to highlight things that inspire your interest, including:

- Books you have read
- Work or voluntary experience you have undertaken
- Lectures or courses you have attended
- Any relevant extracurricular activities you’ve taken part in

This a really important part of your personal statement so make sure that your passion really shines through.

This means avoid saying you want to study something just because it is interesting.

Instead, explain what you find interesting about it, for example, a particular topic you know you’ll be able to carry on studying at university or something related to it.

Don’t be afraid to use language which shows your enthusiasm for the course or life in general. Be positive and focus on things you thoroughly enjoy.

## Your skills and aptitude

### What strengths make you a good fit for the course?

At Imperial, you'll have the freedom to take control of your learning, with options within many courses to customise what you learn and the experiences that you have.

As a research-led university, we'll encourage you to get hands-on in the process of discovery, innovation and learning through and from your own mistakes.

You will also learn to communicate your research findings and have the opportunity to work in teams, as well as on your own.

Think about the sort of skills that you have gained that are relevant to your chosen subject – you'll be able to find more detail about the style of learning for your chosen subject on our [course pages](/study/courses/).

Don't forget to provide evidence for the skills and knowledge that you have. And remember to choose quality over quantity – we'd rather read about a smaller number of skills with evidence than a long list which doesn't explain how you've acquired each skill/quality and how it will help you be successful in your chosen course of study.

## Relevant extra curricular activities

### How have these experiences helped you develop?

Work experience isn’t essential for most Imperial courses, but if it is a course requirement, make sure you get some and then tell us how it's helped to confirm your choice of subject.

You may also wish to use your personal statement to outline your career aspirations and motivation for the future.

## Get the right balance

Imperial is not all work and no play. Our students have a huge range of interests – evidenced in our 350+ student-run clubs and societies.

Tell us about your passions and your hobbies, particularly how they have helped you develop skills and qualities that make you a strong candidate. For example, how have they made you a better leader, or more resilient, or improved your teamwork and communication skills.

Also tell us about something you do simply for the fun of it. You don't need to go into too much detail as you don’t have the space, but it helps us to get to know you, and it may be something we ask you more about if you're invited for an interview at a later stage.

## Make it personal

Your personal statement should be about you so don't focus on what your friends are writing. Be clear about your own motivations and what you can bring to Imperial.

Use it to create a unique picture for our admissions tutors of who you are and why you'll be successful in your chosen course of study.

Also avoid the temptation to copy anyone else's. UCAS runs all personal statements through its similarity detection system, Copycatch, to compare them with previous statements.
