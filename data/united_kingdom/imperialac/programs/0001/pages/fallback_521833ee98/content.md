A human, or human being, is a member of the [species](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species) [Homo sapiens](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_sapiens), which means 'wise man' in [Latin](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin).[Carolus Linnaeus](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolus_Linnaeus) put humans in the [mammalian](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammal) [order](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_(biology)) of [primates](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primate). [Humans are a species of](#cite_note-msw3-1) [hominid](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hominidae), and [bonobos](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonobo), [chimpanzees](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimpanzee), [gorillas](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorilla) and [orangutans](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orangutan) are their closest living relatives.

Humans are mammals. They are also social animals, and usually live in groups. They help and protect each other. They care for their [children](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child) for many years until they can do so for themselves. Humans are [bipedal](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipedalism), which means they walk on two [legs](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legs).

Humans have complex [brains](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain), which is much larger than that of other living apes. They use [language](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language), make [ideas](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idea), and feel [emotions](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emotion). This brain, and the fact that [arms](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arm) are not needed for [walking](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walking), lets humans use [tools](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tool). Humans use tools far more than any other species. Their tools can range from simple sticks as weapons, to very advanced projectile weapons, such as [guns](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun).

Humans first evolved in Africa. They soon spread out across the world, and began living on every [continent](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continent). [In 2023, there were more than 8.1](#cite_note-6) [billion](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billion) humans living on [Earth](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth).

Humans have a long period of development after birth. Their life depends less on [instinct](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instinct) than other animals and more on [learning](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learning). Humans are also born with their [brains](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain) not so well developed as those of other [mammals](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammal). This makes for an unusually long [childhood](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Childhood), so [family](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family) life is important. If their brains were better developed at birth, their head would be larger, and this would make [birth](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth) more difficult. In birth, the baby's head has to get through the 'birth canal', the passageway through the mother's [pelvis](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelvis).

Many animals use [signs](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sign) and sounds to communicate with each other. But humans have [language](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language). It lets them express ideas by using words. Humans are capable of [abstract ideas](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstraction) and can communicate them to others. Human language can express things which are not present or talk about events that are not happening at that time. [The things might be elsewhere, and the events may also have occurred at another place or time.](#cite_note-8)

No known animals have a system of [communication](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communication) that is as elaborate as human language. By using words to communicate with each other, humans make [complex](https://simple.wiktionary.org/wiki/complex) [communities](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community) with [laws](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laws), [traditions](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tradition) and [customs](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customs). Humans like to understand the [world](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/World) around them. They try to explain things through [myth](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myth), [science](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science) and [philosophy](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy). Wanting to understand things has helped humans make important discoveries.

Humans are the only species living today known to build [fires](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire), to [cook](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooking) their [food](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food) and wear [clothes](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clothes). Humans use more [technology](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology) than any other animal on Earth ever has. Humans like things that are [beautiful](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beauty) and like to make [art](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art), [literature](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literature) and [music](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music). Humans use [education](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education) and [teaching](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teaching) to pass on [skills](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skills), [ideas](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idea) and [customs](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Custom) to the next [generations](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generation).

Humans are part of the animal kingdom. They are [mammals](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammal), which means that they give birth to their young, and females feed their babies with breast milk. Humans belong to the order of [primates](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primate). Apes like orangutans, gorillas, chimps and [gibbons](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibbon) are also primates.

The closest living relatives of humans are the two chimpanzee species: the [common chimpanzee](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_chimpanzee) and the [bonobo](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonobo). Scientists have examined the [genes](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics) of humans and chimpanzees and compared their [DNA](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA). The studies showed that 95% to 99% of the DNA of humans and chimpanzees is the same.

Biologists explain the similarity between humans and other [hominoids](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ape) by their descent from a common ancestor. In 2001, a [hominid](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hominid) skull was discovered in [Chad](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad). The skull is about 7 million years old and has been classified as [Sahelanthropus tchadensis](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahelanthropus_tchadensis). This [skull](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skull) may show that the date at which humans started to [evolve](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution) (develop differently) from other primates is 2 million years earlier than scientists had previously thought.

Humans are part of a [subfamily](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subfamily) called the Homininae (or [hominins](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hominin)), inside the [hominids](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hominid) or [great apes](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_ape).

There used to be other types of hominins on Earth. They were similar to but not the same as modern humans. Homo sapiens are the only type of hominins who are alive today. [The earliest known fossils of genus Homo have been called](#cite_note-16) [Homo habilis](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_habilis) (handy man). The first [fossils](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil) of Homo habilis were found in [Tanzania](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania). Homo hablilis is thought to have lived about 2.2 to 1.7 million years ago. [Another human species thought to be an ancestor of the modern human is](#cite_note-17) [Homo erectus](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_erectus). [There are other extinct species of Homo known today. Many of them were likely our 'cousins', as they developed differently to our ancestors.](#cite_note-18) [Different species of plants and animals moved from Africa to the Middle East, and then elsewhere. Early humans may have moved from Africa to other parts of the world in the same way.](#cite_note-19)

The first truly modern humans seem to have appeared between 300,000 [and 200,000 years ago in](#cite_note-20) [East Africa](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Africa). [In](#cite_note-Hua_Liu,_2006_230–237-23) [paleontology](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleontology), 200,000 years is a "short" time. So, [scientists](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientist) speak of a "recent single origin" of humans. Some of these early humans later moved out from [Africa](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa). By about 90,000 years ago, they had moved into [Russia](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia). This was the area where [Neanderthals](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neanderthal), Homo neanderthalensis, had been living for a long time (at least 350,000 years).

By about 42,000 to 44,000 years ago Homo sapiens had reached western [Europe](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe), including [Russia](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia). [In Europe and western Asia, Homo sapiens replaced the Neanderthals by about 35,000 years ago. The details of this event are not known.](#cite_note-24)

At roughly the same time, Homo sapiens arrived in [Australia](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia). Their arrival in the [Americas](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americas) was much later, about 15,000 years ago. [All these earlier groups of modern man were](#cite_note-25) [hunter-gatherers](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer).

[Early human history](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-history) is commonly divided into three ages. The time periods are labeled with the material used for tools.

- The [Stone Age](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Age)
- The [Bronze Age](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age)
- The [Iron Age](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age)

The "Stone Age" is commonly subdivided into the [Paleolithic](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic), [Mesolithic](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesolithic), and [Neolithic](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) periods.

Up to about 10 thousand years ago, most humans were [hunter-gatherers](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer). They did not live in one place. Instead, they [moved around](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomads) as the [seasons](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Season) changed. The start of planting [crops](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crops) for food, called [farming](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farming), made the [Neolithic revolution](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_revolution). Some people chose to live in [settlements](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_settlement). This also led to the invention of [metal](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal) tools and the training of [animals](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal). About 6,000 years ago, the first proper civilizations began in places, like [Egypt](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt), [India](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India), and [Syria](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria). The people formed governments and [armies](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army) for protection. They competed for area to live and resources, and sometimes they [fought with each other](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/War). About 4,000 years ago, some states took over or conquered other states and made [empires](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire). Examples include ancient [Greece](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece) and the [Roman](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) Empire.

Some modern-day religions also began at this time, such as [Judaism](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism) and [Hinduism](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism). From the Middle Ages and beyond, humanity saw an explosion of new technology and inventions. The printing press, the [car](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car), the [train](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway), and [electricity](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity) are all examples of this kind of invention. As a result of the developments in technology, modern humans live in a world where everyone is connected, for example by [telephone](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone) or by [internet](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet). People now control and change the [environment](https://simple.wiktionary.org/wiki/environment) around them in many different ways.

In early times, humans usually settled near to [water](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water) and other [natural](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature) [resources](https://simple.wiktionary.org/wiki/resource). In modern times, if people need things, they can [transport](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport) them from somewhere else. Therefore, basing a settlement close to resources is no longer as important as it once was. Since 1800, the number of humans, or [population](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population), has [increased](https://simple.wiktionary.org/wiki/increase) by six billion. [Most humans (61%) live in](#cite_note-26) [Asia](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia). The rest live in [the Americas](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Americas) (14%), [Africa](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa) (14%), [Europe](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe) (11%), and [Oceania](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania) (0.5%).

Most people live in [towns](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town) and [cities](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/City). This number is expected to get higher. In 2005, the [United Nations](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations) said that by the end of that year, over half the world would be living in cities. This is an important change in human settlement patterns: a century earlier in 1900, only 14% of people lived in cities; in 2000, 47% of the world's population lived in cities. In developed countries, like the [United States](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States), 80% of the population live in cities.

Humans have a large effect on the world. Humans are at the top of the [food chain](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_chain) and are generally not eaten by any animals. Humans have been described as super [predators](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predator) because of this. [Because of](#cite_note-28) [industry](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industry) and other reasons, humans are said to be a big cause of [global](https://simple.wiktionary.org/wiki/global) [climate change](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change).

Human body measurements differ. The worldwide average [height for an adult human](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_height) male is about 172cm (5ft 7+1⁄2in), and the worldwide average height for adult human females is about 158cm (5ft 2in). The average weight of an adult human is 54–64kg (119–141lb) for females and 70–83kg (154–183lb) for males. [Body weight and body type is influenced by](#cite_note-31) [genetics](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics) and environment. It varies greatly among individuals.

Human [hair](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hair) grows on the underarms, the [genitals](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genitalia), legs, arms, and on the top of the [head](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head) in adults of both genders. Hair will usually grow on the [face](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face) of most adult males and on the [chest](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chest) and back of many adult males. In human children of both genders, long hair grows only on the top of the head. Although it might look like humans have fewer hairs than most [primates](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primate), they actually do not. The average human has more hair follicles, where hair grows from than most chimpanzees have. [Human hair can be](#cite_note-32) [black](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black), [brown](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown), [red](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red) or [blond](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blond). [When humans get older, hair can turn](#cite_note-33) [grey](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey) or [white](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/White).

Human [skin](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin) colors vary greatly. They can be a very pale [pink](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink) all the way to dark [brown](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown). There is a reason why people in tropical areas have dark skins. The dark pigment ([melanin](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanin)) in the skin protects them against [ultraviolet](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraviolet) rays in [sunlight](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunlight). The damage caused by UV rays can and does cause [skin cancer](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin_cancer) in some people. Therefore, in more sunny areas, [natural selection](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_selection) favors darker skin color. [Sun tanning has nothing to do with this issue, because it is just a temporary process, which is not inherited. In colder climates, the advantage of light-coloured skin is two-fold. It radiates less heat and absorbs more sunlight. In weaker sunlight, a darker body produces less](#cite_note-35) [vitamin D](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_D) than a lighter body. The selection for lighter skin is driven by these two reasons. Therefore, in less sunny areas, natural selection favours lighter skin colour.

Humans are not as strong as other [primates](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primate) of the same size. An average [female](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Female) orangutan is at least three times as strong as an average human.

The average human needs 7 to 8 hours [sleep](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleep) a day. People who sleep less than this are generally not as [healthy](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health). A child needs even more sleep, 9 to 10 hours on average.

The human life cycle is similar to most other [mammals](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammal). However, there are some differences. The young grow inside the female [mother](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother) for nine [months](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Month). After this time, the [baby](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baby) is pushed out of the woman's [vagina](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vagina), with its brain only half developed.

Unlike most other mammals, human childbirth is somewhat dangerous. Babies' heads are large, and the mothers [pelvis](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelvis) [bones](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bones) are not very wide. Since people walk on two [legs](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legs), their hips are fairly narrow. This means that birth can be difficult. Rarely, mother or baby may [die](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death) in childbirth. [The number of mothers dying in childbirth is lower in the 21st century. This is because of better](#cite_note-40) [medication](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medication) and treatment. If a difficult birth is foreseen, the [foetus](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foetus) can be removed surgically, and [incubated](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incubation) (developed, but not in the womb).

In many poor countries, the number of mothers dying is higher. Sometimes it is up to 10 times as many as richer countries.

In the human female, her fertile period in the [oestrous cycle](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oestrous_cycle) is hidden, and mating can take place at any time. That is quite unusual. In mammals generally, the fertile period is very noticeable. Mating only takes place when the female signals her fertility. The human cycle is unusual, and it is thought that there is a reason. Humans band together in tribes which have many people. It helps the tribe if the father of a child is not known for certainty. Men live together and work together in much larger groups than do chimpanzees (our nearest living relatives). They have a collective interest in the tribe. It is thought that the human mating system helps this.

The average human baby [weighs](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weight) 3–4 [kg](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kg) at birth and is 50–60 [cm](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centimetre) tall. Babies are often smaller in poorer countries [and may die early because of this.](#cite_note-44)

Humans have four stages in their lives: [childhood](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children), [adolescence](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teenager), [adulthood](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult) and [old age](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_age).

[Life expectancy](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy) is how long you are expected to live. This depends on many things including where you live. The highest life expectancy is for people from [Monaco](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monaco), 89.52 years. The lowest is for people from [Chad](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad) where life expectancy is only 49.81 years. [Human lifespans can be longer than other mammals, even](#cite_note-46) [elephants](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elephant).

[Psychology](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology) is the study of how the human [mind](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind) works. The human [brain](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain) is the main controller of what a person does. Everything from [moving](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement) and [breathing](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breathing) to [thinking](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thought) is done by the brain. The human [neocortex](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neocortex) is huge compared with other [mammals](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammals). It gives us our thinking ability and the ability to speak and understand [language](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language).

[Neurology](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurology) is the study of how the brain works while [psychology](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology) is the study of how and why people think and [feel](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emotion). Many aspects of life are also influenced by the [hormone system](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormone_system), including [growth](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growth) and sexual development. The hormonal system (especially the [pituitary gland](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_gland)) is partly controlled by the brain.

Human behaviour is hard to understand, so sometimes, psychologists study [animals](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal) because they may be simpler and easier to know. Psychology overlaps with many other sciences, including [medicine](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicine), [biology](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology), [computer science](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) and [linguistics](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics).

[Language](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language) at its most basic is talking, [reading](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reading) and [writing](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writing). The study of language is called [linguistics](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics). Humans have the most complicated languages on Earth. Although almost all animals communicate, human language is unique. Its use of [syntax](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syntax) and its huge learnt [vocabulary](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocabulary) are its main features. [There are over 7,300 languages spoken around the world. The world's most spoken](#cite_note-47) [first language](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_language) is [Mandarin Chinese](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese), and the most spoken language is English. [This includes speakers of English as a](#cite_note-48) [second language](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_language).

This is to explain the difference between features which are long established, and ones which are relatively recent in evolution. The human ability to walk and run upright on two legs is recent. The ability to speak is even more recent. Contrast those two with something ancient, like the way most land vertebrates walk on four legs.

In every case, the older way is very reliable in its development, and the new tactic is more variable, and involves some learning.

[Art](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art) has existed almost as long as humans. People have been doing some types of art for thousands of years as the picture on the right shows. Art represents how someone feels in the form of a [painting](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Painting), a [sculpture](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sculpture) or a [photograph](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photograph).

[Music](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music) has also been around for thousands of years. Music can be made with only your [voice](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice), but most of the time, people use [instruments](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_instrument). Music can be made using simple instruments only such as simple [drums](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drums) all the way up to [electric guitars](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_guitar), [keyboards](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keyboard_instrument) and [violins](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violin). Music can be loud, fast, quiet, slow or many other different styles. Music represents how the people who are playing the music feel.

[Literature](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literature) is anything made or written using [language](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language). This includes [books](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Books), [poetry](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poetry), [legends](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legend), [myths](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myths) and [fairy tales](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairy_tale). Literature is important as without it many of the things we use today, such as [Wikipedia](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia), would not exist.

Humans often categorize themselves by race or ethnicity. Modern [biologists](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology) know that human [gene](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene) sequences are very similar compared to many other animals. Ethnic groups are often linked by linguistic, cultural, ancestral, and national or regional ties. Race and ethnicity can lead to different social treatment called [racism](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism).

[Religion](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion) is a [belief](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belief) of [faith](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faith) in a higher being, spirit, or any system of ideas that a group of people believe in. To have [faith](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faith) in a belief is to have the belief without proof that it is true. Faith can bring people together because they all believe in the same thing. Some of the things that religions talk about are what happens after [death](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death), why humans exist, how humans came to exist ([creation](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creation_myth)), and what is good to do and not to do ([morality](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morality)). Some people are very [religious](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious). Many people believe in one all-powerful [god](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/God); some people believe in more than one god; some people are [atheists](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism) who do not believe in a god; and some people are [agnostics](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnosticism) who are not sure if there is a god.

Technology are the things and methods which humans use to make tasks easier. Science is understanding how the [universe](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universe) and the things in it work. Technology used to be quite simple. It was passed on by people telling others until [writing](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writing) was invented. This allowed technology to [develop](https://simple.wiktionary.org/wiki/develop) much quicker. Now people understand more and more about the world and the universe. The use of the [telescope](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telescope) by [Galileo](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo), [Einstein's](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein) theory of [relativity](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_relativity), [lasers](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasers), and [computing](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computing) are all scientific discoveries. Technology is important to science, to medicine, and to everyday life.

A [war](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/War) is a [lethal](https://simple.wiktionary.org/wiki/lethal) fight between large groups of people, usually [countries](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country) or [states](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/State). A war involves the use of lethal weapons as both sides try to kill the other. It is estimated that during the 20th century, between 167 and 188 million humans [died](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death) because of war. [The people who fight for a state in wars are called](#cite_note-54) [soldiers](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soldiers). The people who fight in wars, but not for a state, are usually called "fighters".

Modern wars are very different from wars a thousand or even a hundred years ago. Modern war involves sabotage, [terrorism](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism), [propaganda](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda), and [guerrilla warfare](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerrilla_warfare). In modern-day wars, civilians (people who are not soldiers) are often targets. An example of this is the [nuclear bomb](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_bomb) dropped on [Hiroshima](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroshima) and [Nagasaki](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagasaki) at the end of [World War II](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II). The bombs killed as many as 140,000 people in Hiroshima and 80,000 in Nagasaki by the end of 1945, [about half on the days of the bombings. Since then, thousands more have died from wounds or illness because of](#cite_note-rerf-deaths-55) [exposure to radiation](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_radiation_syndrome) released by the bombs. [In both cities, the overwhelming majority of the dead were civilians. In Germany, Austria, and Great Britain, conventional bombs were used. About 60,595 British](#cite_note-DOE-HIRO-56) [and 550,000 German](#cite_note-57) [civilians were killed by planes bombing cities.](#cite_note-58)
