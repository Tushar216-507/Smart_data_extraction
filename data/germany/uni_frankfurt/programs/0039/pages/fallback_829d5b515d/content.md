American Airlines, Inc. is a [major airline in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_airlines_of_the_United_States) headquartered in [Fort Worth, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Worth,_Texas), within the [Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas–Fort_Worth_metroplex). It is the [largest airline in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Largest_airlines_in_the_world) in terms of passengers carried and daily flights. [American, along with its regional subsidiaries and contractors operating under the brand name](#cite_note-8) [American Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Eagle_(airline_brand)), operates an extensive international and domestic network with almost 6,800 flights per day to nearly 350 destinations in 48 countries. [The airline is also a founding member of the](#cite_note-10) [Oneworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oneworld) alliance, one of the world's three major [airline alliances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airline_alliance).

American Airlines and American Eagle operate out of ten hubs, anchored by [Dallas Fort Worth International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Fort_Worth_International_Airport) (DFW), the airline's largest. [Charlotte Douglas International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Douglas_International_Airport) (CLT) follows as the second-largest hub, serving as a primary gateway for Southeast and transatlantic travel. [Miami International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_International_Airport) (MIA) and [Philadelphia International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_International_Airport) (PHL) share the third-place spot, each serving a distinct strategic role: Philadelphia acts as the premier connection point for flights between the western U.S. and Europe, while Miami remains the leading hub for travel to the Caribbean, South America, and Latin America. The airline serves more than 200 million passengers annually and averages more than 500,000 daily. As of 2024[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=American_Airlines&action=edit), the company employs 103,440 staff members.

The earliest predecessor company of American Airlines to commence operations was [Robertson Aircraft Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robertson_Aircraft_Corporation), which began [mail flights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airmail) on April 15, 1926.

American Airlines was established in 1930 through the union of more than eighty small airlines. [The two primary organizations from which American Airlines originated were](#cite_note-ap-3) [Robertson Aircraft Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robertson_Aircraft_Corporation) and [Colonial Air Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Air_Transport). The former was first created in Missouri in 1921, with both being merged in 1929 into holding company [The Aviation Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Aviation_Corporation). This, in turn, was made in 1930 into an operating company and rebranded as American Airways. In 1934, when new laws and attrition of mail contracts forced many airlines to reorganize, the corporation redid its routes into a connected system and was renamed American Airlines. The airline fully developed its international business between 1970 and 2000. It purchased [Trans World Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans_World_Airlines) in 2001.

American Airlines played a direct role in developing the [Douglas DC-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC-3), which grew out of a marathon telephone call from American Airlines CEO [C. R. Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._R._Smith) to [Douglas Aircraft Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Aircraft_Company) founder [Donald Wills Douglas Sr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Wills_Douglas_Sr.). During the call, Smith persuaded a reluctant Douglas to design a sleeper aircraft based on the [DC-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC-2) to replace American's [Curtiss Condor II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curtiss_T-32_Condor_II) biplanes. The existing DC-2 cabin was 66 inches (1.7m) wide, making it too narrow for side-by-side berths. Douglas agreed to proceed with development only after Smith informed him of American Airline's intention to purchase 20 aircraft.

The prototype [Douglas Sleeper Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Sleeper_Transport) (DST) first flew on December 17, 1935, which marked the 32nd anniversary of the [Wright Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright_Brothers)' flight at Kitty Hawk, North Carolina. Its cabin was 92in (2.3m) wide. A modified version configured with 21 seats instead of the DST's 14 to 16 sleeping berths was given the designation DC-3. There was no prototype for the DC-3; the first unit built followed seven DSTs off the production line and was delivered straight to American Airlines. [American Airlines inaugurated its passenger service on June 26, 1936, with simultaneous flights departing from](#cite_note-14) [Newark, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark,_New_Jersey), and [Chicago, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago,_Illinois).

American also had a direct role in the development of the [DC-10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC-10), which resulted from a specification from the airline to manufacturers in 1966 for a widebody aircraft smaller than the [Boeing 747](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747) but capable of flying similar long-range routes from airports with shorter runways. [McDonnell Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonnell_Douglas) responded with the DC-10 [trijet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trijet) shortly after the two companies' merger. [On February 19, 1968, the president of American Airlines, George A. Spater, and James S. McDonnell of McDonnell Douglas announced American's intention to acquire the DC-10. American Airlines ordered 25 DC-10s in its first order.](#cite_note-Waddington_p06-16) [The DC-10 made its first flight on August 29, 1970,](#cite_note-18) [and received its type certificate from the FAA on July 29, 1971.](#cite_note-19) [On August 5, 1971, the DC-10 entered commercial service with American Airlines on a round-trip flight between](#cite_note-20) [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles) and Chicago.

In 2011, due to a downturn in the airline industry, American Airlines' parent company, the [AMR Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMR_Corporation), filed for bankruptcy protection. In 2013, American Airlines merged with [US Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Airways) but kept the American Airlines name, as it was the more globally recognized brand. The combination of the two airlines created the largest airline in the United States, and ultimately the world.

In December 2023, the company was added to the [Dow Jones Sustainability World Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dow_Jones_Sustainability_Indices), and was included for a second consecutive year in December 2024.

[American Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Eagle_(airline_brand)) is the [brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand) for regional airline flights operated for American Airlines, encompassing flights by wholly owned affiliates [Envoy Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Envoy_Air), [PSA Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSA_Airlines), and [Piedmont Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont_Airlines), as well as third-party carriers [Republic Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Airways) and [SkyWest Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkyWest_Airlines). These regional carriers serve smaller markets, facilitating connections to American Airlines hubs, and supporting operations in mainline markets. All American Eagle carriers share similar logos, uniforms, and aircraft paint schemes as American Airlines’ mainline operations. In 2023, 46 million passengers flew on American Eagle regional flights, with about 45% connecting to or from mainline flights. These flights operate under capacity purchase agreements with both third-party and wholly owned regional carriers, controlling all aspects of marketing, scheduling, ticketing, pricing, and seat inventories. American Airlines pays fixed fees for operating specified aircraft and covering certain variable costs, such as fuel, landing fees, and insurance.

As of June2026[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=American_Airlines&action=edit), the [American Airlines fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines_fleet) consists of 1,030 mainline aircraft, making it the second [largest commercial airline fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airlines_by_fleet_size) in the world. The fleet consists of [Airbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus) and [Boeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing) narrow-body aircraft, and Boeing wide-body aircraft. American exclusively ordered Boeing aircraft throughout the 2000s until July 20, 2011, when American announced the largest combined aircraft order in history for 260 Airbus A320 family and 200 Boeing 737 aircraft. [As of October2025](#cite_note-41)[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=American_Airlines&action=edit), American has 301 Airbus and Boeing aircraft on order along with 20 orders and 40 options for [Boom Overture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boom_Overture) [supersonic aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersonic_aircraft). [The average age of the American mainline fleet is 14.2 years as of February 2026.](#cite_note-43)

As of 2024, American Airlines has four maintenance bases: [Tulsa International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulsa_International_Airport), [Pittsburgh International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_International_Airport), [Dallas Fort Worth International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Fort_Worth_International_Airport), and [Charlotte Douglas International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Douglas_International_Airport).

Flagship First is American's international and [transcontinental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcontinental_flight) [first class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_class_(aviation)) product. It is offered only on [Boeing 777-300ERs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_777-300ER) and select Airbus A321s, which American designates "A321T". The seats are fully lie-flat and offer direct aisle access with only one on each side of the aisle in each row. [As with the airline's other premium cabins, Flagship First offers wider food and beverage options, larger seats, and lounge access at certain airports.](#cite_note-46) [American offers domestic Flagship First service on transcontinental routes between](#cite_note-47) [New York–JFK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy_International_Airport) and [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_International_Airport), New York–JFK and [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_International_Airport), New York-JFK and [Santa Ana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wayne_Airport), [Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_International_Airport) and Los Angeles, and [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_International_Airport) and Los Angeles, as well as on the standard domestic route between New York-JFK and Boston. [The airline will debut new Flagship Suite premium seats and a revamped aircraft interior for its long-haul fleet with fresh deliveries of its Airbus A321XLR and Boeing 787-9 aircraft, beginning in 2024.](#cite_note-48)

Flagship Business is American's international and transcontinental [business class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_class) product. It is offered on all Boeing 777-200ERs, Boeing 777-300ERs, Boeing 787-8s, and Boeing 787-9s, as well as select Airbus A321s. All Flagship Business seats are fully lie-flat. [The amenities in Flagship Business include complimentary alcoholic/non-alcoholic beverages, multi-course meals, and lounge access.](#cite_note-SM-50)

First Class is the highest class of service on domestically configured aircraft. When such aircraft are used on international services this cabin is branded as business class. Seats range from 19–21 inches (48–53cm) in width and have 37–42 inches (94–107cm) of pitch. [Dining options include complimentary alcoholic and non-alcoholic beverages on all flights as well as standard economy snack offerings, enhanced snack basket selections on flights over 500 miles (800km), and meals on flights 900 miles (1,400km) or longer.](#cite_note-SM-50)

Premium Economy is American's [economy plus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premium_economy_class) product. It is offered on all widebody aircraft. The cabin debuted on the airline's Boeing 787-9s in late 2016 [and is also available on Boeing 777-200s and -300s, and Boeing 787-8s. Premium Economy seats are wider than seats in the main cabin (American's economy cabin) and provide more amenities: Premium Economy customers get two free checked bags, priority boarding, and enhanced food and drink service, including free alcohol. This product made American Airlines the first U.S. carrier to offer a four-cabin aircraft.](#cite_note-52)

Main Cabin (economy class) is American's economy product found on all mainline and regional aircraft in its fleet. Seats range from 17–18.5 inches (43–47cm) in width and have 30–32 inches (76–81cm) of pitch. American markets several rows within the main cabin immediately behind Main Cabin Extra as "Main Cabin Preferred", which requires an extra charge to select for those without status.

Main Cabin Extra seats are located in the front few rows and exit rows of the economy cabin on all aircraft and have additional pitch, complimentary alcoholic beverages and boarding one group ahead of the main cabin. [It is available on all of the mainline fleet and](#cite_note-53) [American Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Eagle_(airline_brand)) aircraft.

American also offers [Basic Economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Economy), the airline's lowest main cabin fare on many routes. Basic Economy consists of a Main Cabin ticket with numerous restrictions, including waiting until check-in for a seat assignment, no upgrades or refunds, and boarding in the last group. [Originally Basic Economy passengers could only carry a personal item. Later, American revised their Basic Economy policies to allow for a carry-on bag.](#cite_note-54)

In May 2017, American announced it would add more seats to some of its [Boeing 737 MAX 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737_MAX_8) jets and reduce overall legroom in the basic economy class. The last three rows were to lose 2 inches (5.1cm), going from the current 31 to 29 inches (79 to 74cm). The remainder of the main cabin was to have 30 inches (76cm) of legroom. This "Project Oasis" seating configuration has since been expanded to all 737 MAX 8s as well as standard [Boeing 737-800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737-800) and non-transcontinental Airbus A321 jets. New [Airbus A321neo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A321neo) jets have been delivered with the same configuration. This configuration has been considered unpopular with passengers, especially American's frequent flyers, as the new seats have less padding, less legroom, and no seatback entertainment.

AAdvantage is the [frequent flyer program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequent_flyer_program) for American Airlines. It was launched on May 1, 1981, and remains the largest frequent flyer program, with over 115 million members as of 2021. The most active members, based on the accumulation of Loyalty Points with American Airlines, are designated AAdvantage Gold, AAdvantage Platinum, AAdvantage Platinum Pro, and AAdvantage Executive Platinum elite members, with privileges such as separate check-in, priority upgrade, and standby processing, or free upgrades. AAdvantage status corresponds with Oneworld status levels allowing elites to receive reciprocal benefits from American's](#cite_note-58) [Oneworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oneworld) partner airlines.[[](#cite_note-59)[bettersourceneeded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Questionable_sources)]

AAdvantage co-branded credit cards are also available and offer other benefits. Currently cards are issued by CitiCards, a subsidiary of [Citigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citigroup) in the United States, [by several banks including](#cite_note-60) [Butterfield Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butterfield_Bank) and [Scotiabank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotiabank) in the Caribbean, [and by](#cite_note-62) [Banco Santander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banco_Santander) in Brazil. AAdvantage allows one-way redemption, starting at 7,500 miles.

The Admirals Club was created by AA president C.R. Smith as a marketing promotion shortly after he was made an honorary [Texas Ranger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Ranger_Division). Inspired by [honorary titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_of_honor) like the [Kentucky colonels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky_colonel), Smith designated high value passengers as "admirals" of the "Flagship fleet," which was AA's branding term for its aircraft at the time.[[](#cite_note-66)[bettersourceneeded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Questionable_sources)] The list of admirals included celebrities, politicians, VIPs, and frequent customers.

The first physical Admiral's Club opened shortly after the debut of LaGuardia Airport, then known as New York Municipal Airport. During construction, New York Mayor Fiorello LaGuardia had an upper-level lounge for press conferences and business meetings. When a reporter asked if that specific space would be leased along with the rest of the terminal, LaGuardia confirmed it would, and an AA executive immediately offered to rent it. The airline secured a liquor license and opened the space as the Admirals Club in 1939.

A second location opened at Washington National Airport. Because it was illegal to sell alcohol in Virginia at the time, the club provided refrigerators for members to store their own liquor. [For many years, membership in the Admirals Club and most other airline lounges was strictly by invitation. After a passenger sued for discrimination,](#cite_note-69) [the club switched to a paid membership program in 1974.](#cite_note-timeac-70)

Though affiliated with the Admirals Club and staffed by many of the same employees, the Flagship Lounge is a separate lounge designed explicitly for customers flying in first class and business class on international flights and transcontinental domestic flights.

American Airlines, Inc., is publicly traded through its parent company, [American Airlines Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines_Group) Inc., under NASDAQ: AAL [Nasdaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasdaq):[AAL](https://www.nasdaq.com/market-activity/stocks/aal), with a market capitalization of about $11 billion as of 2024. In September 2024, it was removed from the [S&P 500 index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S&P_500_Index) and placed into the [S&P MidCap 400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S&P_400).

American Eagle is a network of six regional carriers that operate under a codeshare and service agreement with American, operating flights to destinations in the United States, Canada, the Caribbean, and Mexico. Three of these carriers are independent, and three are subsidiaries of American Airlines Group: [Envoy Air Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Envoy_Air), [Piedmont Airlines, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont_Airlines), and [PSA Airlines Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSA_Airlines)

American Airlines is headquartered across several buildings in [Fort Worth, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Worth,_Texas) that it calls the "Robert L. Crandall Campus" in honor of former president and CEO [Robert Crandall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Crandall). The 1,700,000-square-foot (160,000m2), five-building office complex was designed by [Pelli Clarke Pelli Architects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelli_Clarke_Pelli_Architects). [The campus is located on 300 acres, adjacent to](#cite_note-89) [Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas/Fort_Worth_International_Airport), American's [fortress hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortress_hub).

Before it was headquartered in Texas, American Airlines was headquartered at 633 Third Avenue in the [Murray Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Hill,_Manhattan) area of [Midtown Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midtown_Manhattan), New York City. [In 1979, American moved its headquarters to a site at](#cite_note-92) [Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas/Fort_Worth_International_Airport), which affected up to 1,300 jobs. [Mayor of New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_New_York_City) [Ed Koch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Koch) described the move as a "betrayal" of New York City. [American moved to two leased office buildings in](#cite_note-93) [Grand Prairie, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prairie,_Texas). [On January 17, 1983, the airline finished moving into a $150million ($485,000,000 when adjusted for inflation), 550,000-square-foot (51,000m2) facility in Fort Worth; $147million (about $475,000,000 when adjusted for inflation) in Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport bonds financed the headquarters. The airline began leasing the facility from the airport, which owns the facility.](#cite_note-FortWorthHQFinished-94) [Following the merger of US Airways and American Airlines, the new company consolidated its corporate headquarters in Fort Worth, abandoning the US Airways headquarters in Phoenix, AZ.](#cite_note-FortWorthHQFinished-94)

As of 2015, American Airlines is the corporation with the most significant presence in Fort Worth.

In 2015, American announced it would build a new headquarters in Fort Worth. Groundbreaking began in the spring of 2016, and occupancy was completed in September 2019. [The airline plans to house 5,000 new workers in the building.](#cite_note-OwensWhitelyDouglas-96)

It will be located on a 41-acre (17ha) property adjacent to the airline's flight academy and conference and training center, west of [Texas State Highway 360](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_State_Highway_360), 2 miles (3.2km) [west from the current headquarters. The airline will lease 300 acres (120ha) from Dallas–Fort Worth International Airport, and this area will include the headquarters.](#cite_note-OwensWhitelyDouglas-96) [Construction of the new headquarters began after the demolition of the Sabre facility, previously on the site.](#cite_note-BrownSteveHQ-95)

The airline considered developing a new headquarters in [Irving, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving,_Texas), on the old [Texas Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Stadium) site, before deciding to keep the headquarters in Fort Worth.

In 1931, an American employee, Goodrich Murphy designed the AA logo as an entry in a logo contest. The eagle in the logo was copied from a Scottish hotel brochure. [The initial logo contained large red letters on either side of the eagle, enclosed in a double red and white circle against a blue background.](#cite_note-97) [This initial logo laid the foundation for the visual identity of the airline for 80 years.](#cite_note-:0-98)

The logo was modified for the first time in 1946, eliminating the red and white circles and making the eagle more prominent. The corporate name was standardized in a sans-serif font in italic capital letters.

The visual identity was again updated in 1962 with the addition of a lighter red as an accent color and a medium grey replacing the grayish dark blue. The logo was enclosed in a red circle, the eagle designed to be more figurative, and the double A's compacted.

In 1967 with the airline expanding its fleet and competing for new routes, a review of its visual identity was initiated. [in 1968, under the direction of](#cite_note-:0-98) [Massimo Vignelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massimo_Vignelli) (founding partner of Unimark) a new identity program was created. [According to Vignelli, the approach was "not styling but reductionist based on the notion of timelessness."](#cite_note-100) [A blue stylized bird (created by Henry Dreyfuss) was placed between Unimark's AA symbols, a design which endured until 2013.](#cite_note-:0-98)

Thirty years later, in 1997, American Airlines was able to make its logo Internet-compatible by buying the domain AA.com. AA is also American's two-letter [IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA) [airline designator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airline_codes).

On January 17, 2013, American launched a new rebranding and marketing campaign with FutureBrand dubbed "A New American". This included a new logo, which includes elements of the 1967 logo.

American Airlines faced difficulty obtaining copyright registration for their 2013 logo. On June 3, 2016, American Airlines sought to register it with the [United States Copyright Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Copyright_Office), [but in October of that year, the Copyright Office ruled that the logo was ineligible for copyright protection, as it did not pass the](#cite_note-US_Copyright_Office_Ruling-103) [threshold of originality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threshold_of_originality), and was thus in the [public domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_domain). [American requested that the Copyright Office reconsider. Still, on January 8, 2018, the Copyright Office affirmed its initial determination.](#cite_note-US_Copyright_Office_Ruling-103) [After American Airlines submitted additional materials, the Copyright Office reversed its decision on December 7, 2018, and ruled that the logo contained enough creativity to merit copyright protection.](#cite_note-104)

American's early [liveries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_livery) varied widely, but a standard livery was adopted in the 1930s, featuring an [eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle) painted on the fuselage. [The eagle became a symbol of the company and inspired the name of](#cite_note-106) [American Eagle Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Envoy_Air). Propeller aircraft featured an [international orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_orange) lightning bolt running down the length of the fuselage, which was replaced by a simpler orange stripe with the introduction of jets.

In the late 1960s, American commissioned designer [Massimo Vignelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massimo_Vignelli) to develop a new livery. The original design called for a red, white, and blue stripe on the fuselage and a simple "AA" logo, without an eagle, on the tail; instead, Vignelli created a highly stylized eagle, which remained the company's logo until January 16, 2013.

On January 17, 2013, American unveiled a new livery. [Before then, American had been the only major U.S. airline to leave most of its aircraft surfaces unpainted. This was because C. R. Smith would not say he liked painted aircraft and refused to use any liveries that involved painting the entire plane. Robert "Bob" Crandall later justified the distinctive natural metal finish by noting that less paint reduced the aircraft's weight, thus saving fuel costs.](#cite_note-109)

In January 2013, American launched a new rebranding and marketing campaign dubbed "The New American." In addition to a new logo, American Airlines introduced a new livery for its fleet. The airline calls the new livery and branding "a clean and modern update". [The current design features an abstract](#cite_note-111) [American flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_the_United_States) on the tail, along with a silver-painted fuselage, as a throw-back to the old livery. The new design was painted by [Leading Edge Aviation Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leading_Edge_Aviation_Services) in [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California). [Doug Parker, the incoming CEO, indicated that the new livery could be short-lived, stating that "[the] only reason this is an issue now is that they just did it right in the middle [of the merger], which kind of makes it confusing, so that allows us, actually, to decide if we are going to do something different because we have so many airplanes to paint".](#cite_note-112) [The current logo and livery have had mixed criticism, with Design Shack editor Joshua Johnson writing that they "boldly and proudly communicate the concepts of American pride and freedom wrapped into a shape that instantly makes you think about an airplane",](#cite_note-113) [and AskThePilot.com author Patrick Smith describing the logo as a linoleum knife poking through a shower curtain'.](#cite_note-designshackliverypraise-114) [Later in January 2013,](#cite_note-patricksmithcriticism-115) [Bloomberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomberg_L.P.) asked the designer of the 1968 American Airlines logo ([Massimo Vignelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massimo_Vignelli)) on his opinion over the rebranding. Vignelli replied "now they have something other than [Helvetica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helvetica) that's not as good or as powerful", adding that "it has no sense of permanence".

In the end, American let their employees decide the new livery's fate. On an internal website for employees, American posted two options, one the new livery and one a modified version of the old livery. All of the [American Airlines Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines_Group) employees (including US Airways and other affiliates) were able to vote. [American ultimately decided to keep the new look. Parker announced that American would keep a US Airways and America West heritage aircraft in the fleet, with plans to add a heritage TWA aircraft and a heritage American plane with the old livery.](#cite_note-117) [As of September 2019, American has heritage aircraft for](#cite_note-118) [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont_Airlines_(1948–1989)), [PSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Southwest_Airlines), [America West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America_West_Airlines), [US Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Airways), [Reno Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reno_Air), [TWA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans_World_Airlines), [Allegheny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegheny_Airlines), and [AirCal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_California) in their fleet. [They also have two AA branded heritage](#cite_note-120) [737-800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/737-800) aircraft, an AstroJet N905NN, [and the polished aluminum livery used from 1967 to 2013, N921NN.](#cite_note-121)

American, both before and after the merger with US Airways, has consistently performed poorly in rankings. The Wall Street Journal's annual airline rankings have ranked American as the worst or second-worst U.S. carrier for ten of the past twelve years, and in the bottom three of U.S. airlines for at least the past twelve years. The airline has persistently performed poorly in the areas of losing checked luggage and bumping passengers due to oversold flights.

The main representatives of key groups of employees are:

- The [Allied Pilots Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_Pilots_Association)is an in-house union which represents the nearly 15,000 American Airlines pilots; it was created in 1963 after the pilots left the[Air Line Pilots Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Line_Pilots_Association)(ALPA).[However the majority of American Eagle pilots are ALPA members.](#cite_note-132)
- The [Association of Professional Flight Attendants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Professional_Flight_Attendants)represents American Airlines flight attendants, including former USAirways flight attendants.
- Flight attendants at wholly owned regional carriers (Envoy, Piedmont, and PSA) are all represented by [Association of Flight Attendants – Communications Workers of America](http://www.afacwa.org/)(AFA-CWA). US Airways flight attendants were active members of AFA-CWA before the merger, and they are honorary lifetime members. AFA-CWA is the largest flight attendant union in the industry.
- The [Transport Workers Union-International Association of Machinists alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_Workers_Union_of_America)(TWU-IAM) represents the majority of American Airlines employed fleet service agents, mechanics, and other ground workers.
- American's customer service and gate employees belong to the [Communications Workers of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communications_Workers_of_America)/[International Brotherhood of Teamsters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Brotherhood_of_Teamsters)Passenger Service Association.
- PAFCA-AAL represents the nearly 550 FAA-certificated [Aircraft Dispatchers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_dispatcher)and Operations Specialists at American Airlines. This specialized group, many of whom are licensed pilots, former Air Traffic Control personnel, and military airmen share equal responsibility with the Pilot-in-Command for the safe conduct of each the flight.

In September 2024, the Association of Professional Flight Attendants ratified a five-year contract that included immediate wage increases of up to 20.5% and $514 million in retroactive pay, the first contract to include boarding pay for a unionized cabin crew in the United States. [In January 2025, the TWU-IAM alliance reached a two-year contract extension covering approximately 34,000 ground workers, with raises of 18 to 26 percent.](#cite_note-139) [On February 9, 2026, the APFA board of directors issued a unanimous vote of no confidence in CEO Robert Isom, the first such action in the union's history.](#cite_note-140)

In 1942, American Airlines established [Sky Chefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Chefs), a wholly owned subsidiary, as a catering company to serve their fleet. [In 1986, Sky Chefs was sold to](#cite_note-chefsolutions-142) [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto)-based [Onex Capital Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onex_Capital_Corporation) for $170 million. [Sky Chefs became a subsidiary of Onex Food Services Inc.](#cite_note-143) [Since 2001, it has been fully owned by the](#cite_note-jones-144) [LSG Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LSG_Group).

In the late-1960s, American Airlines established the Flagship Hotels chain as a subsidiary of Sky Chefs. On July 21, 1972, American Airlines leased four hotels from the [Loews Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loews_Corporation), three of them branded as Americana Hotels, for a period of thirty years. American merged the hotels with their Flagship Hotels, and rebranded the entire chain as Americana Hotels. [In 1980, American Airlines sold Americana Hotels to](#cite_note-147) [Bass Brothers Enterprises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bass) of Fort Worth, Texas.

Between October 1993 and July 1998, American Airlines received multiple citations for using high-sulfur fuel in motor vehicles at 10 major U.S. airports, a violation of the [Clean Air Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clean_Air_Act_(United_States)).

In 1981, to generate revenue during a period of financial losses, American Airlines offered an unlimited lifetime travel pass for an initial cost of $250,000. [This pass allowed holders to fly worldwide without additional fares, and 28 passes were sold. However, the airline later determined that the program was costing them money, with some pass holders costing up to $1 million each due to high flight volumes, such as flying interstate for lunch or flying to London multiple times a month. AA raised the cost of the lifetime pass to $3 million before discontinuing it in 2003. The airline then used litigation to terminate two of the lifetime passes, citing fraudulent activity.](#cite_note-151)

In 1988, on American Airlines Flight 132's approach into Nashville, flight attendants notified the cockpit that there was smoke in the cabin. The flight crew in the cockpit ignored the warning, as on a prior flight, a [fume event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fume_event) had occurred due to a problem with the [auxiliary power unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auxiliary_power_unit). However, the smoke on Flight 132 was caused by improperly packaged hazardous materials. According to the [NTSB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Transportation_Safety_Board) inquiry, the cockpit crew persistently refused to acknowledge that there was a serious threat to the aircraft or the passengers, even after they were told that the floor was becoming soft and passengers had to be reseated. As a result, the aircraft was not evacuated immediately on landing, exposing the crew and passengers to the threat of smoke and fire longer than necessary.

On April 11, 2007, toxic smoke and oil fumes leaked into the aircraft cabin as American Airlines Flight 843 taxied to the gate. A flight attendant who was present in the cabin subsequently filed a lawsuit against Boeing, stating that she was diagnosed with neurotoxic disorder due to her exposure to the fumes, which caused her to experience memory loss, tremors, and severe headaches. She settled with the company in 2011.

In 2009, Mike Holland, deputy chairman for radiation and environmental issues at the [Allied Pilots Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_Pilots_Association) and an American Airlines pilot, said that the pilot union had started alerting pilots of the danger of contaminated [bleed air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleed_air), including contacting crew members that the union thinks were exposed to contamination based on maintenance records and pilot logs.

In a January 2017 incident on American Airlines Flight 1896, seven flight attendants were hospitalized after a strange odor was detected in the cabin. The Airbus A330 involved subsequently underwent a "thorough maintenance inspection", having been involved in three fume events in three months.

In August 2018, American Airlines flight attendants picketed in front of the Fort Worth company headquarters over a change in sick day policy, complaining that exposure to ill passengers, toxic uniforms, toxic cabin air, radiation exposure, and other issues were causing them to be sick.

In January 2019, two pilots and three flight attendants on Flight 1897 from Philadelphia to Fort Lauderdale were hospitalized following complaints of a strange odor.

On October 24, 2017, the [NAACP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAACP) issued a travel advisory for American Airlines urging [African Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Americans) to "exercise caution" when traveling with the airline. The NAACP issued the advisory after four incidents. In one incident, a black woman was moved from first class to coach while her white traveling companion was allowed to remain in first class. In another incident, a black man was forced to give up his seats after being confronted by two unruly white passengers. [According to the NAACP, while they did receive complaints on other airlines, most of their complaints in the year before their advisory were on American Airlines.](#cite_note-PBS20171025-163) [In July 2018, the NAACP lifted their travel advisory saying that American has made improvements to mitigate discrimination and unsafe treatment of African Americans.](#cite_note-164)

In October 2024, the [United States Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Transportation) imposed a $50 million penalty on American Airlines for violations of federal disability protection laws between 2019 and 2023, the largest such penalty ever assessed against an airline. The violations included unsafe physical assistance to wheelchair users and mishandling of thousands of wheelchairs. Of the $50 million, half was payable to the U.S. Treasury and half credited toward remediation investments including a wheelchair tracking system and additional staff at hub airports.

In 1999, American Airlines sued [Allied Pilots Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_Pilots_Association) for $45.5 million over damages due to [strike action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strike_action), one of the largest fines ever levied against a [trade union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_union).

In January 2020, the Allied Pilots Association filed a lawsuit to stop flights between the United States and [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China) over concerns about [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19). [The Association of Professional Flight Attendants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Professional_Flight_Attendants) supported the court action. At the time, COVID-19 was mostly contained to China and had not reached [pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandemic) status yet. [Because of this action, American Airlines suspended their flights the following day.](#cite_note-168)

As of January 2025, American Airlines has had almost 60 aircraft [hull losses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hull_loss), beginning with the crash of a [Ford 5-AT-C Trimotor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Trimotor) in August 1931. [Of the hull losses, most were propeller-driven aircraft, including three](#cite_note-List_of_American_Airlines_accidents_and_incidents_ASNAA-171) [Lockheed L-188 Electra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_L-188_Electra) aircraft (of which one, the crash in 1959 of [Flight 320](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines_Flight_320), resulted in fatalities). [The two accidents with the highest fatalities in both the airline's and U.S. aviation history were](#cite_note-List_of_American_Airlines_accidents_and_incidents_ASNAA-171) [Flight 191](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines_Flight_191) in 1979 and [Flight 587](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines_Flight_587) in 2001.

Out of the 17 hijackings of American Airlines flights, two aircraft were hijacked and destroyed in the [September 11 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11_attacks): [Flight 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines_Flight_11) crashed into the [North Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_World_Trade_Center_(1973–2001)) of the [World Trade Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Center_(1973–2001)) and [Flight 77](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines_Flight_77) crashed into [the Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon). [Flight 11, which is responsible for an estimated 1,700 deaths, is the deadliest air crash in the history of aviation.](#cite_note-173)

Two training flight accidents have occurred in which the crew was killed and six that resulted in no fatalities. [Another four jet aircraft have been written off due to incidents while they were parked between flights or while undergoing maintenance.](#cite_note-List_of_American_Airlines_accidents_and_incidents_ASNAA-171)

American Airlines reported total [CO2e emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_footprint) (direct and indirect) for the twelve months ending December 31, 2020, at 20,092 Kt (-21,347 /-51.5% y-o-y). [The company aims to achieve net zero carbon emissions by 2050.](#cite_note-AA_emissions_2020Q4-174) [In November 2023, American Airlines purchased the first](#cite_note-175) [carbon credit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_offsets_and_credits) contract (for 10,000 [metric tons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonne) of CO2 [sequestered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_sequestration) at $100 per ton) from [Graphyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphyte), a [carbon removal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide_removal) [startup company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Startup_company) invested in by [Breakthrough Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_Energy) that compresses [sawdust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sawdust), [tree bark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bark_(botany)), [rice hulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_hulls), [plant stalks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_stem), and other [agricultural waste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_waste) into [biomass bricks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomass_(energy)) wrapped in a [polymer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymer) barrier to prevent decomposition that are stored underground.
