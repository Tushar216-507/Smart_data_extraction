How Long Should I Practice Keyboarding Daily?

As touch typing is a cumulative motor skill, regular typing practice is essential for improvement. Ideally, you should aim to practice daily or at least every other day. Consistent practice helps reinforce the neural pathways associated with touch typing, making it easier to recall key locations without looking at the keyboard. Short, focused practice sessions are often more effective than infrequent, longer ones. Aim for periods ranging from 15 minutes to about 45 minutes.

Everyone's learning curve is different, so it's important to find a practice routine that works best for you. Some may find shorter, more frequent sessions beneficial, while others may prefer longer sessions less frequently. The key is to maintain consistency and keep challenging yourself with new exercises and drills. Incorporating variety into your practice — such as different types of exercises or story texts — can help maintain interest and improve various aspects of your keyboarding skills.

Is Typing Faster Than Writing?

Whether keyboarding is faster than writing by hand depends on individual proficiency, the nature of the task, and personal preference. For most people, touch typing tends to be faster than handwriting. It allows for quick edits, easy sharing, and efficient data input. Typing also enables you to keep pace with the speed of thought more effectively, which can be particularly beneficial during brainstorming or note-taking sessions.

However, some individuals may find that writing by hand offers advantages, such as better retention of information or the ability to draw diagrams and annotations easily. Ultimately, the choice between keyboarding and writing depends on the context and the individual's comfort and proficiency with each method. Both skills have their place, and being proficient in both can provide flexibility in different situations.
