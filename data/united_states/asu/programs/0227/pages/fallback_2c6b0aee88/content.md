Asia ([//](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)[ⓘ](/wiki/File:En-us-Asia.ogg) [AY-zhə](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key), [UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English) also [//](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English) [AY-shə](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is the largest [continent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continent) [in the world by both land area and population.](#cite_note-Boudreau-12) [It covers an area of more than 44 million square kilometres,](#cite_note-Boudreau-12) [about 30% of Earth's total land area and 8% of Earth's total surface area. The continent, which has long been home to the majority of the](#cite_note-13) [human population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_population), [was the site of many of the](#cite_note-14) [first civilisations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_civilisations). Its 4.7billion people constitute roughly 60% of the world's population.

Asia shares the [landmass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landmass) of [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia) with [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe), and of [Afro-Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Eurasia) with both Europe and [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa). In general terms, it is bounded on the east by the [Pacific Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Ocean), on the south by the [Indian Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean), and on the north by the [Arctic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Ocean). The border of Asia with Europe is a [historical and cultural construct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_constructionism), as there is no clear physical and geographical separation between them. A commonly accepted division places Asia to the east of the [Suez Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Canal) separating it from Africa; and to the east of the [Turkish straits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_straits), the [Ural Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_Mountains) and [Ural River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_River), and to the south of the [Caucasus Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus_Mountains) and the [Caspian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_Sea) and [Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea) seas, separating it from Europe.

Since the concept of Asia derives from the term for the eastern region from a European perspective, Asia is the remaining vast area of Eurasia minus Europe. Therefore, Asia is a region where various independent cultures coexist rather than sharing a single culture, and its boundary with Europe is somewhat arbitrary and has moved since its first conception in [classical antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_antiquity). The division of Eurasia into two continents reflects [East–West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East–West_dichotomy) cultural differences, some of which vary on a spectrum.

[China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China) and [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India) traded places as the [largest economies in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_regions_by_past_GDP_(PPP)) from 1 to 1800 CE. China was a major economic power for much of recorded history, with the highest [GDP per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP_per_capita) until 1500. [The](#cite_note-18) [Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road) became the main east–west trading route in the Asian hinterlands while the [Straits of Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straits_of_Malacca) stood as a major sea route. Asia has exhibited [economic dynamism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_dynamism) as well as robust population growth during the 20th century, but overall population growth has since fallen. [Asia was the birthplace of most of the world's mainstream religions including](#cite_note-19) [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam), [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism), [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity), [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism), [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism), and many other religions.

Asia varies greatly across and within [its regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Asia) with regard to [ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups), cultures, environments, economics, historical ties, and government systems. It also has a mix of many different climates ranging from the equatorial south via the hot deserts in parts of [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia), [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia), [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia), and [East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia) and tropical jungles in [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia), temperate areas in the east and the continental centre to vast subarctic and polar areas in [North Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Asia).

## Etymology

The term "Asia" is believed to originate in the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) toponym [Assuwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assuwa) ([Hittite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittite_language): 𒀸𒋗𒉿, romanized:aš-šu-wa) which originally referred only to a portion of northwestern [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia). The term appears in [Hittite records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittite_texts) recounting how a confederation of Assuwan states including [Troy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy) unsuccessfully rebelled against the Hittite king [Tudhaliya I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudhaliya_I) around 1400BCE. [Roughly contemporary](#cite_note-22) [Linear B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_B) documents contain the term aswia ([Mycenaean Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycenaean_Greek_language): 𐀀𐀯𐀹𐀊, romanized:a-si-wi-ja), seemingly in reference to captives from the same area.

Herodotus used the term in reference to [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia) and the territory of the [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire), in contrast to Greece and Egypt. He reports that Greeks assumed that Asia was named after the wife of [Prometheus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prometheus), but that [Lydians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia) say it was named after Asies, son of Cotys, who passed the name on to a tribe at [Sardis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardis). [In](#cite_note-25) [Greek mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mythology), "Asia" (Ἀσία or Ἀσίη) was the name of a "[Nymph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nymph) or [Titan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titan_(mythology)) goddess of Lydia". [The](#cite_note-26) [Iliad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iliad) (attributed by the ancient Greeks to [Homer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer)) mentions two Phrygians in the [Trojan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trojan_War) named [Asios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asius_(mythology)) (literally 'Asian'); [and also a marsh or lowland containing a marsh in Lydia as ασιος.](#cite_note-27)

The term was later adopted by the [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_people), who used it in reference to the [province of Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Asia), located in western Anatolia. [One of the first writers to use Asia as a name of the whole continent was](#cite_note-29) [Pliny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder).

## Definition

### Asia–Europe boundary

The threefold division of the [Old World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_World) into Africa, Asia, and Europe has been in use since the 6th centuryBCE, due to [Greek geographers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_geographers) such as [Anaximander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaximander) and [Hecataeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hecataeus_of_Miletus). [Anaximander placed the boundary between Asia and Europe along the](#cite_note-31) [Phasis River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phasis_River) (now the Rioni) in [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_(country)) of Caucasus (from its mouth by [Poti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poti) on the [Black Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea) coast, through the [Surami Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surami_Pass) and along the [Kura River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kura_(Caspian_Sea)) to the Caspian Sea), a convention still followed by [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus) in the 5th centuryBCE. [During the](#cite_note-32) [Hellenistic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period), [this convention was revised, and the boundary between Europe and Asia was now considered to be the](#cite_note-33) [Tanais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_River_(Russia)) (the modern Don River). This is the convention used by Roman era authors such as [Posidonius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posidonius),[Strabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strabo) [and](#cite_note-35) [Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy).

The border between Asia and Europe was later redefined by European academics. [In 1730, five years after the death of](#cite_note-Lineback-2013-37) [Peter the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great), [Philip Johan von Strahlenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Johan_von_Strahlenberg) published in Sweden a new atlas proposing the Ural Mountains as the border of Asia. [Vasily Tatishchev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasily_Tatishchev) announced that he had proposed the idea to von Strahlenberg. The latter had suggested the [Emba River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emba_River) as the southern part of this boundary. Over the next century various proposals were made until the [Ural River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_River) prevailed in the mid-19th century. The border had been moved perforce from the Black Sea (the Romans' definition) to the Caspian Sea into which the Emba and Ural rivers project. [Between the Black Sea and the Caspian, the border is often placed along the crest of the](#cite_note-38) [Caucasus Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus_Mountains), although it is sometimes placed further north, [on the](#cite_note-Lineback-2013-37) [Kuma–Manych Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuma–Manych_Depression).

### Asia–Africa boundary

The boundary between Asia and Africa is the [Suez Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Canal), the [Gulf of Suez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Suez), the [Red Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea), and the [Bab-el-Mandeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bab-el-Mandeb). [This makes](#cite_note-39) [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt) a [transcontinental country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcontinental_country), with the [Sinai Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_Peninsula) in Asia and the remainder of the country in Africa.

### Asia–Oceania boundary

The border between Asia and [Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania) is usually placed somewhere in the [Indonesian Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Archipelago), specifically in [Eastern Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Indonesia). The [Wallace Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_Line) separates the Asian and [Wallacea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallacea) biogeographical realms, a transition zone of deep water straits between the Asian and Australian continental shelves. [Weber's Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weber's_Line) split the region in two with regard to the balance of fauna between Asian origin or Australo-Papuan origin. [Wallacea's eastern boundary with](#cite_note-Simpson-1977-40) [Sahul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_(continent)) is represented by the [Lydekker's Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Lydekker). [The](#cite_note-42) [Maluku Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maluku_Islands) (except the [Aru Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aru_Islands_Regency)) are often considered to lie on the border of southeast Asia, with the Aru Islands and [Western New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_New_Guinea), to the east of the Lydekker's Line, being wholly part of Oceania, as both lie on the Australian continental plate. [Culturally, the Wallacea region denoted the transition between](#cite_note-Simpson-1977-40) [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_people) and [Melanesian people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanesian_people), with varying degrees of intermixing between the two. In general, the further west and coastal a region is, the stronger the Austronesian influences, and the further east and inland a region is, the stronger the Melanesian influences. [The terms Southeast Asia and Oceania, devised in the 19th century, have had several vastly different geographic meanings since their inception. The chief factor in determining which islands of the Indonesian Archipelago are Asian has been the location of the colonial possessions of the various empires there (not all European). Lewis and Wigen assert, "The narrowing of 'Southeast Asia' to its present boundaries was thus a gradual process."](#cite_note-43)

### Asia–North America boundary

The [Bering Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bering_Strait) and [Bering Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bering_Sea) separate the landmasses of Asia and [North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_America), as well as forming the international boundary between Russia and the United States. This [national](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_boundary) and continental boundary separates the [Diomede Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diomede_Islands) in the Bering Strait, with Big Diomede in [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) and Little Diomede in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States). The [Aleutian Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleutian_Islands) are an island chain extending westward from the [Alaskan Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaskan_Peninsula) toward Russia's [Komandorski Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komandorski_Islands) and [Kamchatka Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamchatka_Peninsula). Most of them are always associated with North America, except for the westernmost [Near Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_Islands) group, which is on Asia's continental shelf beyond the [North Aleutians Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Aleutians_Basin) and on rare occasions could be associated with Asia, which could then allow the United States to be considered a transcontinental state. The Aleutian Islands are sometimes associated with Oceania, owing to their status as remote Pacific islands, and their proximity to the Pacific Plate. [This is extremely rare however, due to their non-tropical biogeography, as well as their](#cite_note-47) [inhabitants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleut), who have historically been related to the [Indigenous peoples of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas).

[St. Lawrence Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Lawrence_Island) in the northern Bering Sea belongs to the US state of [Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska) and may be associated with either continent but is almost always considered part of North America, as with the [Rat Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rat_Islands) in the Aleutian chain. At their nearest points, Alaska and Russia are separated by only 4 kilometres (2.5 miles).

### Ongoing definition

Geographical Asia is a cultural artifact of European conceptions of the world, beginning with the [Ancient Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greeks), being imposed onto other cultures, an imprecise concept causing endemic contention about what it means. Asia does not exactly correspond to the cultural borders of its various types of constituents.

From the time of Herodotus, a minority of geographers have rejected the three-continent system (Europe, Africa, Asia) on the grounds that there is no substantial physical separation between them. [For example, Sir](#cite_note-51) [Barry Cunliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Cunliffe), the emeritus professor of European archeology at Oxford, argues that Europe has been geographically and culturally merely "the western excrescence of the continent of Asia".

Geographically, Asia is the major eastern constituent of the continent of [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia) with Europe being a northwestern [peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsula) of the landmass. Asia, Europe and Africa make up a single continuous landmass—[Afro-Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Eurasia)—and share a common [continental shelf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_shelf). Almost all of Europe and a major part of Asia sit atop the [Eurasian Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Plate), adjoined on the south by the [Arabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Plate) and [Indian Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Plate) and with the easternmost part of Siberia (east of the [Chersky Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chersky_Range)) on the [North American Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Plate).

## History

### Prehistory

About 1.8 million years ago, [Homo erectus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_erectus) left the African continent. [This species is believed to have lived in East and Southeast Asia from 1.8 million to 110,000 years ago.](#cite_note-53)

Researchers believe that the modern human, or [Homo sapiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_sapiens), migrated about 60,000 years ago to [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia) along the Indian Ocean. [Modern humans](#cite_note-55) [interbred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interbreeding_between_archaic_and_modern_humans) with an [archaic human](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaic_human) species called [Denisovans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denisovans) in Southeast Asia. [Before the arrival of modern humans,](#cite_note-56) [Flores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flores) was occupied by [Homo floresiensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_floresiensis), a small archaic human.

Ancestors of [East Eurasians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_East_Eurasians) split from Ancient West Eurasians about 46,000 years ago, migrating out of the hub in the [Iranian Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Plateau). [Evidence from full genomic studies suggests that the first people in the Americas diverged from](#cite_note-:0-58) [Ancient East Asians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_history_of_East_Asians) about 36,000 years ago and expanded northwards into Siberia, where they encountered and interacted with a different Paleolithic Siberian population (known as [Ancient North Eurasians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_North_Eurasian)), giving rise to both [Paleosiberian peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleosiberian_peoples) and [Ancient Native Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Beringian).

Modern [South Asians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics_and_archaeogenetics_of_South_Asia) are descendants of a combination of Western Eurasian ancestries (notably [Neolithic Iranian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_hunter-gatherers) and [Western Steppe Herder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Steppe_Herders) components) with a proposed indigenous [East Eurasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_East_Eurasians) component (termed Ancient Ancestral South Indians) closest to the non–West Eurasian part extracted from South Asian samples; distantly related to the [Andamanese peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andamanese_people), East Asians and [Aboriginal Australians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aboriginal_Australians).

### Ancient era

The history of Asia can be seen as the distinct histories of several peripheral coastal regions: [East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia), [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia), [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia), [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia), and [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia). The coastal periphery was home to some of the world's earliest known civilisations, each of them developing around fertile river valleys. The civilisations in [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia), the [Indus Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley) and the [Yellow River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River) shared many similarities. These civilisations may well have exchanged technologies and ideas such as [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics) and the [wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheel). Other innovations, such as writing, seem to have been developed individually in each area. Cities, states and empires developed in these lowlands.

The central steppe region had long been inhabited by horse-mounted nomads who could reach all areas of Asia from the [steppes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steppe). The earliest postulated [expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_migrations) out of the steppe is that of the [Indo-Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Europeans), who spread their languages into West Asia, South Asia, and the borders of China, [where the](#cite_note-Narasimhan_2019-60) [Tocharians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tocharians) resided. The northernmost part of Asia, including much of [Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberia), was largely inaccessible to the steppe nomads, owing to the dense forests, climate and [tundra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tundra). These areas remained very sparsely populated.

The center and the peripheries were mostly kept separated by mountains and deserts. The [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus) and [Himalaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalaya) mountains and the [Karakum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karakum_Desert) and [Gobi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gobi) deserts formed barriers that the [steppe horsemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_nomads) could cross only with difficulty. While the urban city dwellers were more advanced technologically and socially, in many cases they could do little in a military aspect to defend against the [mounted hordes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomadic_empire) of the steppe. However, the lowlands did not have enough open grasslands to support a large equestrian force; for this and other reasons, the nomads who conquered states in China, India, and the Middle East often found themselves adapting to the local, more affluent societies.

### Medieval era

The Islamic [Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate)'s defeats of the [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine) and Persian empires led to West Asia and southern parts of [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia) and western parts of South Asia under its control during [its conquests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Muslim_conquests) of the 7th century; Islam also [spread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spread_of_Islam) over centuries to the southern regions of India and Southeast Asia through trade along the [Maritime Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Silk_Road). [The](#cite_note-63) [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire) conquered a large part of Asia in the 13th century, an area extending from China to Europe. Before the Mongol invasion, [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty) reportedly had approximately 120 million citizens; the 1300 census which followed the invasion reported roughly 60 million people.

The [Black Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Death), one of the most devastating [pandemics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandemic) in human history, is thought to have originated in the arid plains of central Asia, where it then travelled along the [Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road).

### Modern era

European involvement in Asia became more significant from the [Age of Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Discovery) onward, with Iberian-sponsored sailors such as [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus) and [Vasco da Gama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_da_Gama) paving the way for new routes from [Atlantic Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Europe) to [Pacific Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Asia) and the Indian Ocean respectively in the late 15th century. [The](#cite_note-66) [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) also began to expand into northwestern Asia from the 17th century, and would eventually take control of all of Siberia and most of Central Asia by the end of the 19th century.

Among non-European empires, the [Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire) controlled Anatolia, most of the Middle East, North Africa and the Balkans from the mid 16th century onward, while in the 17th century, the [Manchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu) conquered China and established the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty). The Islamic [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) (preceded by the [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate) of the 13th to early 16th century) [and the Hindu](#cite_note-67) [Maratha Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire) controlled much of India in the 16th and 18th centuries respectively.

[Western imperialism in Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_imperialism_in_Asia) from the 18th to 20th centuries coincided with the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution) in the West and the dethroning of India and China as the world's foremost economies. [The](#cite_note-69) [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) first became dominant in South Asia, with most of the region being [conquered by British traders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_rule_in_India) in the late 18th and early 19th centuries before falling under [direct British rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) after a failed [1857 revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1857_revolt); the 1869 completion of the [Suez Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Canal), which increased British access to India, went on to [further European influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Imperialism) over Africa and Asia. [Around this time, Western powers started to dominate China in what later became known as the](#cite_note-70) [century of humiliation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Century_of_humiliation), with the British-supported [opium trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_opium_in_China#Growth_of_the_opium_trade) and later [Opium Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium_Wars) resulting in China being forced into an unprecedented situation of importing more than it exported.

Foreign domination of China was furthered by the [Japanese colonial empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_colonial_empire), which controlled some of East Asia and briefly much of Southeast Asia (which had earlier been [taken over](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonisation_of_Southeast_Asia) by the British, Dutch and French in the late 19th century),[New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Guinea) and the [Pacific islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_islands); Japan's domination was enabled by its rapid rise that had taken place during the [Meiji era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_era) of the late 19th century, in which it applied industrial knowledge learned from the West and thus overtook the rest of Asia. [One significant influence on Japan had been the United States, which had begun projecting influence across the Pacific after its early-to-mid-19th century](#cite_note-75) [westward expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westward_expansion). [The](#cite_note-76) [breakup of the Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decline_of_the_Ottoman_Empire) in the early 20th century led to the Middle East also being [contested and partitioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Middle_East#Modern_Middle_East) by the British and French.

### Contemporary era

With the end of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) in 1945 and the wartime ruination of Europe and imperial Japan, many countries in Asia were able to rapidly [free themselves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonisation_of_Asia) of colonial rule. [The](#cite_note-78) [independence of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_India) came along with the [carving out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) of a separate nation for the majority of [South Asian Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_Muslims), which in 1971 [further split](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War) into Pakistan and [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh); [The](#cite_note-79) [Cold War in Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War_in_Asia) strained relations between India and Pakistan and affected Asia more generally. [The](#cite_note-80) [end of the Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End_of_the_Cold_War) and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) by 1991 saw the independence of the five modern Central Asian countries.

Some Arab countries took economic advantage of massive oil deposits that were discovered in their territory, becoming globally influential, [though stability in the Middle East has been affected since 1948 by the](#cite_note-82) [Arab–Israeli conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab–Israeli_conflict) and American-led [interventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_foreign_policy_in_the_Middle_East). [East Asian nations (along with Singapore in Southeast Asia) became economically prosperous with high-growth "](#cite_note-84)[tiger economies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger_economies)"; [China, having undergone the](#cite_note-85) [reform and opening up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_and_opening_up) under [Deng Xiaoping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deng_Xiaoping), [regained its place among the top two economies of the world by the 21st century.](#cite_note-86) [India has also grown significantly because of](#cite_note-87) [economic liberalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalisation_in_India) that started in the 1990s, [with extreme poverty now below 20%;](#cite_note-88) [India and China's rise has coincided with growing tensions between the two, with the](#cite_note-89) [Indo-Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pacific) now an actively contested area between China and counterbalancing forces.

## Geography

Asia is the largest continent on Earth. It covers 9% of the Earth's total surface area (or 30% of its land area), and has the longest coastline, at 62,800 kilometres (39,022mi). Asia is generally defined as comprising the eastern four-fifths of Eurasia. It is located to the east of the [Suez Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Canal) and the [Ural Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_Mountains), and south of the [Caucasus Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus_Mountains) (or the [Kuma–Manych Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuma–Manych_Depression)) and the [Caspian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_Sea) and [Black Seas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea). [It is bounded on the east by the](#cite_note-93) [Pacific Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Ocean), on the south by the Indian Ocean and on the north by the Arctic Ocean. Asia is subdivided into 49 countries, five of them ([Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_(country)), [Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan), [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia), [Kazakhstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakhstan) and [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey)) are [transcontinental countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcontinental_countries) lying partly in Europe.

The [Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze) in China is the longest river in the continent. The Himalayas between Nepal and China is the tallest mountain range in the world. [Tropical rainforests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest) stretch across much of southern Asia and coniferous and [deciduous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deciduous) forests lie farther north. Between 1990 and 2000 the area of [primary forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_forest) in Asia decreased by 1.06 million hectares per year, between 2000 and 2015 it decreased by 280 000 ha per year.

### Climate

Asia has extremely diverse climate features. Climates range from [Arctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic) and [subarctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subarctic) in Siberia to tropical in southern India and Southeast Asia. It is moist across southeast sections, and dry across much of the interior. Some of the largest daily temperature ranges on Earth occur in western sections of Asia. The [monsoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsoon) circulation dominates across southern and eastern sections, due to the presence of the Himalayas forcing the formation of a thermal low which draws in moisture during the summer. Southwestern sections of the continent are hot. Siberia is one of the coldest places in the [Northern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Hemisphere), and can act as a source of arctic air masses for North America. The most active place on Earth for [tropical cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_cyclone) activity lies northeast of the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines) and south of Japan.

#### Climate change

[Climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change) is particularly important in Asia, as the continent accounts for the majority of the human population. Warming since the 20th century is increasing the threat of [heatwaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heatwave) across the entire continent.[:1459 Heatwaves lead to increased mortality, and the demand for](#cite_note-Climate_change_in_Asia_AR6_WGII_Chapter10-100) [air conditioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_conditioning) is rapidly accelerating as the result. By 2080, around 1 billion people in the cities of [South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia) and [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia) are expected to experience around a month of extreme heat every year.[:1460 The](#cite_note-Climate_change_in_Asia_AR6_WGII_Chapter10-100) [impacts on water cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_climate_change_on_the_water_cycle) are more complicated: already arid regions, primarily located in [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia) and [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia), will see more [droughts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drought), while areas of [East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia), Southeast and South Asia which are already wet due to the [monsoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsoon) will experience more flooding.[:1459](#cite_note-Climate_change_in_Asia_AR6_WGII_Chapter10-100)

The waters around Asia are subjected to the same [impacts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_climate_change_on_oceans) as elsewhere, such as the increased warming and [ocean acidification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_acidification).[:1465 There are many](#cite_note-Climate_change_in_Asia_AR6_WGII_Chapter10-100) [coral reefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_reef) in the region, and they are highly vulnerable to climate change,[:1459 to the point practically all of them will be lost if the warming exceeds 1.5°C (2.7°F).](#cite_note-Climate_change_in_Asia_AR6_WGII_Chapter10-100) [Asia's distinctive](#cite_note-Climate_change_in_Asia_ArmstrongMcKayExplainer-102) [mangrove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove) ecosystems are also highly vulnerable to [sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise).[:1459 Asia also has more countries with large coastal populations than any other continent, which would cause large economic impacts from sea level rise.](#cite_note-Climate_change_in_Asia_AR6_WGII_Chapter10-100)[:1459 Water supplies in the](#cite_note-Climate_change_in_Asia_AR6_WGII_Chapter10-100) [Hindu Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Kush) region will become more unstable as its enormous [glaciers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacier), known as the "Asian water towers", gradually melt.[:1459 These changes to water cycle also affect vector-borne disease distribution, with malaria and dengue fever expected to become more prominent in the tropical and subtropical regions.](#cite_note-Climate_change_in_Asia_AR6_WGII_Chapter10-100)[:1459](#cite_note-Climate_change_in_Asia_AR6_WGII_Chapter10-100) [Food security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_security) will become more uneven, and South Asian countries could experience significant impacts from global food price volatility.[:1494](#cite_note-Climate_change_in_Asia_AR6_WGII_Chapter10-100)

Historical emissions from Asia are lower than those from Europe and North America. However, China has been the single largest emitter of greenhouse gases in the 21st century, while India is the third-largest. As a whole, Asia currently accounts for 36% of world's primary [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy) consumption, which is expected to increase to 48% by 2050. By 2040, it is also expected to account for 80% of the world's [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal) and 26% of the world's [natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas) consumption.[:1468 While the](#cite_note-Climate_change_in_Asia_AR6_WGII_Chapter10-100) [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) remains the world's largest [oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil) consumer, by 2050 it is projected to move to third place, behind China and India.[:1470 While nearly half of the world's new](#cite_note-Climate_change_in_Asia_AR6_WGII_Chapter10-100) [renewable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy) capacity is built in Asia,[:1470 this is not yet sufficient in order to meet the goals of the](#cite_note-Climate_change_in_Asia_AR6_WGII_Chapter10-100) [Paris Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Agreement). They imply that the renewables would account for 35% of total energy consumption in Asia by 2030.[:1471](#cite_note-Climate_change_in_Asia_AR6_WGII_Chapter10-100)

[Climate change adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_adaptation) is already a reality for many Asian countries, with a wide range of strategies attempted across the continent.[:1534 Important examples include the growing implementation of](#cite_note-Climate_change_in_Asia_AR6_WGII_Chapter10-100) [climate-smart agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate-smart_agriculture) in certain countries or the "[sponge city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sponge_city)" planning principles in China.[:1534 While some countries have drawn up extensive frameworks such as the Bangladesh Delta Plan or Japan's Climate Adaptation Act,](#cite_note-Climate_change_in_Asia_AR6_WGII_Chapter10-100)[:1508 others still rely on localized actions that are not effectively scaled up.](#cite_note-Climate_change_in_Asia_AR6_WGII_Chapter10-100)[:1534](#cite_note-Climate_change_in_Asia_AR6_WGII_Chapter10-100)

## Economy

Asia is both the [largest continental economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_continents_by_GDP) in the world by both [GDP nominal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(nominal)) and [PPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity) values and the world's fastest growing economic region. [As of 2026](#cite_note-International_Monetary_Fund-115)[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Asia&action=edit), [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China) is by far the [largest economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Asian_countries_by_GDP) on the continent. It is followed by India, Japan, South Korea, Turkey, Indonesia, Saudi Arabia, and Taiwan, which are all ranked among the top 20 largest economies by both nominal GDP and PPP values. [Based on Global Office Locations 2011, Asia dominated global office locations with 4 of the top 5 being in Asia:](#cite_note-116) [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong), [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore), [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo) and [Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul). Around 68% of international firms have an office in Hong Kong.

In the late 1990s and early 2000s, the economy of China [had an average annual growth rate of more than 8%. According to economic historian](#cite_note-118) [Angus Maddison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angus_Maddison), India had the world's largest economy for much of the past three millennia prior to the 19th century, accounting for 25% of the world's industrial output. [China was the](#cite_note-122) [largest and most advanced economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_history_of_China) on Earth for much of recorded history and shared the mantle with India. [For several decades in the late twentieth century, Japan was the largest economy in Asia and second-largest in the world, after surpassing the](#cite_note-124) [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) (measured in net material product) in 1990 and Germany in 1968. This ended in 2010, when China overtook Japan to become the world's second largest economy. In 2025, India overtook Japan in terms of nominal GDP to become the [world's 4th largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(nominal)) and [Asia's 2nd largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Asian_countries_by_GDP) economy.

In the late 1980s and early 1990s, Japan's GDP by currency exchange rates was almost as large as that of the rest of Asia combined. [In 1995, Japan's economy nearly equaled that of the US as the largest economy in the world for a day, after the Japanese currency reached a record high of 79](#cite_note-International_Monetary_Fund-115) [yen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yen)/US$. Economic growth in Asia since [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) to the 1990s had been concentrated in Japan as well as South Korea, Taiwan, Hong Kong, and Singapore, known as the [Four Asian Tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Asian_Tigers), which are now all considered developed economies, having among the highest GDP per capita in Asia.[[](#cite_note-125)[failed verification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability)]

Asia is the largest continent in the world by a considerable margin, and it is rich in natural resources, such as [petroleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum), [forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest), [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish), [water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water), [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper) and [silver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver). Manufacturing in Asia has traditionally been strongest in East and Southeast Asia, particularly in China, [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan), South Korea, Japan, India, the Philippines, and Singapore. Japan and South Korea continue to dominate in the area of [multinational corporations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinational_corporation), though increasingly China and India are making significant inroads. Many companies from Europe, North America, South Korea, and Japan have operations in Asia's developing countries to take advantage of its abundant supply of cheap labour and relatively developed infrastructure.

According to [Citigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citigroup) in 2011, 9 of 11 [Global Growth Generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3G_countries) countries came from Asia driven by population and income growth. They are Bangladesh, China, India, Indonesia, Iraq, Mongolia, the Philippines, Sri Lanka, and Vietnam. [Asia has three main financial centers: Hong Kong, Tokyo, and Singapore.](#cite_note-128) [Call centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_center) and [business process outsourcing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_process_outsourcing) firms (BPOs) are becoming major employers in India and the Philippines due to the availability of a large pool of highly skilled, English-speaking workers. The increased use of outsourcing has assisted the rise of India and the China as financial centers. Due to its large and extremely competitive information technology industry, India has become a major hub for outsourcing.

Trade between Asian countries and countries on other continents is largely carried out via sea routes. The main route leads from the Chinese coast south via Hanoi to Jakarta, Singapore, and Kuala Lumpur, through the [Strait of Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Malacca) via [Colombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombo) to the southern tip of India, then via Malé to [Mombasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombasa) in East Africa (see also [Indo-Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pacific)), from there to [Djibouti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djibouti), then through the Red Sea and the [Suez Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Canal) into the Mediterranean (see also: [Indo-Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Mediterranean)), then via Haifa, Istanbul, and [Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athens) to the northern Italian hub of [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste), with its rail connections to Central and Eastern Europe, or further to [Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona) and around Spain and France to northern European ports. A far smaller part of the goods traffic runs via [South Africa to Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Route). A particularly significant part of Asian goods traffic is carried out on the [Pacific Rim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Rim), toward [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles) and [Long Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Beach). The melting of the Arctic is also paving the way for [new shipping routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_shipping_routes) from Northeast Asia to Europe and North America. [The land route to Europe is also the subject of construction projects, comparatively smaller in scope. Intra-Asian trade, including sea trade, is growing rapidly.](#cite_note-130)

In 2010, Asia had 3.3million millionaires (people with net worth over US$1 million excluding their homes), slightly below North America's 3.4 million. In 2011, Asia topped Europe in the number of millionaires.[Citigroup in The Wealth Report 2012 stated that the total wealth of people in Asia with over $100 million in assets exceeded that of their North American counterparts for the first time, as the world's "economic center of gravity" continued moving east. At the end of 2011, there were 18,000 people in Asia, mainly in Southeast Asia, China, and Japan, with at least $100 million in disposable assets, compared to North America with 17,000 and Western Europe with 14,000.](#cite_note-137)

### Energy

Asia's electricity demand has quadrupled since 2000 (rising from 4,199 TWh to over 16,000 TWh in 2024). This growth, driven largely by China and increasingly by India, Indonesia, and Viet Nam, [makes Asia the fastest-growing region for electricity demand globally, at about 5% per year.](#cite_note-:02-139) [The region now accounts for about 36% of global](#cite_note-:1-140) [primary energy consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_energy_supply_and_consumption), a share projected to approach 50% by 2050.[: 1468](#cite_note-AR6_WGII_Chapter10-141) [Fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel) remain central in Asia: 66% of its energy is generated from fossil fuels, and Asia makes up 83% of the world's coal generation. [However, coal is slowly being phased out and](#cite_note-:2-142) [natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas) is displacing oil and oil products in various sectors, supported by policies and regulations. Natural gas demand in emerging market and developing economies in Asia expanded by around 6% in 2024, accounting for nearly 40% of incremental global gas demand. [By 2040, Asia is expected to account for the majority of global coal use and over a quarter of natural gas consumption.](#cite_note-:3-143)[: 1468](#cite_note-AR6_WGII_Chapter10-141)

At the same time, Asia is the global leader in [renewable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy) expansion. It is currently the only region on track to meet the goal of tripling renewable capacity by 2030, largely due to China, the leading nation in renewable energy generation. [China's wind and solar capacity has surged in recent years, together overtaking that of coal,](#cite_note-146) [while in 2024 India become the world's third largest generator of electricity from wind and solar.](#cite_note-:4-147) [Nearly half of the world's new renewable energy capacity is built in Asia.](#cite_note-:5-148)[: 1470](#cite_note-AR6_WGII_Chapter10-141)

### Languages

Asia is home to several [language families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_families) and many [language isolates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_isolate). Most Asian countries have more than one language that is natively spoken. For instance, according to [Ethnologue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnologue), more than 700 languages are spoken in Indonesia, more than 400 languages spoken in India, and more than 100 are spoken in the Philippines. China has many languages and dialects in different provinces.

### Religions

Many of the world's [major religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religions) have their origins in Asia, including the five most practiced in the world (excluding [irreligion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion)), which are Christianity, Islam, Hinduism, Chinese folk religion (classified as Confucianism and Taoism), and Buddhism. Asian mythology is complex and diverse. The story of the [Great Flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Flood) for example, as presented to [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews) in the [Hebrew Bible (Tanakh)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Bible) in the narrative of [Noah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah)—later translated from [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew) to [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koine_Greek) and included in [Christanity’s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity) [Old Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Testament), and to Muslims in the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran)—is earliest found in [Mesopotamian mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamian_mythology), in the [Enûma Eliš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enûma_Eliš) and [Epic of Gilgamesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_of_Gilgamesh). [Hindu mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_mythology) similarly tells about an [avatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avatar) of [Vishnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishnu) in the form of a [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matsya) who warned [Manu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sraddhadeva_Manu) of a terrible flood. Ancient [Chinese mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_mythology) also tells of a [Great Flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Flood_(China)) spanning generations, one that required the combined efforts of emperors and divinities to control.

#### Abrahamic

The [Abrahamic religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrahamic_religions) including [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism), [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity), [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam), [Druze faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze_faith), [and](#cite_note-151) [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baháʼí_Faith) originated in West Asia.

[Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism), the oldest of the Abrahamic faiths, is practiced primarily in [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel), the indigenous homeland and historical birthplace of the [Hebrew nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews): which today consists both of those Jews who remained in [the Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizrahi_Jews) and those who returned from the [diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_diaspora) in Europe, North America, [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia), India, and other regions, [though various Israelite diaspora communities still exist worldwide. Jews are the predominant ethnic group in Israel (75.6%) numbering at about 6.1million.](#cite_note-154) [Levels of Jewish religious observance vary within the Jewish State. Outside of Israel, there are small ancient Jewish communities in](#cite_note-155) [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey) (17,400),[Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan) (9,100), [Iran (8,756),](#cite_note-157) [India (5,000) and](#cite_note-158) [Uzbekistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbekistan) (4,000), [among many other places. As of 2016, there are am estimated 14.4–17.5million (2016, est.)](#cite_note-159) [Jews alive in the world today, making them one of the smallest Asian minorities, at roughly 0.3–0.4& of the total population of the continent.](#cite_note-160)

[Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity) is a widespread religion in Asia, with more than 286million adherents in 2010 according to [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center), [and nearly 364million according to](#cite_note-161) [Britannica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britannica) Book of the Year 2014. [Christians constitute around 12.6% of the total population of Asia. In the Philippines and](#cite_note-162) [Timor-Leste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timor-Leste), [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism) is the predominant religion; [it was introduced by the Spaniards and the Portuguese, respectively. In](#cite_note-Pew_Research_Center-163) [Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia) and Georgia, [Eastern Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy) is the predominant religion. [In the Middle East, such as in the](#cite_note-Pew_Research_Center-163) [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant), [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia) and [Fars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fars_(territory)), [Syriac Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_Christianity) ([Church of the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_East)) and [Oriental Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_Orthodoxy) are prevalent minority denominations, [which are both](#cite_note-164) [Eastern Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Christian) sects mainly adhered to [Assyrian people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_people) or Syriac Christians. Vibrant indigenous minorities in [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia) are adhering to the [Eastern Catholic Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Catholic_Churches) and Eastern Orthodoxy.[Saint Thomas Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Thomas_Christians) in India trace their origins to the evangelistic activity of [Thomas the Apostle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_the_Apostle) in the 1st century. [Significant Christian communities also found in](#cite_note-165) [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia), [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia), [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia) and [East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia).

[Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam), which originated in the [Hejaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hejaz) located in modern-day Saudi Arabia, is the second largest and most widely-spread religion in Asia with at least 1billion Muslims constituting around 23.8% of the total population of Asia. [With 12.7% of the world Muslim population, the country currently with the largest Muslim population in the world is Indonesia, followed by Pakistan (11.5%), India (10%), Bangladesh, Iran and Turkey.](#cite_note-166) [Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca), [Medina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medina) and [Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holiest_sites_in_Islam#Jerusalem) are the three holiest cities for Islam in all the world. The [Hajj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajj) and [Umrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umrah) attract large numbers of Muslim devotees from all over the world to Mecca and Medina. Iran is the largest [Shia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia) country.

The [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze) originated in West Asia, is a monotheistic religion based on the teachings of figures like [Hamza ibn Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamza_ibn_Ali) and [al-Hakim bi-Amr Allah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Hakim_bi-Amr_Allah), and Greek [philosophers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophers) such as [Plato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plato) and [Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle). The number of Druze people worldwide is around one million. Around 45–50% live in [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria), 35% to 40% live in [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon), and less than 10% live in [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel). Recently there has been a growing Druze diaspora.

The [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baháʼí_Faith) originated in Asia, in Iran (Persia), and spread from there to the Ottoman Empire, Central Asia, India, and Burma during the lifetime of [Bahá'u'lláh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahá'u'lláh). Since the middle of the 20th century, growth has particularly occurred in other Asian countries, because Baháʼí activities in many Muslim countries has been [severely suppressed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Baháʼís) by authorities. [Lotus Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotus_Temple) is a big [Baháʼí temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baháʼí_temple) in India.

#### Indian and East Asian religions

Almost all Asian religions have philosophical character and Asian philosophical traditions cover a large spectrum of philosophical thoughts and writings. [Indian philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_philosophy) includes [Hindu philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_philosophy) and [Buddhist philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_philosophy). They include elements of nonmaterial pursuits, whereas another school of thought from India, [Cārvāka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cārvāka), preached the enjoyment of the material world. The religions of [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism), [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism), [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism) and [Sikhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism) originated in India, South Asia. In East Asia, particularly in China and Japan, [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism), [Taoism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoism) and [Zen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zen) Buddhism took shape.

As of 2012[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Asia&action=edit), Hinduism has around 1.1billion adherents. The faith represents around 25% of Asia's population and is the largest religion in Asia. However, it is mostly concentrated in South Asia. Over 80% of the populations of both India and Nepal adhere to Hinduism, alongside significant communities in Bangladesh, Pakistan, Bhutan, Sri Lanka and [Bali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bali), Indonesia. Many overseas Indians in countries such as Burma, Singapore and Malaysia also adhere to Hinduism.

Buddhism has a great following in [Mainland Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_Southeast_Asia) and East Asia. Buddhism is the religion of the majority of the populations of Cambodia (96%),[Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand) (95%),[Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma) (80–89%), [Japan (36–96%),](#cite_note-171)[Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan) (75–84%),[Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka) (70%),[Laos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laos) (60–67%) [and](#cite_note-175) [Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolia) (53–93%).[Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan) (35–93%), [South Korea (23–50%),](#cite_note-180)[Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia) (19–21%),[Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal) (9–11%),[Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam) (10–75%), [China (20–50%),](#cite_note-184)[Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore) (31%),[North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea) (2–14%), [and small communities in India and Bangladesh. The Communist-governed countries of China, Vietnam and North Korea are officially atheist, thus the number of Buddhists and other religious adherents may be under-reported.](#cite_note-189)

[Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism) is found mainly in India and in overseas Indian communities such as the United States and Malaysia. [Sikhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism) is found in Northern India and amongst overseas Indian communities in other parts of Asia, especially Southeast Asia. [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism) is found predominantly in mainland China, South Korea, Taiwan and in overseas Chinese populations. [Taoism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoism) is found mainly in mainland China, Taiwan, Malaysia and Singapore. In many Chinese communities, Taoism is easily syncretised with [Mahayana Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahayana_Buddhism), thus exact religious statistics are difficult to obtain and may be understated or overstated.

## Culture

The [culture of Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Asia) is a diverse blend of customs and traditions that have been practiced by the various ethnic groups of the continent for centuries. The continent is divided into six geographic sub-regions: [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia), [East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_cultural_sphere), [North Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Asia), [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_ethnic_groups), [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia), and [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia). [These regions are defined by their cultural similarities, including common religions, languages, and ethnicities. West Asia, also known as Southwest Asia or the](#cite_note-190) [Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East), has cultural roots in the ancient civilisations of the [Fertile Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertile_Crescent) and [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia), which gave rise to the [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran), [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world), [Ottoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire) empires, as well as the Abrahamic religions of Judaism, Christianity and Islam. [These civilisations, which are located in the](#cite_note-191) [Hilly flanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilly_flanks), are among the oldest in the world, with evidence of farming dating back to around 9000BCE. [Despite the challenges posed by the vast size of the continent and the presence of natural barriers such as deserts and mountain ranges, trade and commerce have helped to create a](#cite_note-192) [Pan-Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Asian) culture that is shared across the region.

### Nobel laureates

[Rabindranath Tagore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabindranath_Tagore), a [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_literature) dramatist and author from [Santiniketan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiniketan) (now in [West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bengal), India), won the [Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Literature) in 1913, becoming the first Asian [Nobel laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_laureate). The prize was awarded for Tagore's prose works and poetry, which had a significant additional impact on national literatures throughout the Western world. Tagore also authored both the Indian and Bangladeshi national anthems.

Other Asian writers who won Nobel Prize for literature include [Yasunari Kawabata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasunari_Kawabata) (Japan, 1968), [Kenzaburō Ōe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenzaburō_Ōe) (Japan, 1994), [Gao Xingjian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gao_Xingjian) (China, 2000), [Orhan Pamuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orhan_Pamuk) (Turkey, 2006), [Mo Yan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mo_Yan) (China, 2012), and [Han Kang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Kang) (South Korea, 2024). Some may consider the American writer, [Pearl S. Buck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_S._Buck), an honorary Asian Nobel laureate, having spent considerable time in China as the daughter of missionaries, and based many of her novels, namely [The Good Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Good_Earth) (1931) and [The Mother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mother_(1934_novel)) (1933), as well as the biographies of her parents for their time in China, [The Exile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Exile_(1936_book)) and [Fighting Angel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fighting_Angel), all of which earned her the Literature prize in 1938.

[Mother Teresa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_Teresa) of India and [Shirin Ebadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirin_Ebadi) of Iran were awarded the Nobel Peace Prize for their significant and pioneering efforts for [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy) and human rights, especially for the rights of women and children. Ebadi is the first Iranian and the first Muslim woman to receive the prize. Another Nobel Peace Prize winner is [Aung San Suu Kyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aung_San_Suu_Kyi) from [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma) for her peaceful and non-violent struggle under a military dictatorship in Burma. She is a nonviolent pro-democracy activist and leader of the National League for Democracy in Burma (Myanmar) and a noted prisoner of conscience. She is a [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist) and was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 1991. Chinese dissident [Liu Xiaobo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Xiaobo) was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize for "his long and non-violent struggle for fundamental human rights in China" on 8 October 2010. He is the first Chinese citizen to be awarded a Nobel Prize of any kind while residing in China. In 2014, [Kailash Satyarthi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kailash_Satyarthi) from India and [Malala Yousafzai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malala_Yousafzai) from Pakistan were awarded the Nobel Peace Prize "for their struggle against the suppression of children and young people and for the right of all children to education".

[C.V. Raman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.V._Raman) is the first Asian to get a Nobel prize in Sciences. He won the [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics) "for his work on the scattering of light and for the discovery of the [effect named after him](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raman_scattering)".

Japan has won the most Nobel Prizes of any Asian nation with 24 followed by India which has won 13.[[needs update](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items)]

[Amartya Sen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amartya_Sen) (b.1933) is an Indian economist who was awarded the 1998 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences for his contributions to welfare economics and [social choice theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_choice_theory), and for his interest in the problems of society's poorest members.

Other Asian Nobel Prize winners include [Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subrahmanyan_Chandrasekhar), [Abdus Salam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdus_Salam), [Robert Aumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Aumann), [Menachem Begin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menachem_Begin), [Aaron Ciechanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Ciechanover), [Avram Hershko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avram_Hershko), [Daniel Kahneman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Kahneman), [Shimon Peres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimon_Peres), [Yitzhak Rabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Rabin), [Ada Yonath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ada_Yonath), [Yasser Arafat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasser_Arafat), [José Ramos-Horta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/José_Ramos-Horta) and Bishop [Carlos Filipe Ximenes Belo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Filipe_Ximenes_Belo) of [Timor-Leste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timor-Leste), [Kim Dae-jung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Dae-jung), and 13 Japanese scientists. Most of the said awardees are from Japan and [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel) except for Chandrasekhar and Raman (India), Abdus Salam (Pakistan), Arafat (Palestinian Territories), Kim (South Korea), and Horta and Belo (Timor-Leste).[[needs update](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items)]

In 2006, the Bangladeshi [Muhammad Yunus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Yunus) of was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize for the establishment of [Grameen Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grameen_Bank), a community development bank that lends money to poor people, especially women. He is known for the concept of micro credit which, allows poor and destitute people to borrow money. The borrowers pay back money within the specified period and defaulting is very low. Yunus also became the leader of an [interim government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunus_interim_government) after the [2024 Bangladesh quota reform movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Bangladesh_quota_reform_movement). [The](#cite_note-194) [Dalai Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalai_Lama) received the Nobel Peace Prize, in [Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo), Norway in 1989.

## Notes

- Asia is normally considered its own continent in the English speaking world, which uses the seven continent model.[Other models consider Asia as part of a Eurasian or Afro-Eurasian continent (see](#cite_note-9)[Continent §Number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continent#Number)).
- 44,579,000 square kilometres (17,212,000 square miles)
- Siberia lies in Asia geographically, but is considered part of Europe culturally and politically.
- Azerbaijan is a [transcontinental country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcontinental_country), straddling the border between[Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe)and Asia. - Egypt is a [transcontinental country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcontinental_country)located primarily in[Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa), with the[Sinai Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_Peninsula)being located in Asia.
- Cairo is located in [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa).
- Georgia is a [transcontinental country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcontinental_country), with a small northern portion lying in[Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe). Despite being primarily located in Asia, Georgia leans strongly towards[European culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Europe)and has closer ties with the[UN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations)and[NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO).
- Indonesia is a [transcontinental country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcontinental_country), with the island of[New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Guinea)being located in[Melanesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanesia)- a region of[Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania). New Guinea's eastern portion is shared with[Papua New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papua_New_Guinea).
- Kazakhstan is a [transcontinental country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcontinental_country), with about 15% of its land located in[Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe).
- Russia is a [transcontinental country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcontinental_country)located in[Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe)and[North Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Asia), but is considered European historically, culturally, ethnically, and politically, and the vast majority of its population (78%) lives within its[European part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Russia).
- Moscow is located in [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe).
- Turkey is a [transcontinental country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcontinental_country)located mainly in[West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia)with a smaller portion in[Southeastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeastern_Europe).
- Yemen is a [transcontinental country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcontinental_country). While its mainland is geographically part of Asia, the[Socotra Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socotra_Archipelago)islands are geographically located in[Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa).
